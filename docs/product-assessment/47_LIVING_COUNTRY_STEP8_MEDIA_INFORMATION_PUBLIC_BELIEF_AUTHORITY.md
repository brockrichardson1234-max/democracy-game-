# 2. Accepted central answer

The Living Country media/information/public-belief layer is accepted conceptually as:

> **A provenance-preserving ecology of autonomous speakers, evidence, claims, editorial and investigative decisions, publication attempts, channels, platform decisions, audience-specific presentation, one-human/multi-context recipient reconciliation, recipient attention, trust, memory, belief, attribution, salience, and later political action—not one NewsSystem, one MediaAttention or Virality meter, one narrative director, or one approval/election modifier.**

The accepted end-to-end relationship is:

```text
canonical reality or occurrence
→ bounded evidence, observation, record, testimony, or direct experience
→ speaker, journalist, institution, or organization receives what it can access
→ source/evidence assessment and autonomous choice whether to investigate,
  claim, frame, publish, brief, leak, advertise, correct, share, delay,
  qualify, retract, or remain silent
→ canonical communication/publication/distribution attempt
→ versioned information artifact with proposition, evidence, framing,
  claimed confidence, provenance, and derivative lineage
→ outlet/channel/platform admission, moderation, ranking, targeting,
  scheduling, availability, or non-distribution
→ realized presentation/delivery and exposure opportunity
→ recipient notice, attention, comprehension, source recognition,
  trust, memory, and interpretation
→ recipient-owned belief, uncertainty, attribution, and salience
→ separately owned willingness, decision, sharing, organization,
  turnout, voting, or other political action
→ polls and other measurements may estimate portions of recipient state
→ institutions and actors may respond based on what they know or believe
→ the administration learns only through valid receipts and escalation
```

No arrow guarantees the next.

# 3. Accepted ownership boundaries

## 3.1 Underlying reality

Material, social, fiscal, legal, administrative, institutional, actor, population, election, and external facts remain with their accepted semantic owners.

Media and public belief do not own reality.

A report, claim, trend, platform label, audience reaction, or viral narrative cannot make an underlying event true.

## 3.2 Evidence and measurement

Evidence and observations retain the process, support, method, vintage, access, uncertainty, and dependency semantics accepted in Step 7.

A media actor may acquire, copy, analyze, corroborate, dispute, or publish evidence.

The media actor does not thereby become the original evidence owner.

## 3.3 Speaker and communicator

An eligible speaker or institution owns its decision or attempt to:

- speak;
- claim;
- publish;
- brief;
- leak;
- advertise;
- recommend;
- share;
- qualify;
- correct;
- retract;
- remain silent.

The speaker may select proposition, evidence, framing, claimed confidence, audience, channel, and timing within its authority and capabilities.

The speaker does not own recipient understanding or belief.

## 3.4 Information artifact

An artifact owns its own informational identity and intrinsic properties, including as applicable:

- content;
- proposition references;
- evidence references;
- producer/source references;
- quotes and summaries;
- framing and emphasis;
- claimed certainty;
- creation/release time;
- intrinsic access classification;
- version, correction, retraction, clip, translation, quotation, edit, and derivative lineage.

The artifact does not own:

- underlying truth;
- realized distribution;
- recipient notice;
- belief;
- public salience;
- election outcome.

## 3.5 Outlet, channel, and platform

Outlets, institutions, persons, channels, and platforms own their eligible publication, admission, distribution, moderation, ranking, recommendation, scheduling, search, targeting, or removal decisions and attempts.

`InformationEnvironment` owns represented availability, presentation/delivery, and exposure-opportunity occurrences.

Recipients own cognitive incorporation.

## 3.6 Recipient

The actual receiving human, population scope, individualized actor, office, institution, organization, campaign, or other valid recipient context owns the applicable:

- receipt;
- notice;
- attention;
- comprehension;
- source recognition;
- memory;
- trust;
- belief;
- uncertainty;
- attribution;
- salience;
- intention or willingness;
- later decision and action through the appropriate process.

## 3.7 Polling and elections

Polls remain Step-7 measurement products concerning recipient-owned political state.

They do not own that state.

Elections separately own ballots, counting, result, certification, entitlement, and office transfer.

Information does not directly resolve elections.

# 5. Proposition and derivative lineage

## 5.1 Proposition identity

A load-bearing claim or belief transition identifies a proposition, proposition family, or bounded semantic target sufficient to distinguish:

- subject;
- asserted relation;
- target/object;
- time;
- geography;
- population/entity scope;
- magnitude or quantifier;
- modality such as observed, alleged, estimated, predicted, normative, causal, or attributive;
- uncertainty;
- semantic/version lineage.

No universal formal-logic engine is required.

## 5.2 Proposition identity is not truth authority

A proposition identity names the content being asserted, denied, evaluated, or believed.

It does not own truth.

Truth remains with the relevant semantic owners.

Evidence support remains with accepted evidence and analytic processes.

Relations such as `supports`, `contradicts`, `corrects`, `qualifies`, or `falsifies` require provenance and may themselves be assessments or claims.

No omniscient PropositionGraph is accepted.

## 5.3 Derivative lineage

Artifacts may:

- quote;
- paraphrase;
- translate;
- clip;
- repeat;
- summarize;
- update;
- narrow;
- broaden;
- correct;
- qualify;
- dispute;
- satirize;
- impersonate;
- share evidence or source.

Derivative artifacts preserve lineage where load-bearing.

A new artifact ID does not create independent evidence.

Repeated derivatives may still create additional presentation and familiarity.

# 6. Truth, belief, intent, and presentation

The accepted design separates:

```text
canonical truth status
speaker-accessible evidence
speaker private belief
speaker objective and intent
claimed confidence
artifact content and framing
recipient perception
recipient belief and attribution
```

A speaker may:

- honestly reach a false conclusion;
- knowingly deceive;
- omit consequential context;
- exaggerate confidence;
- accurately disclose inconvenient evidence;
- repeat without checking;
- use real evidence for unsupported causal attribution;
- remain uncertain;
- remain silent.

The simulation’s evidence support remains honest even when the actor’s analysis is poor or dishonest.

No universal honesty or credibility stat is accepted.

# 7. Media entities and decision loci

## 7.1 Entity identity

A parent organization, outlet, newsroom, station, publication, desk, journalist, investigative team, standards/legal unit, and platform may be distinct only when the distinction changes a supported:

- decision;
- resource;
- source relationship;
- legal responsibility;
- audience;
- geographic reach;
- distribution channel;
- investigation;
- correction;
- political consequence.

Step 4's one-entity rule prevents duplicated cash, staff, stories, actions, and liabilities.

## 7.2 Persistent identity

Individualize an outlet, journalist, editor, investigative team, or platform when identity, history, sources, audience trust, access, resources, or one decision can change a supported consequence.

Aggregate routine media activity when identity does not matter.

## 7.3 Decision locus

A consequential media or platform action identifies the actual decision locus, potentially:

- journalist;
- editor;
- newsroom or desk process;
- standards/legal review;
- owner/publisher within valid governance;
- platform policy process;
- automated ranking process under declared rules;
- syndication process;
- aggregate distributional response.

“The media decided” is insufficient.

# 8. Journalism and investigation

A load-bearing investigation follows:

```text
tip, anomaly, filing, public record, source communication,
direct observation, prior story, or data pattern
→ journalist/outlet receives bounded lead
→ assesses relevance, evidence, source risk, legal risk,
  public value, audience, cost, and opportunity
→ allocates or withholds scarce investigative work
→ seeks records, interviews, analysis, corroboration,
  target response, and standards/legal review
→ creates evidence and investigative records
→ decides whether to publish, narrow, delay, continue,
  abandon, qualify, or refer
→ publication attempt and artifact
→ independent target/institution response
→ later evidence, correction, litigation, or follow-up
```

Investigation may create new observable evidence about preexisting or contemporaneous reality.

It may not create the underlying misconduct, failure, payment, condition, or scandal because political drama is needed.

Source identity, source access, public anonymity, source credibility, evidence support, and corroboration remain separate.

Several apparent sources may share one origin. Step 7 dependency lineage remains controlling.

# 9. Editorial selection and framing

An outlet may autonomously select or decline coverage based on bounded factors such as:

- evidence strength;
- novelty;
- timeliness;
- geography;
- audience relevance;
- editorial mission;
- public-service judgment;
- ideology/worldview;
- source access;
- exclusivity;
- competition;
- expected audience interest;
- revenue pressure;
- legal risk;
- investigative cost;
- staff and publication capacity;
- prior coverage and correction burden.

There is no canonical `Newsworthiness` or `NationalImportance` score that commands every outlet.

Framing may change proposition ordering, comparison, causal attribution, uncertainty emphasis, quoted voices, moral language, context, images, and headline.

Framing does not rewrite evidence or reality.

Consequential omission may be retained when the producer possessed the omitted information and the omission can return as different interpretation, correction, trust, relationship, or action.

Publication identifies producer, artifact, channel, time, audience intention, access conditions, prominence, legal/editorial state, embargo, and any syndication or platform dependency where relevant.

Public classification does not mean universal practical access.

A local story, paywalled report, obscure filing, press release, or leaked summary may be available to very different audiences.

## 10.2 Channels

Supported channels may include, at deliberately bounded resolution:

- broadcast;
- print;
- outlet website/app;
- email/newsletter;
- search;
- social feed;
- direct message;
- audio/video platform;
- press conference;
- official portal;
- interpersonal communication;
- organization/member network;
- advertising;
- syndication.

No product scope is required to model every channel.

## 10.3 Platform decisions

Platform actions such as:

- admission;
- removal;
- labeling;
- recommendation eligibility;
- ranking/downranking;
- search indexing;
- sharing restriction;
- paid-promotion restriction;
- account suspension

remain distinct.

A post remaining available does not mean it is recommended.

A label does not guarantee attention or correction.

## 10.4 Personalized distribution

Platforms may use records and proxies they actually possess, such as:

- follows;
- prior engagement;
- searches;
- geography/language;
- recency;
- content category;
- network relationships;
- user controls;
- safety/quality rules;
- paid targeting.

They may not directly read exact hidden recipient belief or future behavior.

Prediction, recommendation, presentation, engagement, and belief remain distinct.

# 11. Closed information-delivery stages

The accepted terms are:

## Availability

The artifact can be accessed through a channel under stated conditions.

## Presentation or delivery

A channel, platform, institution, or person places/transmits content onto a declared recipient surface or scope.

## Exposure opportunity

The presented content is perceptible to the declared recipient under the represented context.

## Notice

The recipient minimally perceives the content.

## Attention

The recipient allocates further cognitive processing.

## Engagement

The recipient performs a separate behavior such as click, reaction, view duration, search, comment, or share.

Availability, presentation, exposure opportunity, notice, attention, comprehension, memory, belief, and action do not collapse.

The informal word `exposure` must identify its intended stage in any load-bearing contract.

# 12. One human across several recipient contexts

## 12.1 Identity and linkage

An individualized human retains one underlying identity and one Step-4 population-linkage status for an effective interval even when the human also participates as:

- ordinary/private person;
- individualized actor;
- officeholder;
- organization or campaign member;
- account holder;
- subscriber;
- confidential source;
- classified or privileged recipient.

The contexts do not create additional people, population weight, voters, or independent minds.

## 12.2 Context-owned state

Different contexts may retain distinct purpose-bounded state:

- private personal notice and memory;
- actor-private interpretation and intention;
- office receipt and official record;
- organization or campaign receipt and adopted analysis;
- Population-level exact/modeled/bounded distributions.

No context automatically writes every other context.

## 12.3 Personal knowledge and role use

An exact human may legitimately use personally noticed information in a later actor decision.

This is not another exposure.

It also does not create official office receipt or institution-wide knowledge.

Official propagation requires a forwarding, communication, record, meeting, instruction, or other valid transfer.

## 12.4 Access separation

Classified, privileged, embargoed, or official role knowledge does not become public-population belief or ordinary media exposure.

Private/public exposure does not automatically become institutional receipt.

Any cross-context transfer creates a separate canonical act with access, time, sender, recipient, artifact/proposition, and consequences.

## 12.5 Statistical actors

A statistically represented actor may not inherit exact personal delivery, notice, memory, trust, belief, attribution, salience, or media use from aggregate audience state.

The answer remains independently evidenced, modeled, bounded, unsupported, or unknown.

## 12.6 Exact and aggregate overlap

Exact named recipients and aggregate audiences must declare exclusion, inclusion without personal resolution, joint allocation, or modeled/bounded overlap.

One person may not be counted twice in unique reach merely because exact and aggregate representations coexist.

## 12.7 Occurrence idempotency

One canonical presentation or delivery occurrence is consumed at most once by the same underlying recipient context at the same semantic stage.

Projection aliases do not create additional presentations, notice, memory, or belief.

Distinct presentations through different times/channels may create legitimate repetition.

# 13. Recipient cognition

## 13.1 Notice and comprehension

A recipient may:

- fail to notice;
- notice only headline or image;
- misunderstand;
- understand qualification;
- remember content but not source;
- remember source but not content;
- infer a broader proposition;
- fail to process uncertainty.

The artifact does not dictate comprehension.

## 13.2 Trust

Trust is relational and contextual. It may vary by:

- recipient;
- source;
- outlet;
- journalist;
- institution;
- topic;
- format;
- time;
- prior history;
- direct experience;
- perceived motive;
- corroboration.

No global outlet credibility scalar controls all audiences and subjects.

Actual support and perceived credibility remain distinct.

## 13.3 Memory

Memory may be sparse and purpose-bounded.

Retain it when it can return as different belief, trust, salience, attribution, sharing, vote, organization, relationship, response to correction, or presidential politics.

Memory may decay, reinforce, retrieve, or become source-confused under later accepted processes.

No universal memory-decay formula is accepted.

## 13.4 Belief

Belief is proposition-specific where that distinction matters.

Recipients may be:

- correct;
- mistaken;
- uncertain;
- overconfident;
- underconfident;
- internally inconsistent;
- source-confused;
- correct for weak reasons;
- wrong from honest evidence.

Recipient belief may differ from canonical truth.

## 13.5 Attribution

Attribution identifies who or what the recipient believes caused, deserves credit for, or bears responsibility for an event or condition.

Belief that an event occurred does not establish agreement on cause or responsibility.

## 13.6 Salience

Salience is recipient-owned and perspectival.

It may reflect availability, urgency, identity, importance, direct experience, repetition, organization, and decision relevance.

It is not equivalent to material severity and is not one canonical issue-importance value.

# 14. Recipient disposition and later action

Belief, attribution, salience, preference, willingness, intention, propensity, and readiness may influence later conduct.

They are not the conduct itself.

The later:

- share;
- discussion;
- donation;
- protest;
- organization membership;
- contact;
- consumption;
- compliance;
- turnout;
- ballot

requires the appropriate actor, population, organization, market, institutional, or election process.

`willing to share` is not `shared`.

`intends to vote` is not `ballot cast`.

# 15. Repetition, correction, and evolving stories

## 15.1 Repetition

Repeated presentation can affect familiarity, memory, perceived prevalence, or salience.

It does not create independent evidence automatically.

The recipient process may distinguish:

- same artifact repeated;
- derivative artifact;
- shared proposition;
- new source;
- independent corroboration;
- contradiction;
- correction;
- satire;
- uncertain semantic match.

## 15.2 Correction and retraction

Corrections, qualifications, updates, and retractions create new artifacts and occurrences with target proposition/artifact, evidence, producer, time, and lineage.

They do not:

- erase original artifact;
- erase prior availability/presentation;
- rewind memory or belief;
- delete prior political action;
- automatically establish truth;
- automatically fail.

Correction distribution is separate and may reach a different audience.

## 15.3 Moderation and fact-check annotations

A platform label, warning, context panel, fact-check link, authenticity marker, or recommendation restriction is decomposed into:

- eligible decision;
- annotation/artifact;
- evidence and claimed assessment;
- distribution consequence;
- recipient exposure opportunity;
- recipient interpretation.

The annotation does not own truth or set belief.

# 16. Sharing and interpersonal transmission

Recipients may become speakers through a new communication attempt.

They may quote, share, repost, summarize, distort, joke, endorse, condemn, fact-check, or remain silent.

Ordinary-person sharing may be aggregated when individual identity is not load-bearing.

The aggregate process yields distributions, rates, counts, network diffusion, or sets of bounded unit-level acts—not one public hive mind.

Interpersonal transmission may occur through households, workplaces, unions, religious communities, neighborhoods, organizations, private messaging, and professional networks at the resolution later justified.

Social endorsement may affect perceived trust or salience without becoming independent evidence.

# 17. Misinformation, disinformation, propaganda, and influence

The design distinguishes where support permits:

- error;
- rumor;
- misinformation;
- deliberate disinformation;
- propaganda;
- satire/parody;
- contested claim;
- synthetic/manipulated content;
- authentic content used misleadingly.

Truth, intent, public classification, analytic classification, and recipient belief remain separate.

A deceptive campaign may fail, be detected, reach few recipients, concentrate in one audience, provoke backlash, or succeed with some people.

A coordinated operation may own operators, accounts, channels, targets, resources, timing, and coordination.

Accounts do not add citizen or voter weight without valid linkage.

Foreign actors may create, steal, forge, leak, amplify, or suppress information through accepted actor and information contracts.

No foreign campaign directly modifies election results.

Government countermeasures remain legal, intelligence, investigative, diplomatic, platform-request, sanctions, prosecution, security, and communication attempts with independent outcomes.

# 18. Virality, trending, and platform feedback

Virality is a projection over publication, presentation, engagement, sharing, networks, audience size, repetition, and time.

It is not a global owner that causes distribution.

A valid feedback loop may be:

```text
presentation
→ recipient notice/engagement/share
→ platform records behavior
→ ranking model updates prediction
→ later presentation probabilities change
→ additional exposure opportunity
```

Each stage has an owner and time.

The loop may saturate, fragment, reverse, or fail.

Trending projections summarize platform-specific activity. They do not create underlying public interest, and displaying a trend may itself become a new artifact/presentation.

Feedback and stochastic processes must preserve explicit cadence, same-time ordering, stable causal keys, save/load invariance, and time-chunk invariance.

# 19. Political communication and administration monitoring

## 19.1 Presidential and official communication

The President or administration may select a bounded communication bundle including:

- proposition;
- evidence release;
- claimed certainty;
- audience;
- venue;
- spokesperson;
- timing;
- rebuttal;
- silence;
- acknowledgement of uncertainty or failure.

Each authorized component becomes a distinct instrument or communication under Step 5.

Staff may not silently add an agency directive, congressional commitment, admission, policy change, or paid campaign merely because it would reinforce the message.

The administration does not choose outlet framing, platform presentation, recipient trust, or belief.

## 19.2 Monitoring

Administration offices may receive:

- public releases;
- press-monitoring summaries;
- journalist questions;
- source/whistleblower communications;
- platform analytics;
- polling;
- organization, governor, lawmaker, and agency reaction;
- intelligence on influence operations.

They do not receive every artifact or exact public belief.

## 19.3 Escalation

A media/public matter reaches presidential attention only through accepted administration routes: authority need, known deadline, cross-office conflict, player watch instruction, consequential operational/legal/political effect, or bounded senior-staff judgment based on received evidence.

A viral story does not directly interrupt the player.

Possible presidential responses derive from actual authority, information, and state. No generic `respond to controversy → approval change` action exists.

# 20. Public agenda remains plural

The following are separate projections:

- what one audience finds salient;
- what one outlet covers;
- what one platform trends;
- what one party emphasizes;
- what one campaign advertises;
- what Congress schedules;
- what governors discuss;

A severe condition may remain politically quiet through weak evidence, lag, limited organization, local coverage, low distribution, low notice, or unclear attribution.

The information layer supplies necessary causal pathways for political pressure but does not by itself complete Living Country Step 9's emergent-issue contract.

# 22. Persistence, chronology, compression, and determinism

The historical record preserves load-bearing:

- original artifacts;
- versions and derivatives;
- corrections/retractions;
- publication/distribution attempts;
- availability/presentation occurrences;
- retained recipient state;
- actor and institutional responses;
- evidence and proposition lineage;
- what actors knew at decision time.

Later truth discovery does not rewrite what was previously published, received, believed, or acted upon.

The simulation may compress routine impression detail while preserving the support required for exposure history, belief, legal/investigative evidence, political consequences, and accountability.

Compression cannot recreate exact exposure later.

Stochastic media, platform, attention, sharing, and belief processes must be keyed to stable canonical inputs and identities, independent of list order and global random cursors, save/load stable, and time-chunk invariant.

# 25. Step 8 verdict

## **ACCEPTED**

The Step 8 question is answered at design-contract level:

> **Autonomous speakers, journalists, media organizations, platforms, institutions, and fragmented recipients can create a politically alive information environment through bounded evidence, proposition-aware claims, investigation, framing, publication, audience-specific presentation, one-human/multi-context receipt reconciliation, recipient-owned cognition, additive correction, and independent political action—without media owning reality, duplicate artifacts becoming independent proof, universal exposure, global attention/credibility/virality scores, scripted scandal generation, omniscient targeting, perfect analysts, duplicate people, or direct approval/election modifiers.**

This acceptance establishes the common information/public-belief constitution.

It does not prove the algorithms or product experience implementing it.
