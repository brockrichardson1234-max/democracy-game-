# 2. Blocking finding R5-01 is closed

## 2.1 Presidential intent is now typed

The repaired contract distinguishes:

- objective or priority declaration;
- internal White House instruction;
- executive directive, order, memorandum, or other operative instruction;
- delegation;
- request for information, analysis, recommendation, or reconsideration;
- office-reserved approval, signature, veto, designation, finding, certification, or similar act;
- nomination, appointment, designation, removal, or request for resignation;
- interinstitutional offer or request;
- public communication authorization;
- return, deferral, withdrawal, cancellation, or deliberate inaction.

Those dispositions no longer share one downstream meaning.

In particular:

- a priority is not an agency order;
- a public statement is not a private instruction;
- a request is not a delegation;
- a nomination is not an office assignment;
- approval is not automatic execution;
- a White House coordination decision is not an agency action;
- inaction does not erase external state.

## 2.2 Presidential decision, instrument, dispatch, receipt, and response are separate

The repaired outbound chain is now:

```text
presidential choice or default
→ canonical presidential decision/attempt occurrence
→ typed instrument, instruction, request, delegation,
  nomination, offer, or communication
→ declared addressee, scope, authority claim,
  classification, effective time, response, and deadline/default
→ dispatch/delivery attempt
→ recipient receipt or failed-delivery state
→ recipient interpretation of applicability, authority,
  capability, duties, conflicts, evidence, and timing
→ recipient-owned assignment, queue, response, or action
→ independent downstream resolution
→ completion, partial action, delay, refusal, noncompliance,
  supersession, expiration, or escalation record
```

No `Administration` owner may skip these boundaries by applying a presidential choice directly to subordinate state.

## 2.3 One decision may produce several acts without becoming one universal act

The repair recognizes that a presidential choice may require:

- an internal coordination instruction;
- an agency directive;
- an OMB analysis request;
- a congressional request;
- a public communication.

Each requires a distinct instrument or communication with its own owner, addressee, route, timing, and receipt.

The decomposition itself must be explicit and may not invent actions outside the presidential disposition.

This closes the exploit where one click silently becomes an order, congressional promise, public statement, and budget action simultaneously.

## 2.4 Objectives and workstream labels cannot mutate queues

An administration objective or `Presidential Push` label may become a record legitimately available to offices.

It cannot directly:

- accelerate HUD;
- reorder OMB;
- initiate Legislative Affairs outreach;
- reserve staff;
- create public commitments;
- produce a material outcome.

Each queue or assignment changes through its own instruction, delegation, standing rule, legal deadline, or owner judgment after valid receipt.

## 2.5 Recipients retain autonomy

A recipient can:

- admit the instruction;
- create an assignment;
- reserve its own resources;
- request clarification;
- refer for review;
- partially comply;
- delay;
- reject as inapplicable;
- refuse or contest;
- continue standing policy;
- take no action;
- escalate.

Recipient receipt is not compliance, and compliance is not downstream success.

That is compatible with the accepted Step 4 actor grammar and the production baseline’s existing separation between directive receipt, agency handling, judicial-order receipt, and compliance response.

## 2.6 Partial distribution and version history are real

Recipients may receive:

- different instruments;
- different versions;
- the same instrument at different times;
- a superseding instrument before or after acting on an earlier version.

The administration is not one synchronized memory.

Supersession, revocation, amendment, extension, suspension, or expiration requires a new canonical act or declared automatic rule. Prior decisions and receipts remain historical.

## 2.7 The narrow-addressee test passes

Given a presidential request addressed to HUD and OMB only:

- HUD and OMB can receive and respond;
- Legislative Affairs and Press do not automatically know;
- Congress receives no offer;
- the public receives no statement;
- unrelated agencies do not change queues;
- later dissemination requires another valid route.

The original blocking exploit is therefore closed.

# 3. Bounded clarifications are closed

## 3.1 C5-02 — review semantics

The repair distinguishes:

- consultation;
- review;
- advice;
- factual validation;
- procedural completeness;
- concurrence;
- nonconcurrence;
- certification;
- clearance;
- approval;
- blocking authority;
- presidential disposition.

Every load-bearing review must identify its source, scope, result type, effect of nonconcurrence, waiver/override authority, deadline, default, and record.

No office gains a veto from the word `clearance`. A legally operative condition also cannot be dismissed as optional advice.

## 3.2 C5-03 — combined offices and multi-role holders

One human holding several roles remains one actor and one population-linked person.

The roles retain distinct:

- mandates;
- assignments;
- queues;
- delegations;
- records;
- reporting lines;
- removal and succession semantics.

The holder does not gain duplicate time. Information access does not automatically bleed across purposes or into campaign use.

Combined offices are permitted only when resources, records, mandatory reviews, supported disagreement, authority, and queues remain reconciled.

A flexible organization chart therefore cannot become one super-office.

## 3.3 C5-04 — reorganization migration

Creating, combining, splitting, transferring, or ending an office/function now requires a canonical reorganization occurrence and migration ledger.

The ledger preserves:

- identities;
- holders and assignments;
- queues and live work;
- decision packages;
- delegations;
- resources and personnel;
- access and records;
- deadlines and obligations;
- required reviews;
- dissent;
- successor owners.

Reorganization cannot erase backlog, reset deadlines, duplicate staff, transfer agency authority through naming, or rewrite prior history.

## 3.4 C5-05 — packet non-locking

An incomplete or disputed packet can remain:

- in assembly;
- under review;
- awaiting source material or clearance;
- returned;
- deferred;
- withdrawn;
- superseded;
- expired;
- available but nonblocking;
- governed by a deadline/default;
- accompanied by continuing delegated action.

A packet in an office queue does not stop time.

When a deadline arrives before required work is complete, the President may receive a partial packet with known gaps and risk, or the opportunity may lapse. The system cannot fabricate completed work or freeze indefinitely.

# 4. Full gate evaluation

## G5-01 — administration and its components remain distinct

**PASS.**

Administration, office, officeholder, assignment, agency, career institution, coordination body, procedure, and decision package are not aliases.

## G5-02 — office continuity and holder interpretation remain distinct

**PASS.**

Offices retain mandates, records, assignments, queues, delegations, and obligations. Holders own private belief, judgment, relationships, and discretionary decisions.

## G5-03 — functional closure does not require one fixed chart

**PASS.**

Functions may be grouped or split subject to ownership, resource, record, access, review, and migration integrity.

## G5-04 — President and Vice President remain distinct

**PASS.**

The Vice President can hold delegated portfolios, relationships, Senate functions, and succession significance without becoming a passive modifier or alternate player-owned President.

## G5-05 — Chief of Staff does not become omniscient gatekeeper

**PASS.**

Operations can route, bundle, schedule, and coordinate only received material and known process. Mandatory external state persists. Suppression requires canonical conduct.

## G5-06 — Staff Secretary does not own substance

**PASS.**

The secretariat owns packet assembly, versioning, clearance status, dissent attachment, and routing—not truth, authority, options, or outcomes.

## G5-07 — policy councils do not own domains or agencies

**PASS.**

They coordinate information and advice. Member institutions retain authority, resources, operations, and records.

## G5-08 — OMB, Counsel, liaison, Personnel, Press, and agencies stay in role

**PASS.**

Each function has explicit ownership and exclusions. Advice, review, liaison, communication, and personnel processing do not become external outcomes.

## G5-09 — official and campaign operations remain separate

**PASS.**

Government resources, authority, confidential records, and staff do not become campaign assets through presidential candidacy.

## G5-10 — assignments and queues are specific

**PASS.**

Every load-bearing assignment has owner, objective, authority, sources, target, dates, resources, status, threshold, uncertainty, and closure. Queues are owner-specific.

## G5-11 — delegation preserves autonomy

**PASS.**

Delegation identifies scope, authority, resources, allowed and reserved actions, reporting, duration, revocation, succession, and default. It cannot grant unavailable authority or guarantee success.

## G5-12 — staff knowledge is bounded

**PASS.**

Information requires receipt, role access, direct experience, briefing, measurement, source, meeting, or memory. Support status remains exact, measured, modeled, bounded, unsupported, or unknown.

## G5-13 — disagreement is grounded

**PASS.**

Dissent arises from evidence, duty, institutional mission, legal/fiscal assumptions, implementation knowledge, relationships, risk, timing, or objective.

## G5-14 — competence is task-specific

**PASS.**

No universal staff score resolves outcomes. Task performance follows relevant expertise, evidence, procedures, resources, queues, access, cooperation, complexity, turnover, and management.

## G5-15 — escalation remains information-bounded

**PASS.**

Every presidential item identifies nominator, source, receipt, lead, support, uncertainty, authority need, deadline, default, dissent, and requested decision.

## G5-16 — routine work can remain below President

**PASS.**

Delegated and rule-bound activity can complete quietly. Significant state can remain unknown or un-escalated.

## G5-17 — appointments and vacancies remain real processes

**PASS.**

Candidate, vetting, selection, nomination, confirmation, commission/assignment, acting service, vacancy, resignation, removal, and replacement do not collapse.

## G5-18 — Cabinet and agencies are not hive minds

**PASS.**

Cabinet is a coordination grouping. Agency leadership, career operations, program offices, legal/financial units, statutory duties, and queues remain distinct.

## G5-19 — transition preserves state

**PASS.**

Agency operations, laws, budgets, court orders, queues, records, and obligations persist. Private knowledge and relationships do not transfer automatically.

## G5-20 — temporary bodies do not multiply authority

**PASS.**

Councils/task forces require mandate, chair, members, scope, resources, reporting, records, and sunset. Members act through their own authority.

## G5-21 — hostile proofs remain coherent

**PASS.**

Employment data, Housing implementation, legislative compromise, cyber incident, investigation, vacancy, delegated Cabinet disagreement, and presidential transition can all proceed without a global staff actor, hidden drama director, or direct cross-owner mutation.

## G5-22 — player decisions are strategic, not clerical

**PASS.**

The President chooses people, portfolios, delegations, thresholds, conflict resolution, risk, appointments, analysis, personal intervention, and defaults. Routine memo routing and agency work remain delegated.
