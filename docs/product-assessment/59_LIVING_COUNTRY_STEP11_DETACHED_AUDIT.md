# 2. R11-01 — BLOCKING: January 3 versus January 20 is not resolved by the accepted criteria

## 2.2 The untested alternative

A coherent January 20 package could instead be:

```text

→ January 3 new-Congress assignments and organization
→ January 6 electoral count
```

Those January 3–20 occurrences would not be omitted or treated as traits.

- avoids a 17-day outgoing-administration live segment;
- begins the two-term prehistory structure with a complete term rather than a transition fragment;
- reduces early staff/agency/control-binding complexity;

- aligns the first generated administration's Day 1 with its inauguration;
- better matches the earlier product hypothesis of a January 20 calibration with the 119th Congress already seated.

## 2.3 Why the candidate's reason is insufficient

“Let the generator own the transitions directly” is not automatically superior.

Step 10 already permits occurrence-required generated backstory.

The relevant question is whether the 17-day interval needs live lower-resolution simulation to affect later play.

If only these facts matter:

- Congress was seated;
- leadership and committees were organized;
- the electoral count resolved;
- the incoming administration's entitlement was established;
- transition records and commitments existed;

then compressed frozen occurrences may be sufficient.

If, instead, generated January 3–20 actor behavior is expected to create variable laws, bargains, crises, appointments, or durable political relationships, the candidate must identify those consumers and show why January 3 is worth the additional live interval.

It does not.

# 3. C11-02 — distinguish calibration controls from generated election outcomes

The candidate appropriately leaves exact political aggregate calibration unresolved.

But the final authority should make one distinction binding.

A synthetic 2024 election can be generated in several different ways:

1. **fully generated within configured plausibility constraints**;
2. **conditioned on declared aggregate controls**, such as chamber balance, party of the incoming President, or state-result ranges;
3. **calibrated to actual aggregate 2024 outcomes while replacing individual actors**;
4. **selected from a declared scenario family**.

Those are not the same historical claim.

If the package fixes real or configured aggregate results first, the election generator is constructing a compatible history.

It must not claim those outcomes emerged unconstrained.

Required clarification:

> Every fixed political aggregate or outcome used to condition the 2024 election/backstory must be declared before generation with its source, support, and status. Generated actors, campaigns, state results, and mandates must reconcile within those controls. The generator may not both import an outcome as calibration and later cite the same outcome as independent proof that its campaign model produced it.

This is bounded because Step 10 already prohibits teleological backstory.

# 4. C11-03 — tighten the retrospective-source interval rule

The candidate correctly distinguishes source publication time from reference period and actor knowledge.

The final authority should close one edge case.

A source published after the seam may have a reference interval that:

- ends before the seam;
- straddles the seam;
- occurs entirely after the seam.

A source whose reference interval straddles or follows the seam cannot be used wholesale as an opening-state observation.

Required clarification:

> A later-published source may initialize or refine hidden opening state only for the portion of its reference universe and interval that is legitimately attributable to pre-seam state under a declared transformation. A straddling annual or multi-year source may require decomposition, modeling, bounds, or rejection. It may not import post-seam realized outcomes into the opening root merely because the product is labeled with an earlier reference year.

Examples:

- 2024 ACS products describe a pre-2025 reference period and are admissible with release-time separation;
- a calendar-year 2025 outcome is not an exact January 20 opening fact;
- a 2020–2024 five-year estimate is a period estimate, not a point-in-time January 2025 observation;
- later revisions may improve pre-seam estimates without entering actor knowledge early.
