Frozen evidence boundary:

- Accepted production baseline: `44c1724962830225e6fc34f41d0df0cfdb7dfec0`
- Entirely unaccepted Stage 1 candidate: `a7e04ca78ba1ccb06d8dc3a4dfb0d43389804144`

This report is a game-reality assessment, not a roadmap. It does not recommend implementation order, Early Access scope, or the next proof. It determines what actually exists, how much of it is reusable, what a normal player can reach and understand, where genuine strategy exists, and what remains absent.

## 1. Assessment method

A system does not count as strategic gameplay merely because it has canonical state, tests, autonomous owners, or a production projection.

For this assessment, strategic gameplay requires all of the following:

1. the player can perceive a consequential situation;
2. the player can choose among materially different action or deliberate inaction;
3. the choice encounters a constraint, opportunity cost, or credible risk;
4. resolution includes autonomous actors, uncertainty, or delayed consequences;
5. the result changes later decisions rather than merely terminating one chain.

### Rating scale

Each domain is rated from 0–4 on separate axes.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | Substantially absent |
| **1** | Fixture/proof only, or a very thin capability |
| **2** | Working but narrow |
| **3** | Strong reusable foundation, still incomplete as product gameplay |
| **4** | Broad and mature at the assessed axis; this does not imply final polish |

### Axes

| Code | Axis | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | Causal integrity | Does the domain have authoritative owners, valid transitions, and protected boundaries? |
| **G** | Generality | Can it support multiple configurations and situations without embedding the current fixture? |
| **B** | Breadth | How much of the real domain is represented? |
| **R** | Production reachability | Can a normal player encounter and operate it through the production application? |
| **L** | Player legibility | Can the player understand state, uncertainty, actors, choices, and consequences? |
| **S** | Strategic agency | Does it create consequential choices, constraints, and foregone opportunities? |
| **A** | Autonomous depth | Do relevant actors or institutions independently choose and resolve outcomes? |
| **F** | Feedback continuity | Do outcomes alter later situations and decisions? |
| **I** | Fixture independence | Is the capability content-driven and reusable rather than tied to this one route? |
| **E** | Evidence confidence | How strongly is the rating supported by code, tests, data, and production surfaces? |

A high score in one column does not compensate for a low score in another. Congress can simultaneously have excellent causal integrity and poor strategic agency. Geography can have broad real-world coverage and essentially no player legibility.

## 2. Executive verdict

The accepted baseline is a **strong production-reachable causal vertical and reusable political-simulation foundation**, but only a **very narrow presidential strategy game**.

The repository contains real owners and transitions for Congress, political organizations, public finance, federal implementation, selected recipients, intergovernmental relationships, Housing material execution, measurement and information, Population political response, courts, elections, succession, time, and persistence. Those systems are not fake wrappers around policy meters. They preserve distinctions the target game needs.

However, the production product instantiates almost the smallest possible content set across those systems:

- one anonymous incumbent administration;
- one Housing proposal;
- one active legislative procedure;
- three synthetic political organizations;
- one HUD/HOME implementation family;
- two detailed recipient/project routes;
- one Housing measurement and administration-claim route;
- one bounded legal case;
- one anonymous two-ticket presidential election;
- one succession.

The player normally experiences those systems as a mostly sequential route. Most available actions request the next necessary transition in that route. The game contains autonomous actors, but it rarely gives the President competing objectives, scarce attention, alternative uses of resources, or a reason to sacrifice one opportunity for another.

The core reality is therefore:

> **The simulation is causally deep but situationally sparse. Institutional autonomy is stronger than presidential strategy. Structural national scale is much broader than experiential national life.**

The baseline proves that a deep presidential strategy game can be built on this substrate. It does not yet instantiate enough of that game to deliver the original fantasy.

## 4. Architecture, time, persistence, and history

### 4.1 Configuration and causal ownership

**Reality classification:** strong reusable foundation.

The strongest part of the project is the separation of canonical owners and player-safe projections.

The production state in `src/sim/integrated-runtime.ts` contains distinct roots for:

- legislative state;
- geography;
- weighted Population;
- electoral topology;
- institutional time, terms, elections, and administrations;
- program implementation and public finance;
- Housing;
- Information;
- legal contest.

The configuration loader authenticates content identities and artifact hashes. U.S.-specific counts, names, dates, institutions, and rules live under `src/content/us-v0/`, not inside generic simulation modules. The I10 composition contract explicitly forbids enactment-to-money, money-to-material, material-to-Population, information-to-election-winner, court-to-retroactive-history, and player-to-autonomous-outcome shortcuts.

This architecture is not merely aspirational documentation. The repair history repeatedly removed commands or mutations from the wrong owner. It is a genuine asset.

What it does not do is create player strategy automatically. The owner graph establishes who may cause an outcome. It does not establish what competing outcomes the President must choose among.

### 4.2 Time, save/load, and persistence

**Reality classification:** strong reusable foundation.

The scheduler supports ordered institutional boundaries, autonomous resolution, coarse/fine invariance, term rollover, election stages, administration transfer, and continued world advancement after ControlBinding loss. Save/load binds the world to exact configuration and artifact identities.

This is unusually mature for a walking skeleton. It supports long-running causal consequences and makes a four-year game technically plausible.

Player-facing weaknesses remain:

- time is mostly “advance to the next owner or boundary” rather than a legible governing calendar;
- the player does not see a term plan, budget calendar, legislative calendar, campaign calendar, or competing deadlines;
- most boundaries advance one fixed chain rather than several concurrent workstreams;
- the scheduler creates chronology but not yet strategic time pressure.

### 4.3 History and Record

**Reality classification:** mechanically present but not player-legible.

History exists in several forms:

- legislative procedure history;
- enacted legal sources;
- administration history;
- institutional occurrences;
- project histories;
- implementation records and intentions;
- measurements, claims, deliveries, and responses;
- court filings, rulings, orders, stays, appeals, and compliance;
- election snapshots, results, certificates, declarations, and entitlements.

But the integrated U.S. runtime does not expose one coherent player-facing historical record. `ProductionGameView` contains current summaries and counts, not a causal timeline. The original architecture’s broad HistoricalRecord concept is therefore only partially realized in the U.S. product.

The world remembers more than the player can understand. That weakens both legibility and strategy because the player cannot easily answer:

- what did this administration decide;
- what changed because of it;
- what remained outside its control;
- which promise was compromised;
- which actor supported, opposed, or defected;
- which result is measured versus inferred;
- what the election is now evaluating.

## 5. Presidency, opening state, and agenda

### 5.1 Presidency and ControlBinding

**Reality classification:** strong authority boundary; thin player role.

The President exists as an office, actor assignment, administration head, and ControlBinding owner. The Vice President exists separately. The player loses authority when the officeholder or term changes, and the world persists.

That is a strong answer to “who does the player control?”

It is not yet a strong answer to “who is my administration?”

The default U.S. topology contains:

- an anonymous incumbent President scaffold;
- an anonymous incumbent Vice-President scaffold;
- the Executive Office institution;
- HUD, OMB, Census, NARA, DOJ, and two courts as institutions.

It does not contain a player-legible administration roster, Cabinet, senior staff, agency heads, appointment history, governing coalition, internal disagreements, competence profile, or campaign team. The player is technically bound to the executive office but experiences little institutional identity beyond that permission.

### 5.2 Generated opening state and political history

**Reality classification:** substantially absent.

The default scenario starts on **August 22, 2026**, during the 119th Congress. The initial administration becomes canonical at scenario start and remains until the configured January 20, 2029 transfer.

The game does not possess canonical state for:

- the prior presidential election;
- popular or electoral result that created the current administration;
- inauguration;
- predecessor;
- term number;
- prior Congresses;
- inherited legislative history beyond selected HOME legal/program records;
- campaign commitments;
- party or coalition victory;
- political mandate;
- previous administration performance;
- recent national economic or social trends.

The current administration therefore does not emerge from simulated or configured political history. It simply exists at the scenario epoch.

This matters because the player cannot know what country or political moment they inherited. The absence is not primarily a prose problem. The canonical facts required to generate the prose do not exist yet.

### 5.3 Agenda setting and competing priorities

**Reality classification:** one working effort, not an agenda game.

The production configuration contains exactly one active proposal: the **Housing Delivery and Capacity Agenda**. Its five dimensions are meaningful underneath:

- appropriation magnitude;
- recipient flexibility;
- compliance burden;
- geographic distribution;
- administrative-capacity support.

Those dimensions map to legal appropriation and operative implementation rules. That is a sound policy-design seam.

But the normal production action `agenda:balanced-delivery` submits exactly the same `{7, 7, 6, 7, 8}` values already configured as the proposal’s initial dimensions. It does not ask the player to choose a policy design. Stage 1 correctly classifies that action as routine background and auto-processes it.

There is also only one active agenda/procedure at a time. The runtime can archive a procedure and begin a new one after a congressional boundary, but it cannot currently represent several simultaneous administration priorities or bills competing for attention.

Missing strategic properties include:

- choosing Housing versus another issue;
- choosing several priorities but accepting that some will be neglected;
- deciding how much administration and congressional attention each receives;
- managing campaign commitments;
- abandoning one effort to rescue another;
- responding to an inherited problem not chosen by the player;
- protecting implementation while pursuing new legislation.

The current agenda is a configured route entrance, not a presidential portfolio.

## 6. Congress, parties, and political organizations

### 6.1 Congress and legislative procedure

**Reality classification:** strong reusable engine; working but narrow game.

Congress has substantial real machinery:

- 435 House offices and 100 Senate offices;
- active office assignments;
- individual political actors;
- House and Senate rules;
- sponsorship;
- chamber consideration gates;
- amendments;
- individual recorded votes;
- extended debate and cloture;
- Vice-Presidential tie-breaking;
- text exchange;
- presentment;
- signature, veto, no-signature, and override routes;
- end-of-Congress expiry;
- procedure history and new-Congress rollover.

Individual actors evaluate proposal dimensions, have reservation ranges, negotiation memory, commitments, organization pressure, and deterministic autonomy. Votes consider actor reservations, exact-version commitments, organization records, procedural kind, and autonomous willingness. This is a credible reusable substrate.

The product-level limitations are severe:

- only one bill is active;
- there are no committees;
- no leadership agenda or scheduling competition;
- no party caucus strategy;
- no durable factions beyond three configuration organizations;
- no member career, ambition, seniority, ideological history, or committee power;
- no constituency conditions feeding member priorities;
- no member election vulnerability visible to the player;
- no bargaining across bills, appointments, budgets, investigations, or district interests;
- no reason to preserve a relationship for a future vote;
- no concurrent opposition legislation;
- no routine congressional developments independent of the Housing bill.

Production reachability is also narrower than the engine. The application exposes sponsor outreach and organization outreach, but not the underlying actor-negotiation offer model or meaningful amendment design.

### 6.2 Sponsorship choice

The sponsor decision is a real autonomous seam: the President chooses whom to approach, and the member independently accepts or declines.

But strategic information is insufficient. The default actors are anonymous scaffold occupants. At baseline they appear as raw IDs; Stage 1 humanizes them as “Representative for [district]” and exposes a coarse staff posture.

The player still does not know:

- who the person is;
- their party or faction;
- committee position;
- constituency conditions;
- relationship with the administration;
- electoral risk;
- what choosing one sponsor sacrifices;
- whether a sponsor can shape the bill or coalition differently.

So sponsorship is a genuine choice in formal structure but a weak choice in strategy.

### 6.3 Parties, factions, and organizations

**Reality classification:** autonomous scaffold organizations, not a national political system.

The current U.S. content defines exactly three organizations:

- Delivery Coalition;
- Fiscal Compliance Coalition;
- Regional Bargaining Caucus.

Each receives deterministic House/Senate membership quotas that collectively cover all legislators. Each has a posture across the five Housing dimensions and can coordinate support or opposition through its own owner.

This proves that organizations can influence members without owning their votes.

It does not yet create:

- Democratic and Republican parties or fictional equivalents;
- party leadership and formal caucuses;
- ideological or regional factions that persist across policy domains;
- activist, labor, business, professional, state, donor, or advocacy organizations outside Congress;
- resources, mobilization, endorsements, campaign support, or public communication;
- cross-issue bargaining;
- organization histories or relationships with the administration;
- a governing coalition and organized opposition.

The production interaction also allows the player to request support from every available organization without cost. The organizations resolve autonomously, but there is no offer, concession, relationship cost, attention cost, or reason not to contact all of them.

Autonomy exists. Strategy does not yet follow from it.

## 7. Executive administration, public finance, and federalism

### 7.1 Executive and administrative legal levers

**Reality classification:** one meaningful bounded route.

The accepted production runtime lets the administration issue a configured relationship-requalification rejection that triggers a legal contest. Later, the administration can comply, delay, contest, or not comply with a judicial order and may request a stay.

This is one of the few places where the player has visibly different courses of action with distinct institutional consequences.

But it remains a single configured route. There is no general production surface for:

- executive orders;
- rulemaking;
- enforcement priorities;
- emergency authority;
- appointments;
- procurement direction;
- interagency directives;
- waivers as routine strategy;
- administrative reinterpretation across several programs;
- use of the bully pulpit;
- congressional consultation before executive action;
- choosing among lawful, aggressive, and norm-breaking approaches across issues.

The architecture can support attempted action and contested authority. The product does not yet provide a portfolio of executive choices.

### 7.2 Cabinet, agencies, and personnel

**Reality classification:** institution seams exist; gameplay substantially absent.

HUD, OMB, Census, NARA, and DOJ exist as institutions and own relevant records or processes. This supports causal independence.

But the game has no canonical Cabinet or senior-administration layer:

- no named or generated secretaries;
- no agency-head assignments;
- no appointment or confirmation gameplay;
- no competence, loyalty, ideology, capacity, or scandal state;
- no internal advice competition;
- no presidential staffing decisions;
- no agency priorities competing for administrative attention;
- no Cabinet meetings or generated staff briefings grounded in personnel state.

The player directs requests through one administration origin, but does not manage an administration.

### 7.3 Public finance and federal budget

**Reality classification:** deep one-program chain; national fiscal state absent.

Public finance is one of the strongest causal domains. The game separates:

- enacted legal budget authority;
- public-finance recognition;
- fiscal control/apportionment;
- program allocation;
- award;
- obligation;
- recipient commitment;
- activity;
- draw;
- payment;
- material inputs.

Operative legal terms alter recipient discretion, compliance burden, geographic priority, administrative capacity, and processing latency.

The authenticated I6 artifact explicitly classifies coverage as partial and states:

- two detailed recipient routes do not represent the national HOME universe;
- aggregate outlays are not fully reconciled to project draws;
- administrative and fiscal records do not create physical Housing truth;
- **national balance is not instantiated**.

There is no canonical model of:

- total federal revenue;
- taxation;
- mandatory and discretionary spending;
- annual budget baseline;
- deficit;
- debt;
- interest costs;
- borrowing constraints;
- appropriations across agencies;
- budget reconciliation;
- shutdown risk;
- fiscal room across policy priorities.

Therefore the game has excellent fiscal-execution semantics but no federal-budget strategy.

### 7.4 States, local government, and federalism

**Reality classification:** sound relationship ownership; narrow lived federation.

The game contains all state/DC jurisdiction identities and selected intergovernmental relationships. Local members can enter or leave a consortium route without that decision being falsely promoted to statewide refusal. Federal determinations, recipient choices, and relationship state are distinct.

But most states are identities, population controls, Housing controls, geography, and election units—not political governments.

The current world lacks:

- governors;
- state legislatures;
- state parties and elections;
- state budgets;
- attorneys general as broad actors;
- state administrative capacity outside the selected program;
- state policy positions across multiple domains;
- interstate competition and coalition;
- routine federal-state negotiation;
- local governments outside the selected HOME evidence routes;
- state-level crises or economic conditions.

Federalism is causally correct where instantiated. It is not yet a recurring national strategic arena.

## 8. Housing, BABA, geography, and Population

### 8.1 Housing material domain

**Reality classification:** deep material foundation; narrow presidential strategy.

Housing is the most developed national-condition domain.

The authenticated initialization artifact contains state/DC measures for:

- housing stock;
- vacancy;
- rent burden;
- owner and renter cost exposure;
- margins of error;
- permit-related capacity priors.

It initializes 51 controls and 53 regions, including more detailed Colorado and Texas catchments. Selected projects can be blocked, delayed, fail, become physically complete, later become usable, add units exactly once, and create local material experience.

Validated fiscal, administrative, compliance, and waiver records enter Housing only as inputs. They do not directly set project outcomes. This is exactly the causal depth the broader game should preserve.

The current limits are:

- only selected projects receive detailed dynamic treatment;
- project changes are tiny relative to the national stock;
- no general housing demand model;
- no migration or household formation;
- no wages, income growth, interest rates, credit, or mortgage market;
- no land or zoning system;
- no construction labor or material market;
- no broad state/local housing-policy system;
- no interacting tax, infrastructure, or environmental policy;
- no player-facing national Housing overview or map in the baseline production projection.

Housing therefore proves a material domain. It does not yet supply a full national Housing strategy game, much less a multi-domain presidency.

### 8.2 BABA/domestic-preference waiver

**Reality classification:** mechanically present but not normal gameplay.

The implementation system supports a bounded future waiver request with:

- required supporting records;
- grant of a scoped waiver;
- denial;
- return for supplemental records;
- a later review boundary;
- typed material inputs such as waiver terms, input availability, or compliance hold.

That is a promising decision family because the outcomes can create tradeoffs among compliance, timing, supply availability, and delivery.

At the accepted I10 baseline, it is not exposed through `availablePlayerActions` or the baseline React UI. It exists through lower application/session methods and tests, not the normal production route.

It therefore counts as a system capability, not current gameplay.

### 8.3 Geography

**Reality classification:** broad real structure, almost entirely invisible.

The U.S. content includes:

- 51 state/DC geographies;
- 435 congressional-district geographies;
- state and district polygons;
- constituency nesting;
- two project locators;
- Electoral College allocation geography.

This is a strong foundation for maps, state variation, congressional constituencies, material conditions, and elections.

The production UI exposes none of it. There is no national map, state comparison, district view, project location view, electoral map, or geographic drill-down.

The game possesses the physical outline of the country without letting the player experience it.

### 8.4 Population and demography

**Reality classification:** excellent aggregate mechanics; thin social model.

The runtime initializes 51 unequal resident-population controls and 106 weighted cohorts. It conserves represented people through deterministic split/merge operations. Cohorts can hold material experience, information exposure, political belief, attribution, salience, candidate preference, turnout disposition, and eligibility projections.

This is the correct kind of scalable substrate for a country simulation.

Current cohort dimensions are very limited:

- state residence;
- renter/non-renter material-exposure class;
- state background versus selected project catchment;
- selected Housing material references;
- selected information references;
- political response state;
- CVAP-like eligibility proxy.

The game does not currently model within Population:

- age;
- race/ethnicity;
- sex/gender;
- household structure;
- income;
- employment;
- occupation or industry;
- education;
- health;
- immigration status or generation;
- urban/rural/metropolitan variation;
- religion;
- class or wealth;
- movement between states;
- births, deaths, or demographic change.

The current Population is therefore a strong conservative container for future correlated conditions, not yet a living American society.

## 9. Information, public life, courts, and elections

### 9.1 Information and public response

**Reality classification:** strong causal chain; one bounded information fixture.

The information architecture genuinely separates:

```text
material truth
→ observation
→ measurement artifact
→ release
→ claim
→ delivery
→ exposure
→ Population response
→ election intake
```

Measurements can lag and contain bounded error. Claims do not overwrite measurements. Exposure is separate from release. Direct experience can alter response. Population owns belief, attribution, salience, preference, and turnout.

That is a major reusable foundation.

The U.S. production content instantiates only one route:

- one Housing observation over two projects/regions;
- one official Housing report;
- one administration claim about bounded program performance;
- one generic bounded public channel;
- exposure to two Colorado project-catchment cohorts;
- one response family;
- one later election intake.

The current game has no lived news or information environment:

- no news organizations;
- no journalists;
- no headlines generated from world developments;
- no competing national stories;
- no opposition communication route in the default U.S. content;
- no polling;
- no press briefings;
- no leaks, investigations, scandals, or corrections;
- no media reach, credibility, fragmentation, or regional variation;
- no issue-attention competition;
- no player choice over the default administration claim.

The system can support news as a projection of world state. The product has not instantiated that world.

### 9.2 Courts and legal conflict

**Reality classification:** strongest current strategic branch; one deterministic case.

The legal contest separates:

- final administrative act;
- filing and eligibility;
- docketing;
- interim request;
- ruling;
- legal interpretation;
- order;
- notice;
- compliance state;
- stay;
- appeal;
- later administrative action;
- continued proceedings after succession.

The player can choose comply, delay, contest, or noncomply and can request a stay. Court and appellate owners resolve outcomes independently. Orders constrain future implementation without rewriting completed material history.

This is the closest the current product comes to a real presidential strategic decision.

Its limits are:

- one claimant;
- one program relationship;
- two anonymous judicial actors;
- one trial forum and one appellate forum;
- configured deterministic merits and default outcomes;
- no broader legal docket;
- no judicial composition history;
- no appointments or confirmations;
- no competing legal cases;
- no litigation-resource or legitimacy tradeoff;
- little player-facing explanation of why one response is politically or administratively costly.

The branch is real but under-contextualized.

### 9.3 Elections and succession

**Reality classification:** deep institutional machinery; campaign game absent.

The election system includes:

- 51 state/DC electorate snapshots;
- weighted ballots from Population state;
- state popular results;
- attestations;
- elector appointments;
- elector certificates;
- congressional declaration;
- successor entitlement;
- January 20 office transfer;
- new administration and assignment identities;
- ControlBinding continuation or loss;
- persistent world state after transfer;
- congressional turnover at term boundaries.

This is substantially more rigorous than a timer that checks approval.

But the default election content is a scaffold:

- one anonymous player-aligned ticket;
- one anonymous opposition ticket;
- no parties;
- no primaries;
- no candidate emergence;
- no campaign organization;
- no fundraising, advertising, field effort, debate, endorsement, or scandal;
- no incumbent campaign choices;
- no opposition governing critique beyond the one Housing response route;
- no broad issue coalition;
- deterministic fallback preferences for unresolved cohorts.

Most of the country enters the election through fallback state because only a tiny Housing audience receives the configured public-response route.

The machinery is strong. The election is not yet the culmination of a richly played term.

| Business / industry | No domain | Housing capacity prior exists; there is no general firm, sector, investment, production, or supply-chain state |

| Public mood / issue salience | Very limited | One targeted Housing response; otherwise neutral/unresolved scaffold and election fallback |

This means the opening game cannot truthfully generate a broad State of the Nation. Most required facts do not yet exist canonically.

## 11. Production action audit

The accepted baseline’s normal gameplay authority is the `availablePlayerActions` projection plus `dispatchPlayerCommand` and `advanceProductionWorld`. Lower session APIs and audit injections do not count as ordinary player gameplay.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set a balanced delivery agenda | No; submits the same configured values already present | No | No | Opens the fixed route | Routine operation, not a choice |
| Begin sponsor search | No | No | No | Exposes sponsor candidates | Routine operation, not a choice |

| Request support from an organization | Several organizations exist, but all can be contacted | No cost or reason not to contact all | Yes; organization supports or opposes | Alters pressure and vote likelihood | Autonomous interaction, weak strategy |

### Decision-density verdict

The current game contains several valid institutional choices, but most actions fail at least one strategic-gameplay requirement.

The dominant interaction pattern is:

```text
one required request
→ autonomous owner resolves
→ one next required request appears
```

This is not merely a UI problem. The underlying production content offers no competing recipient, no alternative program, no simultaneous bill, no attention budget, no national emergency, no agency conflict, and almost no reason to preserve one opportunity by abandoning another.

## 12. Stage 1 candidate delta

Stage 1 is correctly classified as an **entirely unaccepted opening usability candidate**.

### What Stage 1 genuinely improves

It introduces useful interaction concepts:

- human-readable role and institutional boundary;
- humanized sponsor labels instead of actor and assignment IDs;
- a distinction among decision required, strategic opportunity, important development, and background;
- automatic processing of the two fixed opening operations;
- “advance until attention” rather than repeatedly clicking advance;
- a decision receipt separating the player’s choice, immediate result, and what remains unresolved;
- an Overview → Details hierarchy for the opening decision.

These are legitimate improvements to interaction grammar.

### What Stage 1 does not change

It adds no canonical state for:

- the country inherited;
- prior election or inauguration;
- predecessor or term number;
- national conditions;
- parties or governing coalition;
- Cabinet or administration roster;
- multiple priorities;
- new political actors or developments;
- news;
- budget position;
- campaign state;
- replayability.

It adds no new strategic choice. The sponsor choice is the same underlying choice as I10, only more readable.

### Hardcoded opening-content problem

The Stage 1 projection directly authors a Housing situation, objective, institutional-boundary paragraph, and status text. The React opening directly says “A new administration term has begun” and “The housing agenda needs a path through Congress.”

Those statements are not generated from canonical political history. The default scenario begins on August 22, 2026 with an already-existing anonymous incumbent administration and no prior election or inauguration state. Stage 1 therefore demonstrates an authored presentation fixture, not the requested generated assumption-of-office briefing.

That is acceptable as evidence for a bounded UI experiment. It is not a solution to the product problem.

### Stage 1 is not a full functional replacement route

The Stage 1 test explicitly limits itself to opening usability and sponsor selection. In the React UI, only sponsor actions receive rendered decision buttons and a handler. Other future attention actions fall into generic explanatory text without controls. When an attention item exists, the advance control is hidden.

Therefore Stage 1 does not preserve the full baseline I10 production journey through the UI. It is an opening proof, not a full six-surface functional interface.

### Stage 1 verdict

> **Stage 1 makes the exposed skeleton easier to read at the first decision. It neither expands the political world nor transforms the fixed route into presidential strategy, and it is incomplete as a route-level production UI.**

## 13. Cross-cutting game-reality findings

### 13.1 The world is causally deep but situationally sparse

The engine can distinguish many kinds of facts. The current scenario creates very few simultaneous situations from them. There is one principal Housing effort, one program, one claim route, one case, and one election.

### 13.2 Structural breadth is not gameplay breadth

The repository can truthfully say it has:

- 51 state/DC population controls;
- 435 House offices;
- 100 Senate offices;
- 535 legislative actors;
- state and district geography;
- 538 electoral votes;
- national Housing controls.

But the player’s meaningful world is still mostly one Housing chain involving a handful of configured organizations, recipients, projects, and audiences.

### 13.3 Institutional autonomy has outpaced presidential agency

The project correctly removed illegitimate player control over sponsors, organizations, votes, fiscal recognition, courts, material outcomes, and elections.

But it has not yet replaced those removed clicks with enough higher-level presidential decisions about priorities, concessions, relationships, timing, resource allocation, communication, or acceptable failure.

The result is a world that acts independently but often leaves the player with only the next request.

### 13.4 Feedback reaches the election more often than it returns to governing

The Housing route can alter material conditions, measurements, beliefs, preferences, turnout, and election results.

But those consequences rarely return before the election to change:

- the next legislative agenda;
- congressional bargaining;
- agency priorities;
- state relationships;
- public communication;
- fiscal allocation;
- crisis response;
- campaign strategy.

The causal chain is long, but it is still mostly linear.

### 13.5 The player sees workflow state, not country state

The baseline production UI displays action buttons, raw IDs, stages, counters, legal status, and election stage. It does not display the country as a strategic object.

The player cannot see:

- a State of the Nation;
- broad trends;
- geographic variation;
- competing issues;
- administration identity;
- congressional composition as a political institution;
- a national budget;
- public attention;
- a term record.

### 13.6 History exists as records, not inherited political context

The world can preserve what happens after scenario start. It does not explain what happened before the player arrived, and the player cannot inspect a coherent generated record afterward.

### 13.7 Replayability is architectural potential, not current product reality

Configurations can vary hashes, schedules, actor scaffolds, legal outcomes, election fallback, and other content. Tests use variants to prove capability.

The default game starts from the same anonymous administration, proposal, organizations, program, projects, claim, case, and election scaffold. There is no exposed scenario generation or differentiated start.

### 13.8 The game currently rewards completing the route, not governing well

Because most actions are required to continue the accepted chain, the obvious objective is to advance the proof. There is little basis for a player-developed governing doctrine or competing definition of success.

## 14. Classification summary

### Strong reusable foundations

- configuration identity, artifact authentication, and U.S./engine separation;
- canonical ownership and player-safe projections;
- deterministic scheduler, save/load, and persistent succession;
- legislative procedure and individual vote ownership;
- political-actor reservations, memory, commitments, organization pressure, and autonomy;
- fiscal/administrative/recipient/material separation;
- Housing material transitions and local aggregation;
- conservative weighted-Population refinement and response ownership;
- measurement, claim, delivery, exposure, and belief separation;
- legal contest, scoped orders, compliance, stays, and appeals;
- election procedure and office transfer.

### Working but narrow

- one U.S. legislative route;
- one HUD/HOME program family;
- selected fiscal and recipient relationships;
- selected Housing projects;
- one Housing information route;
- one legal dispute;
- one presidential election and two congressional rollovers;
- production reachability of the I3–I9 causal vertical.

- the fixed “balanced delivery” agenda package;
- three non-historical political organizations;
- anonymous congressional, executive, opposition, and judicial actors;
- deterministic organization membership scaffold;
- deterministic unresolved-election fallback;
- two detailed project/recipient routes;
- one administration Housing claim;
- one bounded public channel and two Colorado audience cohorts;
- one Arapahoe legal case;

- national Housing controls and uncertainty;

- meaningful amendment and direct actor-negotiation APIs;
- BABA waiver decision family;
- Housing project and region state;
- detailed Population state;
- domain histories and causal records;

### Missing strategic depth

- competing presidential priorities;
- several simultaneous bills and implementation efforts;
- agenda bandwidth and foregone opportunity;
- party/faction strategy;
- committee and leadership politics;
- durable congressional relationships;
- national budget and fiscal tradeoffs;

- generated prior election, inauguration, predecessor, term history, and mandate;

- macroeconomy, employment, wages, inflation, poverty, and inequality;
- health;
- crime/public safety;

## 15. Step 4 conclusion

The accepted baseline is not a hollow codebase. It contains a legitimately reusable causal simulation foundation and several unusually rigorous institutional seams.

It is also not yet a deep presidential political strategy game.

The product gap is not well described as “add more systems.” Many systems already exist. The gap is:

- too few live national situations;
- too little canonical opening context;
- too little player-legible country state;
- too few simultaneous priorities;
- too little opportunity cost;
- too little recurring relationship strategy;
- too little feedback into later governing decisions;
- too much fixture dependence at the production-content layer.

The most accurate current product description is:

> **A production-reachable U.S. presidential governing vertical that proves a strong causal substrate through one Housing-centered route, but exposes only a small number of genuine strategic choices and does not yet present a living country, a generated presidency, or a dense four-year governing game.**

This map deliberately makes no roadmap recommendation. It establishes the reality the later game definition and product master plan must answer.
