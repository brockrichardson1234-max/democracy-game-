# 1. Verdict

## **REVISE — 1 blocking finding, 4 bounded clarifications**

The candidate has the right center:

> **A provenance-preserving information ecology rather than one NewsSystem.**

It successfully separates reality, evidence, claims, framing, publication, platform distribution, exposure, recipient cognition, polling, political action, and elections. It gives journalism autonomous investigative causality without allowing it to manufacture underlying misconduct. It preserves proposition and derivative lineage, allows false and uncertain beliefs, rejects universal exposure and direct approval modifiers, and provides strong quiet-condition and concentrated-misinformation proofs.

However, one cross-authority seam remains open:

> The candidate does not close how one exact human participates simultaneously as an ordinary population member, individualized actor, officeholder, organization member, platform account holder, and information recipient without duplicate exposure, duplicated cognition, or leakage between private role knowledge and public-population belief.

That gap is blocking because it can undo Step 3’s one-population rule and Step 4’s actor/population linkage contract inside the very system that updates belief.

Four additional wording clarifications should be made controlling with the repair.

# 2. What passes cleanly

## P8-01 — Reality, evidence, claim, and belief remain separate

The candidate never lets a story create the event it describes.

A speaker may be honest, mistaken, strategic, deceptive, or overconfident. The artifact records the proposition and presentation. The recipient separately decides what to trust and believe.

This satisfies the Step 7 watchpoint: actors may produce bad analysis, while claimed confidence remains distinct from actual methodological support.

## P8-02 — Proposition and derivative lineage are load-bearing

The candidate correctly rejects treating a multi-claim story as one binary truth object.

It also distinguishes:

- shared proposition;
- different framing;
- repeated exposure;
- derivative publication;
- independent corroboration.

Ten outlets copying one report can create repetition without creating ten independent evidentiary confirmations.

## P8-03 — Journalism has valid initiative

The investigation grammar begins from a tip, anomaly, source, filing, direct observation, record, or data pattern.

The journalist/outlet must:

- receive the lead;
- allocate scarce work;
- obtain or generate evidence;
- assess corroboration and legal risk;
- decide whether to publish, narrow, delay, continue, or abandon.

No scandal appears because the President needs pressure.

## P8-04 — Media entities and decision loci remain plural

Parent owner, outlet, newsroom, desk, journalist, editor, standards/legal review, platform policy, automated ranking process, and syndication are not collapsed into “the media.”

Step 4’s one-entity rule remains visible.

## P8-05 — Platform distribution is bounded

The candidate separates:

- content existence;
- platform admission;
- moderation;
- recommendation eligibility;
- ranking;
- presentation;
- delivery;
- notice;
- belief.

It permits personalization from platform-held records and proxies while rejecting direct access to hidden canonical belief.

## P8-06 — Recipient cognition is not deterministic

Notice, comprehension, source recognition, trust, memory, belief, attribution, salience, preference, sharing, and later action remain distinct.

Recipients may misunderstand, forget, distrust accurate reporting, trust weak claims, retain uncertainty, or hold false beliefs.

## P8-07 — Corrections preserve history

Corrections, qualifications, updates, and retractions create new artifacts and occurrences. They do not delete original publication, exposure, belief, or political action.

The correction may reach a different audience and may introduce the original claim to someone who had never seen it.

## P8-08 — Misinformation and influence are not universal

The candidate permits concentrated, failed, detected, ignored, believed, or counterproductive operations.

Bot accounts do not create citizen weight.

Foreign influence does not directly set election results.

## P8-09 — Public attention remains fragmented

Recipient-specific attention and channel use can be finite.

There is no universal PublicAttention, Newsworthiness, MediaCredibility, or Virality owner.

## P8-10 — Presidential receipt remains bounded

A viral story does not directly interrupt the player.

The administration learns through monitoring, inquiries, polling, staff, agencies, lawmakers, governors, journalists, organizations, or intelligence, then uses Step 5’s escalation contract.

# 3. Blocking finding

## R8-01 — HIGH — Recipient identity and role-facet reconciliation are not closed

### Finding

The candidate says the receiving:

- person;
- population scope;
- actor;
- office;
- or organization

owns notice, memory, trust, belief, attribution, salience, and response.

Each category can be legitimate.

But one human can simultaneously be:

- an exact canonical Population identity;
- an individualized political actor;
- the holder of a Senate office;
- a party member;
- a platform account holder;
- a subscriber to an outlet;
- a recipient of an official classified briefing;
- a member of a public audience.

The candidate does not specify how those facets reconcile.

### Exploit A — duplicate exposure

```text
same senator receives Story X
→ public platform exposure applies to linked Population person
→ press-monitoring delivery applies to actor
→ office briefing packet applies to officeholder
→ three belief updates occur because three recipient objects exist
```

Three genuinely distinct presentations may legitimately produce repetition.

But one delivery record projected through three representations must not.

The candidate does not require a shared recipient identity or occurrence-consumption rule capable of distinguishing those cases.

### Exploit B — official knowledge leaks into public belief

```text
classified office receipt
→ individualized actor knows claim
→ exact Population-linked person automatically receives public belief update
→ public audience projection now treats classified knowledge as ordinary exposure
```

Step 4 explicitly separates actor-private beliefs from Population-owned public belief. Step 8 needs to preserve that distinction through receipt and memory.

### Exploit C — public exposure becomes institutional knowledge

```text
senator's Population cohort receives local story
→ office is treated as having received it
→ senator acts using institutional knowledge
```

A person may notice a story privately without the office possessing an official record or staff analysis.

The reverse is also true: an office may receive a report without it becoming an ordinary-person media exposure.

### Exploit D — statistically represented actor inherits exact exposure

A named governor with `STATISTICALLY_REPRESENTED_WITHOUT_EXACT_IDENTITY` may belong statistically to a statewide media audience.

The candidate does not explicitly prohibit using the modeled audience exposure as proof that the governor personally saw the story.

Step 4 already prohibits exact personal joins from an aggregate. Step 8 must bind that rule to exposure and cognition.

### Exploit E — exact actor plus aggregate audience double count

A named actor exactly linked to Population may be included inside an aggregate audience exposure and also receive an exact direct delivery.

If both are consumed without exclusion, overlap modeling, or occurrence identity, reach and belief updates can double count.

### Why this is blocking

Step 8 owns the causal bridge into recipient belief.

If recipient identity is ambiguous here, the project can retain one population in name while creating several cognitive copies of the same person.

That fails:

- Step 3 one-population continuity;
- Step 4 individualized-human linkage;
- Step 7 access-bounded evidence;
- Step 8 gate items 10, 11, 13, 20, and 24.

### Required repair — Human recipient and role-context contract

Every load-bearing delivery, exposure, notice, memory, and belief transition concerning an individualized human must identify:

1. the underlying human recipient identity or linkage status;
2. the recipient context:
   - ordinary/private person;
   - office or institutional role;
   - organization role;
   - campaign role;
   - source-protected relationship;
   - platform/account context;
3. the access/classification basis;
4. the delivery or exposure occurrence identity;
5. whether this is:
   - a new presentation;
   - a reference to an already-consumed occurrence;
   - a repeated presentation of the same artifact/proposition;
   - a different artifact sharing proposition/source lineage;
6. which knowledge/memory owner is updated;
7. whether any cross-context transfer occurs and through what valid act;
8. population-weight and aggregate-overlap reconciliation;
9. support status for exact versus modeled exposure.

Hard rules:

- an exact human may have several roles but is not several people;
- one occurrence may not be consumed twice through representation aliases;
- distinct presentations may legitimately create repetition;
- official/role knowledge does not automatically become public-population belief;
- ordinary-person exposure does not automatically become office/institutional knowledge;
- statistically represented actors may not inherit exact exposure, notice, or belief from aggregate audience state;
- aggregate audience exposure and exact named-recipient delivery require declared overlap/exclusion/reconciliation;
- actor-private cognition and Population-owned public belief remain distinct purpose-bounded states, with transfer only through legitimate experience, communication, memory, or role process.

### Falsification test

Create one exactly linked senator who:

- receives a classified agency briefing through office;
- is included in a modeled statewide platform audience;
- personally opens a public article later;
- receives the article again in an official staff packet.

The system must distinguish:

- classified role knowledge;
- uncertain modeled public exposure;
- exact personal article notice;
- later office packet receipt;
- legitimate repetition;
- duplicate bookkeeping.

No hidden information may cross contexts, and no single presentation may be applied twice.

# 4. Bounded clarifications

## C8-02 — Close exposure terminology

The candidate uses:

- availability;
- presentation;
- delivery;
- impression;
- exposure opportunity;
- exposure occurrence;
- notice.

These are directionally separated, but the terms can still drift.

Controlling clarification:

```text
availability
= artifact can be accessed through a channel under stated conditions

presentation/delivery
= channel or actor places/transmits the artifact to a declared recipient surface/scope

exposure opportunity
= recipient could perceive the presented content under the modeled context

notice/attention
= recipient-owned cognitive incorporation
```

`InformationEnvironment` may record availability, presentation/delivery, and exposure opportunity.

It may not own notice.

The informal word `exposure` must declare which meaning applies.

## C8-03 — Proposition identity is not a truth owner

The proposition contract is necessary, but a proposition registry or semantic target may not become a new owner of canonical truth.

Controlling clarification:

> A proposition identity names the content of an assertion or belief target. Underlying truth remains with the relevant semantic owners; evidentiary support remains with Step 7 processes and artifacts; a relation such as `contradicts`, `supports`, or `corrects` is itself a semantic/evidentiary claim with provenance, not an automatic truth verdict.

This prevents a global PropositionGraph from silently becoming an omniscient fact checker.

## C8-04 — Moderation labels and fact-check annotations remain artifacts/actions

A platform label, warning, context panel, fact-check link, or recommendation restriction must remain separated into:

- platform or eligible actor decision;
- annotation/artifact content and evidence;
- distribution/presentation consequence;
- recipient notice and interpretation.

Attaching a label does not:

- change the original proposition’s truth;
- guarantee correction exposure;
- set recipient belief;
- prove the platform’s assessment is correct.

## C8-05 — Disposition is not action

The candidate sometimes groups “willingness to share or organize” with recipient state.

Clarify:

- willingness, intention, preference, and propensity are recipient state;
- sharing, joining, donating, protesting, contacting, or voting are separately owned attempts/processes;
- one may influence the other without becoming the action.

This preserves Step 4’s distinction among belief, objective, intention, decision, action, and result.

# 5. Research check

The research claims used by the candidate are directionally and substantively sound for the limited purpose assigned to them.

## 5.1 Fragmented source use

Pew’s 2025 and 2026 findings support:

- strong variation in source use and trust;
- partisan and typology differences;
- age differences;
- active versus incidental news acquisition;
- news fatigue and source avoidance.

These findings justify heterogeneous media habits and source trust, not exact game coefficients.

## 5.2 Personalized platform presentation

TikTok and YouTube’s official materials support treating recommendation as:

- personalized;
- behavior-dependent;
- distinct from publication and eligibility;
- mediated by platform rules and user controls.

Company descriptions are not treated as proof of downstream political effects.

## 5.3 Concentrated misinformation exposure

The cited Science and Science Advances studies support the design conclusion that false-news exposure/sharing can be highly concentrated and is not automatically population-wide.

They do not establish a timeless distribution for a 2033 scenario.

## 5.4 Repetition and correction

The cited research supports:

- repetition can increase perceived truth on average;
- corrections can reduce misperceptions;
- correction effects may be incomplete, heterogeneous, or short-lived.

The candidate correctly avoids universal coefficients or guaranteed effects.
