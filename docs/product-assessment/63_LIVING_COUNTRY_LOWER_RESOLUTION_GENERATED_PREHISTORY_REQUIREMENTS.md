# 1. Executive design verdict

## 1.1 Central answer

**[DI]** Lower-resolution generated prehistory should be:

> **A horizon-neutral, owner-respecting continuation of the accepted Living Country from January 20, 2025 through actual institutional and material time, using adaptive process resolution, exact occurrence retention where later gameplay needs identity or accountability, compressed bundles and aggregates where it does not, and explicit multi-seed falsification—without one historical narrator, mandatory event quotas, future-aware deepening, post-hoc biography repair, hidden dramatic world selection, or direct assignment of the desired player-start state.**

The broad process is:

```text
accepted Early-2025 Transition Package
+ frozen baseline/backstory/election package
+ declared scenario controls
+ generation identity and causal randomness
→ January 20, 2025 reconciled Living Country
→ owner-specific political, institutional, legal, fiscal,
  material, demographic, informational, and external processes
→ adaptive resolution and retention at each causal boundary
→ periodic and event-driven reconciliation
→ generated elections, transitions, laws, implementation,
  conditions, evidence, beliefs, issues, and memories
→ candidate campaign-entry checkpoint
→ bounded player-victory history or autonomous continuation
→ player-start transition package
→ reconciled inherited presidency
```

No single process may substitute:

```text
generate interesting backstory
→ set opening Congress, economy, issues, and crises
```

## 1.2 Product role

The purpose of generated prehistory is not to create lore.

It must make the player inherit:

- a country with path-dependent laws, programs, fiscal obligations, projects, conditions, and institutions;
- officeholders with generated careers, relationships, commitments, and reputations that affect current action;
- a Congress produced by actual elections and turnover;
- states, organizations, firms, media actors, and courts with independent histories;
- public beliefs and political issues that formed through evidence and action rather than setup labels;
- a campaign and mandate whose promises constrain governing;
- unfinished work and opportunities that were not placed merely to entertain the player.

## 1.3 Resolution is subordinate to causality

Lower resolution means fewer represented intermediate details.

It does not mean weaker ownership, magical transitions, or summary text that writes the result.

The generator may compress:

- routine contacts;
- ordinary agency work;
- most minor news items;
- non-load-bearing legislative activity;
- diffuse business and population transitions;
- ordinary court and administrative processing.

It may not compress away a fact later required to explain:

- who lawfully holds office;
- why a law or obligation exists;
- what an actor promised or knew;
- why a relationship changed;
- which population/place experienced an effect;
- why a court order binds;
- how a fiscal or material stock reconciles;
- why a current political issue or governing opportunity exists.

## 1.4 Core deletion rule

A historical detail should be retained at finer resolution only when deleting or aggregating it would make a supported later fact, action, relationship, obligation, right, measurement, belief, explanation, or presidential decision impossible or materially incoherent.

A detail should not be deepened or retained merely because it is vivid, dramatic, controversial, or narratively satisfying.

# 3. Defined Step 12 terms

## 3.1 Prehistory generation package

A versioned package containing, where applicable:

- accepted baseline and transition package identity;
- generated-actor/backstory package identity;
- scenario-family controls;
- selected Living Country domain/process support;
- actor and institution rules;
- election, calendar, legal, fiscal, material, and information rules;
- shock families;
- resolution/deepening policy;
- retention/compression policy;
- generation identity/seed and causal-randomness policy;
- reconciliation and rejection rules;
- target horizon and player-campaign branch policy.

## 3.2 Candidate world

One attempted world history produced by a declared generation package and controls.

A candidate world may be:

- valid and retained;
- valid but not selected for a particular scenario family;
- invalid under explicit invariants;
- outside supported scope;
- incomplete because generation failed or did not terminate.

## 3.3 Selected world

The one valid candidate history chosen under a declared scenario selection policy for a run.

Rejected or unselected worlds do not become canonical history.

## 3.4 Horizon

A candidate player-start boundary, currently January 2029 or January 2033.

The horizon is not permitted to alter earlier history merely because a later start needs more drama or richer actors.

## 3.5 Autonomous prehistory spine

A horizon-neutral history generated without horizon-specific player campaign intervention through the relevant checkpoint.

It exists for generator verification, prefix comparison, and later branching.

It is not necessarily the final player-start history.

## 3.6 Player-start branch

A branch from an eligible campaign-entry checkpoint that incorporates the bounded player campaign/setup decisions accepted by Step 5 and produces a plausible victorious campaign, separate congressional elections, transition, and inauguration.

## 3.7 Resolution profile

The declared mix of exact occurrences, compressed process bundles, aggregates, state transitions, and omitted/unsupported detail used by one process family over one interval.

There is no universal annual or monthly resolution for the whole country.

## 3.8 Consumer closure set

The declared current and future **supported product-process families** whose semantic needs determine what must be retained.

It may include known needs such as:

- office legitimacy;
- current law and fiscal state;
- actor relationships and memory;
- active programs/cases;
- player-start explanation;
- individual-member Congress gameplay;
- population/geography exposure;
- deterministic continuation.

It may not inspect future player choices, future random outcomes, or later generated drama.

# 4. Generation coordinator versus semantic owners

## 4.1 Coordinator role

A future generation coordinator may:

- load the frozen package;
- establish generation identity and causal random keys;
- request time advancement;
- index owner-declared transitions;
- collect validity/reconciliation results;
- create checkpoints;
- record generation attempts and rejection reasons;
- stop at declared campaign or player-start boundaries;
- select among valid candidates under a declared policy.

## 4.2 Coordinator prohibition

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-PRE01] The prehistory coordinator may not directly choose substantive political, legal, fiscal, material, institutional, informational, or electoral outcomes; insert a needed issue or crisis; alter actor objectives; repair a contradictory world by editing owner state; or assign the desired opening because the target horizon requires it.**

## 4.3 Owner-resolved prehistory

The same owners that matter in active play resolve prehistory:

- elections own votes/results/certification;
- office assignment owns current officeholders;
- Congress and procedures own bills, hearings, votes, schedules, and results;
- legal owners own sources, effective scope, cases, orders, and contests;
- public finance owns receipts, outlays, obligations, debt, and payments;
- agencies/programs own implementation and queues;
- material domains own material changes;
- Population owns people, households, residence, and political cognition;
- actors and organizations own their decisions and relationships;
- media actors own investigation/publication decisions;
- information systems own artifacts and delivery occurrences;
- states and local institutions own their actions;
- external actors own supported external actions.

## 4.4 No annual history roll

The following is not accepted:

```text
Year 2026 outcome roll:
- economy improves
- President passes one law
- opposition gains 18 House seats
- one scandal occurs
```

The generator may use aggregated processes, but each result must still emerge through its proper owners, units, time, and dependencies.

# 5. Horizon neutrality and prefix integrity

## 5.1 Core invariant

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-PRE02] Before a declared horizon-specific player-campaign branch, the same generation package, controls, seed, and canonical decisions must produce the same historical prefix regardless of whether the requested automated horizon is 2029, 2033, or another later supported date.**

The target end date may stop generation.

It may not influence earlier actor decisions, shocks, elections, laws, or condition paths.

## 5.2 Forbidden horizon leakage

Invalid:

```text
2033 target selected
→ generator ensures a major 2027 reform
→ richer inherited issue exists in 2033
```

Invalid:

```text
2029 target selected
→ suppress slow implementation because it would not mature by start
```

Valid:

```text
same 2025 state and generation identity
→ same owner-resolved history through shared prefix
→ 2029 checkpoint
→ optional continued generation to 2033
```

## 5.3 Campaign branching caveat

The 2029 player-start variant and 2033 player-start variant need not share identical history after the 2029 variant's player campaign-entry checkpoint because the player-victory setup itself changes history.

Cross-horizon proof must therefore distinguish:

- automated horizon-neutral spine;
- 2029 player-start campaign branch;
- autonomous continuation beyond 2029;
- 2033 player-start campaign branch.

The exact branch comparison protocol remains a later proof detail.

## 5.4 Configuration versioning

Changing rules, controls, backstory, source package, or supported domains creates a new generation package/version.

A changed package is not expected to preserve a prior package's history.

# 6. Adaptive resolution contract

## 6.1 No single prehistory tick

The world retains dated continuity.

Different process families may resolve through:

- exact scheduled occurrences;
- daily or weekly institutional activity;
- monthly/quarterly/annual flows;
- election/calendar boundaries;
- conditional transitions;
- compressed interval bundles;
- state changes at material milestones;
- adaptive deepening.

## 6.2 Permitted resolution forms

A process may retain:

1. **Exact canonical occurrence**
   - identity, owner, time, participants, authority/route, and consequence;
2. **Compressed owner-resolved process bundle**
   - interval, owners, counts/flows, selected occurrences, output, obligations, uncertainty, and omissions;
3. **Aggregate stock/flow/distribution transition**
   - units, universe, geography, time, transformation, accounting, and support;
4. **Contextual/external initiating state**
   - declared source family, eligibility, timing, scope, and uncertainty;
5. **Deferred/unsupported detail**
   - absent without synthetic zero or invented exact history.

A single process family may use several forms at once.

## 6.3 Mandatory exactness families

Exact occurrence identity is normally required where later supported play consumes a specific:

- office assignment, vacancy, appointment, resignation, removal, death, or succession;
- election result, certification, entitlement, or contested result;
- consequential vote, law, amendment, veto, judicial decision, order, filing, or stay;
- obligation, payment, transfer, reversal, contract, award, or fiscal act;
- promise, bargain, commitment, threat, betrayal, endorsement, or relationship-changing act;
- receipt, disclosure, leak, report, measurement release, or correction that changes what an actor knew;
- organization founding, coalition, strike, protest, lawsuit, or major resource commitment;
- program/project milestone or failure that survives into the player start;
- specific campaign commitment or mandate-forming act.

Not every minor member vote or communication must be retained individually unless a later consumer requires it.

## 6.4 Bundle legitimacy

A compressed bundle must preserve enough to prevent later false claims.

Example:

```text
2027 state-outreach bundle
- 42–55 represented contacts
- participating office families
- states reached
- issue families
- aggregate outcomes
- no retained specific promise
```

The bundle may support a broad conclusion that outreach occurred.

It cannot later support an exact claim that Governor X was promised a waiver unless that promise occurrence was retained.

## 6.5 Aggregate legitimacy

An aggregate transition does not imply exact unit histories.

Example:

```text
quarterly establishment births/deaths and job flows
```

may update supported labor/firm distributions while leaving individual ordinary firm identities unrepresented.

If a later plant closure becomes a named political occurrence, the relevant establishment/firm and affected-population scope must have been retained or legitimately resolved at the time.

# 7. Deepening contract

## 7.1 Allowed deepening triggers

Resolution may deepen when current generated state and the declared consumer closure indicate that finer identity or chronology is required, including:

- a named actor, institution, firm, outlet, organization, project, case, or program becomes causally consequential;
- an election, vote, appointment, case, or negotiation becomes close, contested, or outcome-sensitive to individual decisions;
- a current right, obligation, deadline, order, contract, or legal/fiscal chain requires exact identity;
- cross-domain coupling requires a retained affected scope or contribution lineage;
- evidence-at-time, actor memory, responsibility, or accountability requires a specific occurrence;
- geographic/population distribution changes later political or material consequences;
- the process will remain active at the candidate player start;
- the opening briefing or player decision needs a causal explanation that an aggregate cannot honestly provide;
- a supported institution's own procedure requires finer detail.

## 7.2 Forbidden deepening triggers

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-PRE03] Resolution may not deepen solely because an event would be dramatic, because a writer wants a better story, because a later generated outcome made an earlier detail convenient, because the candidate player start needs a particular relationship, or because the generator is searching for an interesting seed.**

## 7.3 No hindsight fabrication

At the moment detail is compressed, the process must retain whatever latent support is needed by declared consumers.

If a later development makes previously discarded detail relevant, the generator may:

- use retained latent allocation/lineage;
- create a prospective exact identity from current state without inventing past exactness;
- produce a modeled or bounded historical reconstruction;
- report the earlier detail as unsupported or unknown.

It may not generate a newly exact prior meeting, vote, promise, motive, receipt, or relationship after seeing the later consequence.

## 7.4 Consumer closure is not future knowledge

The generator may know that the product supports:

- individual Representatives;
- actor relationships;
- programs and court disputes;
- opening historical explanation.

It may not know which particular Representative the future player will contact, which shock will occur, which scandal will become salient, or which option the player will choose.

## 7.5 Deepening symmetry

Equivalent current states with the same declared support and different hidden future random outcomes must make the same deepening/retention decision before those outcomes occur.

# 8. Compression and retention during generation

## 8.1 Retention classes

A later implementation may distinguish:

- permanent exact occurrences;
- active-process history;
- actor/organization memory targets;
- legal/fiscal/accounting ledgers;
- evidence and access history;
- compressed bundles;
- aggregates and checkpoints;
- derived summaries;
- discardable transient work.

Step 12 does not choose the storage architecture.

## 8.2 Retention consumers

Retain sufficient history for:

- current-state reconciliation;
- office/election legitimacy;
- rights, obligations, deadlines, contracts, and cases;
- actor and organization relationships/memory;
- evidence available at historical decisions;
- policy feedback and constituencies;
- party/faction reputation and issue association;
- campaign commitments and vulnerabilities;
- current political agendas/workstreams;
- player-start briefing and explanation;
- deterministic continuation;
- audit/falsification.

## 8.3 Compression may not erase negative history

A failed bill, broken promise, abandoned program, quiet harm, missed deadline, or failed investigation may need retention when it affects current actors, trust, law, money, conditions, evidence, or politics.

Compression cannot retain only successes.

## 8.4 No age-only deletion

A 2025 event may remain highly relevant in 2033.

A 2032 routine communication may be safely discarded.

Retention depends on consumers and state, not age alone.

# 10. Quiet, mediocre, frustrating, and volatile histories

## 10.1 No drama quota

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-PRE04] No term, year, quarter, or player-start package is required to contain a crisis, scandal, major reform, party realignment, court confrontation, economic collapse, or other dramatic event merely to satisfy an activity quota.**

## 10.2 Quiet history

A valid quiet year may contain:

- routine budgets and administration;
- modest condition changes;
- legislation that fails or remains narrow;
- ordinary appointments and turnover;
- local political activity;
- implementation continuing;
- measurements and media activity;
- no major national crisis.

Quiet does not mean frozen.

## 10.3 Mediocre history

A valid mediocre administration may:

- pass some narrow laws;
- fail at major priorities;
- administer competently in some areas and poorly in others;
- produce ambiguous material outcomes;
- avoid spectacular crisis or transformation;
- leave unfinished work and mixed political inheritance.

## 10.4 Frustrating history

A valid frustrating history may include:

- divided government;
- blocked appointments;
- state resistance;
- implementation delays;
- weak evidence;
- missed opportunities;
- policy compromise;
- diffuse accountability;
- political noise without resolution.

## 10.5 Volatile history

A crisis-heavy or transformative history is valid only when supported shocks, conditions, actors, institutions, and information processes produce it.

The generator may not increase crisis frequency because a particular seed looks dull.

## 10.6 Uneventful does not mean unplayable

A quiet start can still present strategic governing through:

- budget choices;
- appointments;
- existing programs;
- congressional windows;
- state relationships;
- long-running conditions;
- campaign promises;
- ordinary administration priorities.

The player is not owed an emergency on inauguration day.

# 11. Shock admissibility

## 11.1 Shock declaration

A supported shock family declares:

- source/actor/environment owner;
- eligibility conditions;
- timing process;
- geography/population/entity scope;
- severity/distribution support;
- correlation/dependence with other shocks;
- observability and evidence;
- downstream typed inputs;
- whether recurrence is possible;
- limitations and unsupported outcomes.

## 11.2 No pre-resolved politics

Invalid:

```text
hurricane occurs
→ President loses approval
```

Valid:

```text
storm/hazard occurrence
→ damage/exposure
→ state/federal response
→ implementation and information
→ recipient experience and attribution
→ later politics
```

## 11.3 No compensating shock

A quiet world may not receive a late scandal, war, hurricane, cyberattack, or market crash merely because the generated opening lacks excitement.

## 11.4 Unsupported escalation

If a generated shock would require systems outside the declared product scope, the generator should normally prevent that shock family or bound its outcomes before generation.

A post-hoc rejection policy may classify a world as outside supported scope only under a predeclared rule and must preserve the attempted generation record.

## 14.5 Valid candidate

A valid world satisfies all accepted semantic, support, history, and reconciliation gates.

Validity does not imply drama, balance, fun, or product selection.

# 16. World selection and scenario controls

## 16.1 Predeclared controls

Scenario controls may condition:

- incoming 2025 political family;

- player-start horizon;

## 16.2 Generated outcomes

Uncontrolled results remain autonomous within the model.

## 16.3 Validity rejection

Invalid or unsupported worlds may be rejected under predeclared rules.

Every rejection should retain:

- attempt identity;
- control family;
- seed;
- failure checkpoint;
- violated invariant or unsupported state;

- a valid player office and transition;
- an understandable inherited country;
- sufficient observable state for the Presidency to operate;
- at least one credible governing route or workstream opportunity;
- no unreconciled contradiction;
- enough history to explain current actors and institutions.

A world that fails those structural conditions may be excluded from a particular product scenario family.

## 16.5 No hidden interestingness score

The product may not select a world through an undisclosed score for:

- drama;
- number of crises;
- conflict density;
- scandal;
- policy novelty;
- emotional arc;
- presumed player excitement.

## 16.6 Quiet valid worlds

Quiet, politically frustrating, incremental, divided, or mediocre worlds remain valid and must appear in the validation ensemble.

A released scenario may use disclosed scenario families, but may not silently reroll until an exciting world appears.

# 17. Four-year versus eight-year generated history

## 17.1 Four-year burden

A 2029 candidate tests whether the generator can produce one coherent full administration and transfer.

It exercises:

- presidential and congressional cycles;
- one midterm;
- one player-victory campaign/election;
- lawmaking and failure;
- appointments and turnover;
- budgets and fiscal flows;
- programs and implementation;
- material/social change;
- evidence/media/public politics;
- issue emergence and policy feedback;
- courts/states/organizations;
- historical inheritance.

It avoids the 2030 census/redistricting burden.

## 17.2 Eight-year burden

A 2033 candidate tests all four-year requirements plus:

- second-term/second-administration compounding;
- another midterm and presidential election;
- longer careers and relationship histories;
- more policy feedback and institutional memory;
- long-latency implementation/material effects;
- more appointments and judicial change;
- more opportunity for party/faction/organization evolution;
- 2030 census/apportionment/redistricting;
- 2032 district and congressional elections;
- greater history-compression and reconciliation stress.

## 17.3 More history is not automatically more game

The extra four years justify their burden only when they produce player-relevant differences that cannot be obtained credibly from the shorter horizon.

Potential added value includes:

- mature policy feedback;
- changed congressional geography;
- longer actor careers and relationships;
- inherited second-order fiscal obligations;
- programs that have had time to succeed/fail materially;
- court composition and precedent changes;
- party/faction reputations shaped by more than one term;
- more varied current laws and state relationships.

## 17.4 Synthetic sludge failure

The 2033 horizon fails its product case when the extra period mainly produces:

- more generic biographies;
- repeated minor laws;
- opaque aggregate drift;
- fragile accounting;
- redundant media/issue records;
- arbitrary crises;
- contradictory district histories;
- little change to actual opening choices.

## 17.5 Current candidate position

**[DI]** January 2029 should serve as the mandatory first generator-credibility horizon.

January 2033 should remain a conditional extension horizon requiring separate proof of:

- coherent prefix continuation;
- census/redistricting;
- acceptable failure/rejection rates;
- richer inherited decisions and constraints;
- manageable history and explanation.

This is a proof-order decision, not acceptance of January 2029 as the commercial player start.

# 19. Multi-seed proof requirements

Step 12 does not claim these proofs have been run.

A later formal or implemented generator proof must include at least the following.

## 19.1 Machine invariant ensemble

Minimum screening floor:

- at least **300 distinct generation identities** for the four-year automated horizon;
- all attempts, including invalid and out-of-scope candidates, retained in the denominator;
- declared controls and seed policy;
- per-checkpoint reconciliation;
- complete failure taxonomy;
- no hidden retries.

The floor is a defect-screening threshold, not proof of real-world statistical validity.

Under an independence approximation, 300 attempts give greater than 95% probability of observing at least one defect whose true incidence is 1%; correlated failure modes and control families may require more.

## 19.2 Eight-year extension ensemble

The 2033 extension proof should:

- continue at least the same 300 automated spines beyond the 2029 checkpoint where supported;
- report prefix-integrity results;
- include all extension failures and out-of-scope states;
- audit census/apportionment/redistricting and 2032 elections;
- separately generate the 2032 player-victory branches;
- not discard valid quiet continuations.

## 19.3 Scenario-control coverage

The ensemble must cover every supported control family.

No family may be represented only by one showcase seed.

Controls may include, when later accepted:

- unified/divided government;
- party continuity/transfer;
- narrow/broad electoral margin;
- low/high external volatility;
- varying inherited conditions;
- different state/organization structures.

## 19.4 Human deep audit

At least **24 predeclared or reproducibly selected worlds per horizon** should receive contiguous historical review, including:

- quiet/low-shock;
- incremental/mediocre;
- divided/frustrating;
- volatile/crisis-heavy;
- party continuity;
- party transfer;
- different congressional-control configurations.

The full ensemble statistics remain visible so deep-audit selection cannot hide failures.

## 19.5 Same-seed perturbation

A later proof should test bounded changes such as:

- remove or disable one shock family;
- alter one election/control range;
- change one source uncertainty;
- remove one organization bridge;
- change one state response;
- delay one measurement;
- change horizon without changing the prefix.

The resulting differences must trace to changed causal inputs rather than global reseeding or story replacement.

## 19.6 Metamorphic tests

Required tests should include:

- time-chunk invariance;
- save/load continuation;
- handler/iteration-order invariance;
- horizon-prefix invariance;
- rejected-world isolation;
- resolution-refinement conservation;
- no future-aware deepening;
- no duplicate occurrence/contribution/evidence/pressure counting;
- no retroactive knowledge;
- no actor/person duplication.

## 19.7 Blind opening review

Human reviewers should assess generated player-start packages without seeing seed quality labels or whether a world was expected to be exciting.

They should evaluate:

- internal coherence;
- political plausibility within the generated world;
- distinguishable history;
- actionable inherited state;
- bounded information;
- explanation quality;
- repetitive/synthetic content;
- whether history alters governing choices.

This is product evidence, not causal authority.

# 20. World rejection and failure-rate reporting

## 20.1 No invisible rerolling

A generation attempt that fails cannot vanish from proof statistics because the system retried automatically.

## 20.2 Failure categories

Report separately:

- semantic invariant failure;
- accounting/reconciliation failure;
- unsupported-scope transition;
- nontermination/performance failure;
- campaign-feasibility failure;
- player-start packaging failure;
- human plausibility/content failure;
- valid but quiet/mediocre history;
- valid and selected history.

## 20.3 Invalid-rate interpretation

A high invalid or unsupported rate is evidence against generator maturity or product scope.

It cannot be cured by selecting only the successful 5% unless the conditioning and rejection process is itself the declared product.

## 20.4 Product selection versus proof selection

Development may inspect edge cases and choose seeds for debugging.

A proof must report the full attempted population.

A released run may sample from a declared valid scenario family.

It may not pretend that the selected world was an unconditional generator output if extensive conditioning or rejection occurred.
