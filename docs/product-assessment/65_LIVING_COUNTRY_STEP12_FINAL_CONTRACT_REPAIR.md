# 4. Quiet-world validity and player-start operability

## 4.1 Closed distinction

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-PRER02] Semantic validity, supported-scope validity, player-start operability, scenario-family eligibility, and subjective product interest are separate judgments.**

## 4.2 Semantic validity

The world obeys accepted ownership, authority, causality, accounting, history, identity, information, and deterministic rules.

## 4.3 Supported-scope validity

The world remains inside declared process families the product can represent honestly.

A state outside supported scope is logged as such.

## 4.4 Player-start operability

Operability requires:

- a lawful and active presidential office/control binding;
- a functioning administration and access route;
- deterministic time advancement;
- ordinary constitutional, legislative, fiscal, legal, administrative, material, and informational processes;
- a valid briefing/current-state projection;
- the ability for future presidential situations to emerge through accepted routes.

Operability does **not** require:

- a decision on inauguration day;
- an active crisis;
- a scandal;
- a major bill;
- an existing White House workstream;
- a dramatic opponent;
- a high-pressure issue;
- a minimum conflict score.

A valid quiet presidency may begin with briefing and advance until a real situation requires attention.

## 4.5 Scenario-family eligibility

A product may declare before generation that a scenario family requires a bounded political configuration such as divided government, party transfer, or a margin range.

That is disclosed conditioning.

It cannot be an after-the-fact excuse to reject quiet or politically inconvenient outputs.

## 4.6 Subjective product-interest assessment

Human review may classify a valid world as bland, repetitive, confusing, or commercially weak.

That is important product evidence.

It does not make the world semantically invalid and may not disappear from generator statistics.

A product may later decide not to ship a generator mode because its valid output distribution is poor.

It may not claim the underlying generator produces better worlds by silently filtering them.

## 4.7 No opening-workstream quota

The candidate Section 16.4 phrase `at least one credible governing route or workstream opportunity` is narrowed.

A valid world must contain functioning ordinary governing routes.

It need not contain a preexisting active workstream or immediate opportunity.

# 5. Autonomous spine and campaign-fork protocol

## 5.1 Core invariant

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-PRER03] Automated horizon comparison and horizon-specific player-victory histories must use explicitly separate branches with shared prefix only until the first declared differing intervention.**

## 5.2 Autonomous validation spine

For generator verification, one autonomous spine may continue through:

```text
2024 transition-package generation
→ January 20, 2025 handoff
→ autonomous 2025–2028 history
→ autonomous 2028 campaign/elections
→ January 20, 2029 checkpoint
→ autonomous 2029–2032 history
→ autonomous 2032 campaign/elections
→ January 20, 2033 checkpoint
```

The spine need not become the player's world.

It exists to test continuation, prefix integrity, long-horizon causality, census/redistricting, and incremental historical effects.

## 5.3 2029 player-start branch

A 2029 candidate branches at the declared 2028 campaign-entry checkpoint:

```text
shared history through campaign checkpoint
→ player-shaped viable campaign history
→ 2028 presidential result
→ separate House/Senate results
→ January 3/6/20 transition
→ 2029 player start
```

This branch receives its own identity and is not written back into the autonomous spine.

## 5.4 2033 player-start branch

A 2033 candidate uses the autonomous 2028 continuation and branches at the declared 2032 campaign-entry checkpoint:

```text
autonomous history through 2028 election and 2029 transfer
→ autonomous 2029–2032 history
→ player-shaped viable 2032 campaign history
→ separate 2032 congressional results
→ January 2033 transition
→ 2033 player start
```

## 5.5 Prefix rule

Histories remain identical only until:

- a declared scenario control differs;
- a declared player intervention differs;
- package/version differs;
- a causally relevant input differs.

The generator may not globally reseed unrelated prior history when one later branch input changes.

## 5.6 2033 marginal comparison

2033's additional value is assessed against the autonomous spine's own January 2029 checkpoint and the history that continues from it.

It is not assessed by comparing the 2033 world to a different 2029 player-victory branch.

The comparison asks:

- what changed from 2029 to 2033;
- which opening facts depend on that period;
- whether those facts alter player options, constraints, actors, obligations, information, or opportunities;
- whether the added burden produces richer governing rather than merely more records.

## 5.7 Campaign finalization does not validate the spine

A successful 2029 or 2033 player campaign branch cannot hide defects in the autonomous history leading to its checkpoint.

Both components must pass.

## 6.3 Extension disposition

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-PRER04] Every attempted/valid four-year autonomous spine must receive an explicit 2033-extension disposition.**

Possible dispositions include:

- eligible and extended;
- eligible but extension not run, with declared resource/experiment reason;
- horizon-ineligible under a predeclared support rule;
- invalid before extension;
- extension attempted and failed;
- extension completed validly;
- extension completed outside supported scope.

`Where supported` is not sufficient without a reason code.

## 6.4 No quiet-world dropping

A world may not be removed from the extension cohort because it is:

- quiet;
- politically awkward;
- mediocre;
- expected to create few 2033 differences;
- computationally expensive within the accepted support envelope;
- likely to expose generator weakness.

## 6.5 Replacement attempts

A replacement seed is an additional attempt.

It never replaces the failed attempt in the denominator.

## 6.6 Reported counts

A later proof reports at minimum:

- initiated Stage A attempts;
- valid transition packages;
- valid January 20 handoffs;
- valid 2029 spines;
- invalid/unsupported/nonterminating counts by checkpoint;
- extension-eligible counts;
- extensions attempted/completed/failed;
- 2029 and 2033 campaign branches attempted/valid/failed;
- valid but quiet/mediocre classifications;
- worlds selected for human review and why.

## 6.7 Screening floor qualification

The 300-attempt floor remains a bug-finding minimum, not statistical proof of political realism or a guarantee of rare-event coverage.

Control-family dependence, common-mode bugs, and correlated failures may require substantially more attempts.

# 7. Frozen consumer closure and endpoint packaging

## 7.1 Core invariant

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-PRER05] The supported consumer closure, resolution policy, deepening policy, and retention policy are versioned and frozen before a generation attempt begins.**

They may know which product-process families are supported.

They may not know future generated values, player choices, random outcomes, or which history will later appear interesting.

## 7.2 Prospective reservation

The policy may reserve support for facts later needed by known process families, such as:

- individual congressional members;
- office legitimacy;
- active laws/programs/cases;
- actor memory and commitments;
- fiscal contribution lineage;
- historical evidence-at-time;
- opening causal explanation;
- census/district/electorate continuity.

Reservation identifies fact families and support obligations, not future event identities.

## 7.3 Current-state trigger

During generation, current owner state may trigger deeper resolution prospectively when accepted criteria become true.

Example:

```text
committee vote becomes close under current whip evidence
→ member-level decisions deepen now
```

This does not require knowing whether the vote later becomes a famous controversy.

## 7.4 Endpoint assembly prohibition

The January 2029/2033 packaging process may:

- query current state;
- select accessible records;
- create projections;
- summarize retained history;
- produce bounded reconstruction;
- expose unsupported/unknown detail.

It may not:

- generate a newly exact past vote, meeting, promise, receipt, relationship, motive, or event;
- reclassify an aggregate as exact because the briefing needs detail;
- rerun only an earlier interval with knowledge of later outcomes and splice the result into history;
- add a backdated actor memory;
- deepen for narrative coherence after the fact.

## 7.5 Unmet explanation need

When final packaging reveals that retained history cannot support a desired exact explanation, the product must:

- present a bounded/uncertain explanation;
- omit the unsupported claim;
- classify the generator package as insufficient for that supported consumer;
- or create a new generator version and regenerate the entire relevant lineage.

It cannot patch the selected world.

## 7.6 Equivalent-future test

Two identical current states and policies with different hidden future random outcomes make the same retention/deepening decision before those outcomes occur.

## 8.3 Four-year historical audit

The audit reports:

- prefix integrity;
- second-term/administration validity;
- 2030 census/redistricting;
- 2032 elections;

- incremental player-relevant inheritance;
- extension failures and unsupported states.

## 8.5 Campaign branches

The proof separately samples:

- 2028 player-campaign branches from eligible 2029 checkpoints;
- 2032 player-campaign branches from eligible 2033 checkpoints.

Campaign branch success cannot erase a failed autonomous spine or transition package.

## 8.6 Selection disclosure

Human showcase examples may be presented only alongside:

- their selection method;
- full ensemble failure/validity distribution;
- quiet and mediocre cases;
- rejected cases;
- known conditioning.
