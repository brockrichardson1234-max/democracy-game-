# 2. Corrected capacity invariant

## 2.1 Universal-resource prohibition

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT]** No universal or cross-domain aggregate `attention`, `presidential capacity`, `overextension`, `agenda slot`, or `political capital` value may causally resolve unrelated institutional outcomes.

The game may not determine that a healthcare bill stalls, an agency memo arrives late, and a governor refuses cooperation because one hidden cross-domain score fell from 71 to 53.

## 2.2 Owner-scoped quantities remain legitimate

The prohibition does **not** ban causally grounded quantities within the correct owner.

Legitimate examples include:

- a congressional committee calendar with finite hearing dates;
- a chamber calendar containing competing floor opportunities;
- a White House office with named or role-bounded assignments and deadlines;
- an agency review unit with owner-scoped throughput and a queue;
- a presidential calendar containing incompatible meetings or travel;
- enacted budget authority measured in dollars;
- a statutory filing period measured in days;
- a program with finite personnel, technical-assistance resources, or legally available funds.

Owner-scoped quantities are valid only when:

1. the owner is identifiable;
2. the quantity represents something in that owner's world rather than a disguised global action currency;
3. competing uses are identifiable;
4. consequences are traceable to queueing, scheduling, assignment, money, authority, or another concrete fact;
5. the player can receive a bounded explanation of the collision when the administration could reasonably know it.

## 2.3 Falsification rule

A contention result fails this contract when its complete explanation is equivalent to:

> “The administration was doing too much, so the game applied a general penalty.”

A valid explanation instead has the form:

> “The same Senate floor window was requested for two measures; leadership scheduled its own appropriations bill first; the administration's offer expired; the healthcare measure remained in committee.”

# 3. Autonomous Actor Initiative Contract

## 3.1 Core grammar

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT]** Any non-player initiative used as evidence that the political country is autonomous must satisfy:

```text
actor/institution receives or observes bounded state
→ actor interprets through role, jurisdiction, objectives,
  constituency, beliefs, relationships, commitments,
  political risk, and current institutional opportunities
→ actor derives actions genuinely available to that actor
→ actor chooses attempt, delay, delegation, or no action
→ choice/attempt becomes a canonical record
→ target or institutional procedure resolves independently
→ the administration learns only through a valid information route
```

The actor is not required to reason perfectly. The actor is required to act from bounded state rather than from a hidden dramatic need.

## 3.2 Initiative provenance fields

Every demonstrated major non-player initiative must identify:

| Field | Required meaning |
|---|---|
| Initiating actor/institution | The canonical owner making the choice |
| Observed/received basis | The condition, communication, measurement, commitment, opportunity, or conflict available to that owner |
| Role and jurisdiction | Why the actor has standing or an institutional route |
| Objectives and constraints | What the actor is trying to accomplish and what limits it |
| Available attempts | Actions actually available to that actor, including delay/no action |
| Timing basis | Why action is available or attractive now |
| Choice record | The canonical attempted action, not a resolved outcome |
| Independent admission/resolution | Committee, chamber, state, organization, court, agency, electorate, or target that resolves the next step |
| Administration discovery | The artifact/channel through which the White House can learn |

If those fields cannot be supplied, the initiative remains a design hypothesis and cannot count as gate evidence.

## 3.3 Actor-family applications

### Lawmaker, committee, and congressional leadership

A congressional initiative can arise from:

- constituency exposure;
- committee jurisdiction;
- party/faction priority;
- reelection incentives;
- member policy objectives;
- a prior commitment;
- an available committee or floor vehicle;
- another bill's failure or expiration;
- observed national/state conditions;
- oversight information.

The actor may introduce a proposal, request a hearing, negotiate, delay, oppose, investigate, seek an amendment, or take no action. Introduction does not guarantee committee admission, leadership scheduling, votes, or enactment.

### Governor or state coalition

A governor/state initiative can arise from:

- state material conditions;
- program participation consequences;
- legal authority or claimed authority;
- fiscal exposure;
- shared interests among states;
- gubernatorial electoral incentives;
- conflict with federal implementation;
- opportunity for a compact, waiver request, lawsuit, or state experiment.

Formation of a coalition must be recorded through participating state decisions. A label such as `Governor Coalition` may summarize the relationship; it may not create agreement among states.

### Party faction or political organization

A faction/organization initiative can arise from:

- policy commitments;
- repeated member alignment;
- campaign promises;
- perceived betrayal or success;
- leadership contests;
- organization membership and resources;
- public/member salience;
- an upcoming primary/election or legislative opportunity.

It may mobilize, endorse, oppose, threaten a primary, demand a commitment, change leadership posture, or stay inactive. Members remain autonomous.

### Agency or institution

An agency/institution may initiate action because of:

- statutory duties;
- delegated authority;
- observed implementation conditions;
- enforcement findings;
- reporting requirements;
- unresolved cross-agency conflict;
- inspector-general or audit findings;
- court orders;
- standing presidential direction.

The agency cannot invent new legal authority because a workstream needs a decision.

### Opposition political actors

Opposition actors may initiate legislation, oversight, messaging, coalition-building, or litigation based on their own objectives, information, electoral incentives, and institutional opportunities. Their activity does not require a prior presidential action.

## 3.4 No global initiative quota or drama selector

No global selector may decide that “the term needs a congressional conflict now” and then choose the highest-scoring eligible initiative.

Multiple actors may independently act on the same date. Many may choose no action. Initiative frequency emerges from actor state and opportunities; it is not filled to a dramatic quota.

## 3.6 Initiative falsification tests

A non-player initiative fails when:

- its only timing reason is “to create pressure”;
- the initiating actor lacks authority, role, or access to the underlying information;
- the action is unavailable under the institution's current state;
- a coalition appears without individual/organizational participation decisions;
- introduction is treated as passage;
- the administration learns before a valid communication or public record exists;
- every actor takes action whenever eligible, eliminating delay and inaction as real outcomes.

# 4. Discretionary Staff Escalation Contract

## 4.1 Staff judgment cannot hide a dramatic director

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT]** Naming an adviser is insufficient provenance. A discretionary staff escalation must be grounded in the adviser's bounded role, received information, delegated instructions or presidential priorities, forecasted consequence, and recorded assessment.

## 4.2 Staff escalation grammar

```text
administration actor receives an artifact/communication
→ role and jurisdiction are checked
→ actor evaluates it against:
    delegated objective or standing instructions
    known presidential priorities
    explicit deadlines/windows
    applicable authority requirements
    forecasted consequences
    confidence and uncertainty
    current staff/agency assignments
→ actor records assessment
→ actor escalates, routes elsewhere, batches, monitors, or does not escalate
```

## 4.3 Required staff-assessment fields

A demonstrated discretionary escalation must identify:

- adviser/office;
- received artifact;
- role/jurisdiction;
- applicable delegated objective, standing instruction, presidential priority, or authority need;
- assessment and confidence;
- known uncertainty or disagreement;
- forecasted consequence of action/inaction;
- escalation or non-escalation record;
- intended recipient.

## 4.4 Fallibility and disagreement

Staff may:

- escalate something that later proves minor;
- fail to escalate because available evidence is weak;
- disagree about legal, political, fiscal, material, or security implications;
- revise an assessment after new information;
- route a matter to Cabinet or agency resolution rather than the President.

The player sees the received assessment, not the hidden canonical truth.

## 4.5 Non-escalation must also be causal

A matter may remain background because:

- it is within delegated tolerance;
- no presidential authority is required;
- no known deadline is near;
- evidence confidence is too low under standing instructions;
- a responsible subordinate owner can resolve it;
- the player requested only periodic summaries.

“Not dramatic enough” is not a valid non-escalation reason.

## 4.6 Staff-escalation falsification tests

A staff escalation fails when:

- the adviser has no role connection to the matter;
- the adviser receives information unavailable to the administration;
- the assessment contains exact hidden motives or future outcomes;
- the only justification is an undefined importance score;
- the adviser always escalates eligible matters and never exercises bounded non-escalation;
- another staff office's disagreement is erased rather than represented where relevant.

# 5. Dynamic Campaign Feasibility Contract

## 5.1 Standard setup meaning

The standard setup constructs **which plausible victorious campaign history the player inhabited**. It is not an unrestricted campaign whose defeat is secretly overridden.

The world, opponent, electorate, party context, and viable nominee histories exist before mandate-forming choices are finalized.

## 5.2 Surviving-victory invariant

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT]** After every finalized setup choice, at least one plausible victorious general-election history must remain inside the previously established world/candidate feasibility space.

This applies after:

- nominee-history selection;
- running-mate selection;
- each campaign commitment;
- each coalition/message emphasis decision;
- each consequential campaign response included in setup.

## 5.3 Choice evaluation

A proposed setup choice filters the remaining plausible victorious histories for compatibility with:

- candidate and running-mate histories;
- party/faction relationships;
- electorate and geographic state;
- opponent history and strategy;
- commitments and ideological/fiscal compatibility;
- plausible turnout and persuasion ranges;
- campaign developments already realized;
- separate House and Senate election processes.

The option does not directly modify an election result. It changes which complete plausible histories remain possible.

## 5.4 When a choice would eliminate the final winning path

For the standard governing start, the player may preview the choice but may not finalize a combination that leaves no plausible victorious history.

The game must:

1. explain the load-bearing contradiction in political terms;
2. identify which current selections jointly eliminate the path;
3. allow the player to revise the current or an earlier setup choice;
4. preserve the opponent, electorate, and prehistory rather than inventing a late collapse or miraculous turnout.

Example explanation:

> Staff finds no plausible victorious history combining this running mate, the selected immigration commitment, and the current Great Lakes strategy. Revise at least one of those choices to continue with the victory-conditioned governing start.

The explanation is a bounded campaign assessment and may contain uncertainty. It does not expose debug probability.

## 5.5 Options are not silently hidden merely for being difficult

A difficult option may remain available when at least one plausible winning history survives, even if it produces:

- a narrower margin;
- a more contradictory coalition;
- reduced co-partisan enthusiasm;
- greater dependence on a region or faction;
- more damaging governing commitments;
- a worse relationship with party leaders or organizations.

Only a combination that eliminates the final plausible victory path is blocked from finalization in the standard mode.

## 5.6 Optional loss-capable campaign remains outside authority

A future full campaign mode may allow the player to leave the victorious feasibility space and risk defeat. This is not accepted by this repair and is not required for the governing-game start.

## 5.7 Congress remains separately owned

The surviving presidential-victory histories constrain shared electoral causes but do not set House or Senate outcomes.

House and Senate elections independently resolve from their candidates, constituencies, turnout, party environment, and other causal state. A presidential choice may affect shared enthusiasm or turnout conditions; it cannot select a convenient Congress.

## 5.8 Dynamic-feasibility falsification tests

The setup fails when:

- a finalized choice leaves no plausible victory but inauguration still occurs;
- the opponent weakens only to rescue the chosen combination;
- turnout exceeds the previously bounded electorate without cause;
- an incompatible running mate or promise is retroactively reconciled by rewriting candidate history;
- congressional outcomes are altered to compensate for a weaker presidential path;
- all surviving histories converge to the same coalition and mandate despite materially different choices.
