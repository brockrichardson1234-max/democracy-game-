# 2. Blocking finding R7-01 is closed

## 2.1 Provenance and dependence are separate

The repaired contract now distinguishes:

```text
ordinary provenance:
where did this product come from?

versus

dependency lineage:
which uncertainty-producing samples, records, controls,
models, panels, sources, or prior products does it share
with another product?
```

This prevents a consumer from interpreting different artifact IDs as evidence of statistical/analytic independence.

## 2.2 Combination support is explicit

Potentially combined evidence can now be classified as:

- independence supported;
- dependence modeled;
- dependence known qualitatively;
- alternate representation/revision;
- derived from another product;
- dependence unknown.

The precise implementation representation remains unresolved.

What is now prohibited is the semantic shortcut:

```text
different artifact IDs
→ independent error
```

## 2.3 Unsupported independence produces bounded or unresolved inference

A consumer may use:

- explicit covariance/dependence;
- conservative dependence assumptions;
- bounds;
- hierarchical/source-family methods;
- preferred nonduplicative evidence;
- unresolved/no combination.

It may not invent precision merely because a downstream decision model expects one number.

## 2.4 Shared subject and shared error remain distinct

Two independent surveys can measure the same phenomenon with substantially independent sampling error.

Two estimates of different phenomena can share one modeled population allocation and therefore share error dependence.

The repaired contract does not infer dependence from subject identity alone.

# 3. Hostile test — same-sample estimates

Two estimates use one survey sample.

Attempted exploit:

```text
Estimate A
Estimate B
→ staff assumes independent errors
→ combined uncertainty shrinks
```

Result under repaired contract:

- sample-family lineage exposes potential dependence;
- consumer must model it, bound it, use a conservative assumption, or refuse unsupported precision;
- independent-error math requires methodological support.

**PASS.**

# 4. Hostile test — polling panel reuse

Two public polls have different sponsors and artifact IDs but share a substantial panel/vendor frame.

Attempted exploit:

```text
Poll A + Poll B
→ treated as two independent samples
→ falsely narrow aggregate uncertainty
```

Result:

- polling products may retain shared panel/recruitment/control lineage;
- dependence may be modeled, conservatively handled, or left unresolved;
- public release count is not sample-independence count.

**PASS.**

# 5. Hostile test — intelligence source echo

Attempted exploit:

→ confidence rises automatically

Result:

- source-report lineage can preserve known or suspected common origin;
- source independence may remain uncertain;
- analyst confidence cannot be derived from report count alone;
- likelihood and analytic confidence remain separate.

**PASS.**

# 8. Release-state clarifications pass

The repair distinguishes normal revision, correction, withdrawal/retraction, and methodological replacement.

Historical receipt survives every status transition.

Thus:

```text
publication contained error
→ President received it
→ later correction
```

can remain true without the corrected artifact being presented as what the President originally saw.

**PASS.**

# 9. Delayed-release clarification passes

An expected statistical release may be delayed by source-data, operational, quality-control, legal, confidentiality, shutdown, or emergency conditions.

The delay is process state.

It does not mean:

- measured condition equals zero;
- missing artifact equals no underlying condition;
- White House automatically sees the unreleased estimate.

**PASS.**

# 10. Source evaluation versus trust passes

Evidence processes may preserve objective/procedural facts and institutional source assessments.

Recipient trust remains actor-owned.

Thus one official release or intelligence source may be trusted differently by:

- White House staff;
- congressional actors;
- media organizations;
- private analysts;
- members of the public;

without rewriting the artifact.

**PASS.**
