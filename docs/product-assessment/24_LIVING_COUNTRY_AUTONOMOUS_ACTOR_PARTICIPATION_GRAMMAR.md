# 1. Executive design verdict

## 1.1 Central answer

**[DI]** Living Country should use a common autonomous-participant grammar best summarized as:

> **A shared decision-and-action handshake, not a shared mind, body, authority model, objective function, or power meter.**

Every retained actor, office, institution, or organization must be able to establish, where causally applicable:

1. what identity exists and which owner holds each fact;
2. whether the decision locus is an individual, officeholder, collective procedure, organization, institution, delegated unit, or rule-bound process;
3. what role, jurisdiction, constituency, mandate, membership, or relationship gives the participant standing to attempt something;
4. what legal authority is claimed or applicable, separately from attemptability, capability, compliance, and success;
5. what bounded information, records, communications, experience, and beliefs are available;
6. what purpose-bounded objectives, obligations, priorities, risks, and horizons shape the choice;
7. what owner-specific resources, queues, calendars, staff, money, access, or capabilities constrain action;
8. which attempts are institutionally available now and why;
9. how the participant chooses action, delay, delegation, refusal, or no action;
10. what canonical attempt or communication enters the world;
11. which target, procedure, institution, domain, or recipient independently resolves the next consequence;
12. what occurrence, current-state change, relationship consequence, information artifact, or later opportunity results;
13. how identity, memory, commitments, knowledge, and obligations persist through time, turnover, and succession.

These are semantic obligations. They are not required fields in one `Actor<T>` object.

## 1.2 What the grammar must prevent

The design must prevent:

- every organization behaving through one generic utility score;
- a party, union, firm, agency, court, or media outlet acting as a hive mind without an identified decision locus;
- a universal `influence`, `politicalCapital`, `power`, `salience`, or `pressure` scalar resolving unrelated actors;
- canonical truth directly triggering actors who lack a valid observation or receipt;
- actors acting because a hidden director needs drama, opposition, or a presidential interruption;
- an actor attempt directly setting another owner’s decision, material outcome, public belief, or election result;
- the player silently owning Cabinet officials, agencies, legislators, states, courts, organizations, or subordinate discretion;
- individual actors and aggregate organizations double-counting the same people, firms, resources, or actions;
- generated actors being assigned real historical personal acts they did not perform;
- no-action freezing the world or evading deadlines;
- identical canonical situations producing different results solely because of UI cadence, iteration order, save/load, or a global random cursor.

## 1.3 Product reason

The accepted presidential core requires a country whose other participants act whether or not the President is focused on them. The game fails if every non-player participant is only:

- a response button after presidential contact;
- an authored obstacle in a workstream;
- a support percentage;
- a generic faction modifier;
- a story-delivery device;
- a material-domain puppeteer;
- or a source of automatic presidential alerts.

A Living Country actor grammar is therefore not primarily about making every entity psychologically rich. It is about preserving independent causation, bounded knowledge, institution-specific choices, and persistent consequences at the minimum resolution needed for the presidential game.

# 2. Semantic taxonomy: participant is an umbrella, not an owner

## 2.1 Individual actor

An **individual actor** is a persistent person identity whose own decision, office, relationship, public role, or history can change a supported outcome.

Examples may include:

- President;
- senator or representative;
- governor;
- judge;
- agency head;
- Cabinet or White House official;
- union leader;
- firm executive;
- journalist;
- organization leader;
- candidate;
- litigant or counsel when individual identity matters.

An individual actor may own purpose-bounded private beliefs, intentions, evaluations, memories, and decision-relevant state. The actor does not personally own authority attached to an office, institutional resources, organization membership facts, material-domain outcomes, or the truth of public claims.

## 2.2 Office

An **office** is a legally, institutionally, or organizationally constituted role.

It may define or reference:

- role identity;
- institution and jurisdiction;
- selection or appointment process;
- term or tenure;
- legally or organizationally assigned functions;
- participation rights in procedures;
- ordinary records and access associated with the role.

The office is not its holder. Authority does not become the actor’s personal property.

## 2.3 Office assignment

An **office assignment** is the time-bounded canonical relationship between an office and an actor.

Changing the assignment does not:

- create a new office;
- erase the actor;
- transfer every private belief or record;
- rewrite prior acts;
- or automatically reset the institution.

## 2.4 Institution

An **institution** is a persistent structure that owns institutional identity, operational state, capabilities, records, resources, queues, and participation in procedures where accepted.

Examples may include:

- Congress and its chambers;
- a committee;
- a court;
- an executive department or agency;
- a treasury or budget office;
- an election-administration body;
- a state agency;
- a university, hospital system, or other service institution when identity matters.

An institution may host several decision loci. It is not automatically one actor.

## 2.5 Organization

An **organization** is a persistent coordinated entity whose own membership, governance, resources, positions, operations, and actions matter.

Examples may include:

- political party;
- caucus or faction;
- union;
- firm;
- trade association;
- advocacy organization;
- nonprofit;
- media outlet;
- professional association;
- campaign organization;
- coalition with genuine persistent organization.

Organizations may act through leaders, boards, votes, delegated staff, standing rules, or other declared internal processes. Organization state is not the same as every member’s state.

## 2.6 Membership, affiliation, employment, and representation relationships

Membership, affiliation, employment, office assignment, representation, delegation, contract, and coalition participation are different relationship families.

They must not be collapsed into one generic `belongsTo` edge.

A senator may simultaneously:

- occupy a Senate office;
- belong to a party;
- join a caucus;
- represent a state;
- participate in a committee;
- negotiate an issue-specific commitment;
- receive a delegated party role.

None of those relationships alone owns the senator’s vote.

## 2.7 Procedure or rule-bound process

A **procedure** or **rule-bound process** may resolve state without a discretionary actor choice at every step.

Examples:

- a statutory deadline expiring;
- an automatic benefit formula;
- a ministerial filing acceptance;
- a vote tally after members vote;
- a queue advancing under a standing rule;
- a scheduled statistical release after completed processing.

The design must not invent a fictional actor decision where the supported causal claim is a rule-governed transition.

## 2.8 Aggregate participant class

An **aggregate participant class** may represent many entities when individual identities do not affect supported outcomes.

Examples may include:

- small firms in a sector;
- local outlets below retained individual resolution;
- routine administrative units;
- distributed advocacy chapters;
- market participants whose individual identity is not load-bearing.

An aggregate class is not one giant conscious actor. Its behavior must be represented as an accepted aggregate process, distribution, or set of bounded decisions appropriate to the claim.

## 2.9 Ordinary population is not an autonomous-actor store

The one canonical ordinary population remains governed by Step 3.

Population units may hold recipient-owned belief, salience, preference, and turnout disposition. They do not become individualized political actors merely because they can later vote, protest, organize, work, consume, or experience policy.

Collective action by ordinary people should arise through:

- organizations;
- modeled participation processes;
- elections;
- protests, petitions, strikes, or other occurrence families when later accepted;
- not by treating every weighted population carrier as one strategic mind.

# 3. Individualization, aggregation, and population accounting

## 3.1 Individualization rule

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT01]** Represent a person or organization individually only when at least one supported process depends on its persistent identity, single decision, office authority, relationship history, resources, public role, or accountability.

Individualization is justified when, for example:

- one vote can change a procedure;
- one officeholder can issue or refuse a consequential act;
- one firm closure or investment decision materially changes a region;
- one union’s bargaining position affects a supported dispute;
- one outlet or journalist’s investigation can expose evidence;
- one organization’s endorsement, lawsuit, mobilization, or campaign matters;
- one person’s trust, commitment, or prior conduct changes later interaction.

“Important in reality” is not by itself sufficient. The identity must change a supported causal claim.

## 3.2 Aggregation rule

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT02]** Aggregate when individual identity cannot change a supported outcome, observation, institutional response, relationship, or presidential decision.

Aggregation must preserve, where needed:

- counts or weights;
- relevant distributions;
- geography;
- organization or sector boundaries;
- resource totals;
- response heterogeneity;
- uncertainty;
- occurrence provenance;
- relationships to canonical population or material domains.

Aggregation may not convert a heterogeneous class into one omniscient, perfectly coordinated actor.

## 3.3 No double representation

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT03]** Individualization or aggregation may not double-count people, firms, memberships, resources, actions, votes, or material effects.

If one firm becomes individually represented, its employment, production, resources, and decisions must be removed from, partitioned within, or explicitly reconciled against any aggregate class that formerly included it.

If a named political actor is represented separately from ordinary population aggregates, the actor object must not add another person of population weight or shadow-own PopulationState facts.

A later implementation may represent the relationship through:

- a non-weight-bearing individualized overlay referencing canonical population identity;
- an exact person carrier within the population substrate;
- another reconciled method.

Step 4 does not choose the mechanism. It requires that individual actor identity does not create duplicate people, residence, household, demographic, or population totals.

## 3.4 Refinement of aggregate participants

An aggregate participant class may be refined when a named supported process now requires one entity or subgroup to diverge.

Refinement must identify:

- parent aggregate;
- child identities or classes;
- effective time;
- cause;
- allocation method;
- resources, obligations, relationships, and current state transferred;
- uncertainty;
- reconciliation.

Refinement does not manufacture a prior exact identity that was never retained.

## 3.5 Retirement, inactivity, and coarsening

A once-individualized actor or organization may become inactive, leave office, dissolve, merge, or cease to require detailed current processing.

Its identity and consequential history may not be erased while referenced by:

- historical occurrences;
- commitments;
- contracts;
- legal disputes;
- office history;
- investigations;
- actor memories;
- public artifacts;
- unresolved obligations;
- later supported queries.

Current-state detail may be compressed only when all supported consumers can still resolve correctly.

# 4. Common autonomous-participant declaration

A candidate actor, organization, institution, or aggregate participant must declare the following where causally applicable.

These are audit obligations, not a required implementation schema.

## 4.1 Semantic identity and owner

- What exists?
- Is it a person, office, assignment, institution, organization, relationship, procedure, or aggregate class?
- Who owns each mutable fact?
- What facts are explicitly excluded?

## 4.2 Representation level

- Why is identity individualized, organizational, institutional, or aggregate?
- What supported process consumes that resolution?
- What is intentionally compressed?

## 4.3 Decision locus

- Who or what actually chooses?
- Is the choice made by one officeholder, several members, a board, a vote, delegated staff, a rule, a queue, or an aggregate process?
- Which result is the institution’s action rather than merely one participant’s preference?

## 4.4 Role, standing, and scope

- What office, assignment, membership, representation, jurisdiction, contract, delegation, or other relationship gives standing to act?
- Toward which targets, geographies, populations, entities, or procedures?
- During what effective interval?

## 4.5 Authority, attemptability, and capability

- What act can the participant meaningfully attempt?
- What legal or organizational authority is claimed or applicable?
- What operational capability exists?
- What target compliance remains independent?

## 4.6 Owned current state

Potentially relevant categories include:

- beliefs and uncertainty;
- evaluations and priorities;
- public or adopted positions;
- private intentions;
- obligations and duties;
- current assignments;
- procedural strategy;
- risk posture;
- time horizon;
- organization strategy;
- operational state;
- purpose-bounded memory.

No category is required merely because it sounds realistic.

## 4.7 Information and records

- What direct records are available through role or institution?
- What artifacts or communications were received?
- What operational experience is directly known?
- What remains unknown, estimated, disputed, or inaccessible?
- What information is private, institutional, public, classified, privileged, or source-restricted?

## 4.8 Objectives, obligations, and decision criteria

- What supported goals or duties can shape this decision?
- Which constraints or commitments matter?
- Which criteria are actor-private, organizational, legal, procedural, material, electoral, or relational?
- Why are separate criteria needed rather than one utility number?

## 4.9 Resources and contention

- What owner-specific resource, queue, calendar, personnel, money, authority, access, or capability constrains the participant?
- What other uses compete for it?
- What unit and owner apply?
- Does commitment differ from actual use or result?

## 4.10 Opportunity and action derivation

- What current facts make an attempt available?
- What prerequisites, deadlines, windows, or targets apply?
- Does the option arise from law, procedure, organization rules, relationships, records, material state, or communication?
- What explicit no-action, delay, delegation, or default path exists?

## 4.11 Attempt or communication

- What canonical action, filing, vote, order, request, refusal, offer, commitment, investigation, publication attempt, allocation, or other occurrence enters the world?
- What role and authority basis are claimed?
- What resources are committed?
- What time, scope, and targets apply?

## 4.12 Independent resolver

- Which procedure, target actor, institution, domain, recipient, or legal process admits and resolves the attempt?
- What may it reject, transform, delay, ignore, partially accept, or contest?
- Which receiver-owned state can change?

## 4.13 Relationships, commitments, and delegation

- Which canonical relationship owner holds membership, contract, coalition, delegation, or negotiated terms?
- What does each participant merely believe about the relationship?
- What can be fulfilled, breached, renegotiated, revoked, or allowed to expire?

## 4.14 Time, cadence, and reconsideration

- What triggers evaluation?
- What deadline or opportunity window applies?
- What can wait?
- When is the participant reconsidered?
- Does a routine process proceed without discretionary review?

## 4.15 History, memory, succession, and closure

- What canonical occurrence is recorded?
- Which current facts remain separate from history?
- What memory or learned state persists?
- What happens when leadership, office assignment, organization, or institution changes?
- How are unfinished assignments, records, obligations, and private knowledge handled?

## 4.16 Presidential relevance and deletion justification

- Through what valid information or institutional route could this participant’s conduct reach the administration?
- Could the participant remain consequential without presidential awareness?
- Would deleting this distinction eliminate a supported action, response, observation, constraint, or presidential decision?

# 5. Ownership distinctions that may not collapse

## 5.1 Identity, role, office, assignment, and institution

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT04]** Person identity, organization identity, institution identity, office, current assignment, membership, and delegated role remain distinct facts.

An actor can leave office without ceasing to exist. An institution can persist through complete leadership turnover. A membership can end without changing office assignment. A delegation can expire without dissolving the office or institution.

## 5.2 Attemptability, authority, capability, compliance, and outcome

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT05]** The following remain distinct:

```text
structurally possible input
→ role can meaningfully attempt it
→ claimed/applicable authority
→ operational capability
→ target or procedure response
→ institutional/legal/material result
→ later observation and political interpretation
```

A President may meaningfully issue a disputed direction without possessing valid authority. A valid agency order may encounter weak operational capacity. A firm announcement may not become an actual closure. A union demand may not become a contract. A court filing may not produce relief.

## 5.3 Belief, objective, position, intention, commitment, decision, action, and result

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT06]** These facts may influence one another but are not interchangeable.

- Belief: what the participant currently thinks or estimates.
- Objective: what outcome or duty the participant currently pursues.
- Position: a public, adopted, or internally recorded stance.
- Intention: current private willingness or plan.
- Commitment: a unilateral pledge or canonical negotiated relationship, according to semantics.
- Decision: the resolved choice at a decision locus.
- Action: the canonical attempt or communication submitted to the world.
- Result: what the receiving owner or process actually resolves.

A commitment can be breached. A stated position can differ from private intention. A decision can fail during execution. An action can create an occurrence without achieving its objective.

## 5.4 Organization state and member state

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT07]** An organization may own membership relationships, adopted positions, leadership, resources, strategy, internal procedures, and organization actions. It does not own every member’s beliefs, intentions, or later decisions.

Member preferences do not automatically become an adopted organizational position. An adopted position does not automatically force member conduct.

## 5.5 Action and information artifact

The actor or institution owns the decision and attempt to speak, publish, brief, file, leak, correct, or otherwise communicate.

The resulting information artifact owns its content, source references, provenance, timestamps, classification, and revision relationship under Step 1.

`InformationEnvironment` owns realized availability, delivery, and exposure occurrences where represented.

Recipients own notice, trust, interpretation, belief, attribution, salience, and response.

## 5.6 Occurrence and current state

A historical vote, order, filing, refusal, publication, award, closure, strike, endorsement, or appointment occurrence does not own the corresponding current belief, office assignment, operational condition, relationship, or material state.

Occurrence ownership follows the one-canonical-occurrence rule accepted in Step 1.

# 6. Bounded information and actor knowledge

## 6.1 No actor reads the world root

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT08]** Autonomous choices consume role-accessible records, received artifacts and communications, direct operational or material experience, retained memory, and purpose-bounded beliefs—not debug access to canonical world truth.

An actor may know exactly:

- its own submitted action;
- a record available through its office;
- a contract it signed;
- a vote it cast;
- an order delivered to it;
- its own organization’s current resources where accurately recorded.

It may remain uncertain about:

- another actor’s private motives;
- hidden population beliefs;
- future material effects;
- incomplete measurements;
- confidential records it cannot access;
- whether a public claim is true;
- whether a target will comply.

## 6.2 Institutional records and private knowledge differ

An officeholder’s private conversation, campaign poll, personal trust judgment, or source relationship does not automatically become an institutional record.

Conversely, a legally retained agency file does not disappear when the agency head changes.

Succession and turnover must identify what transfers through:

- office records;
- institution ownership;
- organization records;
- explicit handoff;
- public availability;
- lawful access;
- or not at all.

## 6.3 Purpose-bounded belief

No participant needs a complete shadow copy of America.

A senator may need beliefs about a proposal, constituency, colleagues, procedure, and political risk. A firm may need beliefs about demand, costs, regulation, financing, and competitors. A journalist may need beliefs about source credibility, evidentiary strength, public importance, legal risk, and editorial opportunity.

Missing belief state does not grant canonical truth.

## 6.4 Shared artifact, different interpretation

Two participants may receive the same report and update differently because they have different:

- priors;
- roles;
- objectives;
- institutional duties;
- source trust;
- constituencies;
- corroborating records;
- uncertainty;
- relationships;
- incentives.

The artifact does not contain a universal downstream reaction.

## 6.5 Observation of actors

The player, public, and other actors may observe:

- public actions;
- official records;
- statements and artifacts;
- known memberships and offices;
- disclosed resources or filings;
- staff or journalistic assessments;
- inferred motives with uncertainty.

They may not automatically observe private intentions, exact utility, hidden decision weights, undisclosed commitments, or source-protected information.

# 7. Opportunity, choice, and action derivation

## 7.1 No balanced menu generator

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT09]** Available attempts arise from current institutional, legal, organizational, relational, material, informational, and temporal state. They do not exist because a designer or hidden director wants three balanced options.

An action candidate must identify why it is currently available, including relevant:

- role;
- target;
- procedure;
- authority basis or claim;
- prerequisites;
- records or information;
- resource commitment;
- deadline or window;
- known uncertainty;
- expected default without action.

## 7.2 Action vocabularies remain type-specific

Step 4 does not require one universal action enum.

A senator may:

- introduce;
- sponsor;
- amend;
- vote;
- negotiate;
- request information;
- issue a public claim;
- delay through an available procedure.

An agency may:

- review;
- determine;
- request clarification;
- issue guidance;
- promulgate or enforce through an accepted process;
- approve, deny, return, inspect, monitor, or refer.

A firm may:

- hire;
- separate;
- invest;
- close;
- relocate;
- price;
- contract;
- lobby;
- communicate.

A media organization or journalist may:

- investigate;
- corroborate;
- allocate reporting resources;
- publish;
- delay;
- correct;
- protect a source;
- abandon a weak lead.

The common grammar applies around these acts. It does not flatten them into one generic `ACT(magnitude)` command.

## 7.3 Structural admission versus world resolution

A malformed or impossible command may be rejected before becoming canonical.

A meaningful but unlawful, disputed, risky, unsupported, or unsuccessful attempt may still enter history and then be resolved by the relevant owner.

This applies to autonomous and player-selected actions alike.

## 7.4 No action, refusal, delay, and default

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT10]** A supported decision must identify the causal meaning of no action, delay, refusal, delegation, abstention, or expiration where applicable.

No action may mean:

- standing policy continues;
- an opportunity expires;
- another actor proceeds;
- a queue retains priority;
- delegated staff use existing instructions;
- a filing deadline passes;
- no public statement occurs;
- a source goes uninvestigated;
- an offer lapses;
- a project or market process continues independently.

No-action does not freeze the world.

## 7.5 Routine work versus discretionary choice

The design must not surface every ministerial or routine transition as actor cognition.

A retained discretionary decision exists when different available choices, delay, or inaction can change a supported consequence.

Routine operations may proceed through standing rules, queues, delegated instructions, or aggregate processes while preserving auditability and relevant resource use.

# 8. Autonomous initiative

## 8.1 Core initiative chain

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT11]** A load-bearing autonomous initiative follows the conceptual chain:

```text
actor/institution receives or observes bounded state
→ interprets through role, beliefs, objectives, duties,
  constituency, relationships, commitments, resources,
  risk, deadlines, and opportunity
→ derives currently available attempts
→ chooses action, delay, delegation, refusal, or no action
→ records the canonical attempt or decision occurrence
→ target/procedure/receiver resolves independently
→ consequences become current state, history, records,
  artifacts, or later opportunities through their owners
```

Not every participant uses every element. The chain identifies ownership boundaries.

## 8.2 Valid initiative triggers

Initiative may arise from:

- a received communication or artifact;
- a direct institutional record;
- material or operational experience;
- a scheduled duty or review;
- a deadline or expiring opportunity;
- a change in law or procedure;
- a constituent, member, client, source, or counterparty request;
- a relationship or commitment condition;
- a resource or queue state;
- an organization agenda;
- a public occurrence;
- proactive monitoring within the participant’s role.

## 8.3 Hidden-director prohibition

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT12]** No actor may initiate, escalate, oppose, investigate, defect, endorse, sue, leak, close, strike, or communicate primarily because a hidden narrative or pressure director requires drama.

Configured opportunities, actor types, goals, shocks, calendars, and content primitives are legitimate.

The realized initiative must still be derivable from canonical state, bounded information, participant state, and available action space.

## 8.4 Quiet actors are valid

An actor or organization may reasonably do nothing because:

- it does not know;
- evidence is weak;
- the issue falls outside its role;
- no valid action is available;
- resources are committed elsewhere;
- expected benefit is low;
- risk is high;
- a deadline has not arrived;
- another actor is already responsible;
- members disagree;
- the opportunity is not salient to that participant;
- delay is strategically preferred.

The simulation does not owe the player a response from every important actor.

## 8.5 Autonomous action need not reach the President

An organization, firm, state, court, agency, journalist, or legislator may act and alter the country without creating immediate presidential attention.

Presidential receipt remains governed by observable information, institutional channels, staff review, and valid escalation under the accepted Step 5 core.

# 9. Objectives and decision policies without one universal utility function

## 9.1 Purpose-bounded criteria

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT13]** Different participant families may use radically different decision criteria and internal processes. Step 4 standardizes causal participation, not psychology.

Potentially relevant criteria include:

- legal duty;
- policy preference;
- constituency interest;
- member interest;
- institutional mission;
- professional norm;
- profit, cost, liquidity, or operational risk;
- electoral ambition;
- ideological or moral objective;
- public reputation as perceived by specific audiences;
- relationship trust;
- commitment honor or breach;
- evidentiary sufficiency;
- procedural strategy;
- organizational survival;
- time horizon;
- uncertainty tolerance.

No list is universal.

## 9.2 Participant examples

A legislator may weigh proposal substance, constituency, party coordination, commitments, procedure, reelection, and trust.

An agency office may weigh statutory authority, record completeness, program criteria, mission, capacity, precedent, deadlines, and review risk.

A governor may weigh state law, fiscal exposure, administration capacity, coalition, local effects, federal terms, and electoral consequences.

A firm may weigh expected revenue, cost, financing, regulation, workforce, supply, location, contracts, and strategic risk.

A union may weigh member distribution, bargaining position, contract terms, strike capacity, leadership mandate, employer condition, law, and public support.

A journalist or outlet may weigh evidence, source credibility, corroboration, public value, audience, legal risk, editorial priorities, competition, and scarce investigative capacity.

A judge or court process must be modeled through applicable law, procedure, record, institutional role, and accepted judicial decision semantics—not a generic political-support utility merely because judges are political people.

## 9.3 Objectives can conflict

A participant may face conflict among:

- legal duty and political preference;
- organization position and personal evaluation;
- short-term survival and long-term strategy;
- member interests across subgroups;
- profit and contract obligation;
- speed and evidentiary quality;
- public position and private intention;
- principal instruction and delegated discretion.

Conflict need not be reduced to one exposed score.

## 9.4 Objective change

Objectives, priorities, risk posture, and beliefs may change only through accepted processes such as:

- new information;
- experience;
- leadership change;
- membership change;
- election;
- legal duty;
- material condition;
- organizational decision;
- relationship consequence;
- fulfilled or breached commitment;
- generated history.

They may not be rewritten by a workstream, UI, story need, or target outcome.

# 10. Resources, capacity, and contention

## 10.1 Owner-specific resources

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT14]** Participant action is constrained by owner-specific resources and institutional opportunities rather than one universal action currency.

Examples may include:

- floor and committee time;
- legislative staff and drafting capacity;
- presidential or governor calendar time;
- agency review staff and case queues;
- appropriated dollars and obligation authority;
- organization cash and field staff;
- union strike funds and organizer capacity;
- firm cash, credit, facilities, contracts, and management attention;
- outlet investigative teams, legal review, source access, and publication space;
- court docket and procedural windows;
- platform distribution capability;
- state administrative capacity;
- information access and records.

## 10.2 Resource semantics

Every load-bearing resource must declare:

- owner;
- unit or classification;
- current availability;
- commitments or reservations;
- competing uses;
- time/geography/scope;
- replenishment, expiration, or release;
- whether it is authority, capability, money, time, access, or another distinct fact.

## 10.3 Commitment is not use; use is not success

Reserving staff, assigning an investigator, appropriating money, announcing investment, scheduling floor time, or authorizing a strike does not prove the intended result occurred.

The resource owner records commitment and use. The receiving process or material domain resolves the consequence.

## 10.4 Influence and credibility are not universal possessions

“Influence,” “credibility,” “legitimacy,” and “reputation” may be useful player-facing summaries only when derived from underlying relationships, audience beliefs, records, offices, resources, and context.

An organization may be credible to one audience and distrusted by another. A senator may have leverage on one committee but none over a state agency. A firm may dominate one local labor market but lack legal authority.

No global scalar may cause all receivers to comply.

# 11. Relationships, organizations, coalitions, and commitments

## 11.1 One relationship owner

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT15]** Membership, office assignment, delegation, employment, contract, negotiated commitment, coalition, representation, and other canonical relationship facts are owned once by their declared relationship or institution owner.

Participants may hold:

- references;
- private beliefs about the relationship;
- trust or evaluation;
- willingness to honor, breach, leave, or renegotiate;
- memories of prior conduct.

They may not maintain competing authoritative copies of the relationship.

## 11.2 Organizations are not hive minds

An organization action requires a declared decision locus or rule.

Depending on the organization and supported resolution, that may be:

- leader authority;
- executive team;
- board vote;
- membership vote;
- convention or caucus procedure;
- delegated staff;
- standing policy;
- aggregate response process.

The organization’s adopted action may then differ from some members’ preferences.

## 11.3 Coalitions remain scoped

A coalition may be:

- a derived estimate of likely aligned actors;
- a real negotiated relationship around one objective;
- an organization with persistent structure;
- an electoral coalition projected over population;
- a temporary coordination network.

Those forms do not collapse into one universal coalition-strength meter.

## 11.4 Commitments influence but do not force

A negotiated commitment may own:

- participants;
- terms;
- conditions;
- scope;
- effective interval;
- visibility;
- status;
- fulfillment, breach, withdrawal, or expiration.

The actor or organization still decides whether and how to honor it when discretion remains.

Breach may alter relationships, information, organization response, electoral interpretation, or future bargaining through their respective owners.

## 11.5 Delegation contract

A load-bearing delegation must identify:

- principal and delegate;
- role or authority basis;
- objective or duty;
- scope and targets;
- resources made available;
- constraints;
- decisions the delegate may make;
- decisions reserved to the principal;
- reporting thresholds;
- deadlines;
- revocation, expiration, and succession rules;
- records and information transferred.

Delegation does not make the delegate a puppet. It does not transfer more authority than the principal can validly delegate. It does not guarantee compliance or success.

# 12. Institutions and internal decision loci

## 12.1 Institutions persist beyond actors

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT16]** Institutional identity, rules, records, resources, queues, obligations, and capabilities persist according to their owners when officeholders or leaders change.

Leadership may alter strategy, priorities, discretionary decisions, appointments, and relationships through accepted processes. It does not automatically replace law, erase records, reset queues, or eliminate prior obligations.

## 12.2 Every consequential institutional act identifies its locus

The phrase “Congress decided,” “the agency chose,” “the court acted,” “the company closed,” or “the media reported” is insufficient when the supported outcome depends on who or what actually decided.

The design must identify whether the locus was:

- an individual officeholder;
- a chamber vote;
- committee action;
- a court or panel under procedure;
- an agency official under delegated authority;
- a rule-bound program process;
- a corporate board or executive;
- a union vote or leader;
- an editor, journalist, or newsroom process;
- an aggregate class response.

## 12.3 Internal disagreement

An institution or organization may contain conflicting members, offices, units, professional roles, or objectives.

Step 4 does not require full internal simulation everywhere. It requires internal distinctions when they can return as different:

- actions;
- delays;
- legal positions;
- records;
- resource allocations;
- public claims;
- compliance;
- presidential options.

## 12.4 Rule-bound processing and discretion

The same institution may contain:

- automatic or ministerial work;
- bounded staff discretion;
- leader discretion;
- collective decisions;
- judicial or administrative review;
- appeal or escalation.

A system may not attribute every result to the leader merely because the leader is visible to the player.

## 12.5 Institutional failure is not one capacity score

Delay or failure may arise from different causes:

- missing authority;
- insufficient appropriations;
- vacancies;
- staff workload;
- incompatible records;
- procedural deadline;
- legal dispute;
- target noncooperation;
- technical or material bottleneck;
- leadership choice;
- internal conflict;
- information failure.

Those causes must remain distinguishable when they change later remedies or accountability.

# 13. Governmental application

## 13.1 Congress

Congressional representation must preserve:

- chambers, committees, leadership, offices, assignments, procedures, parties/caucuses, legislators, and votes as distinct facts;
- individual legislator decision authority where one vote matters;
- collective procedure for chamber or committee action;
- organization coordination that influences but does not own votes;
- bounded member information;
- scheduling and procedural opportunities;
- issue-specific commitments and relationships;
- no automatic conversion of presidential preference into legislative action.

## 13.2 Governors and state government

A state response must distinguish:

- state jurisdiction;
- governor as actor and officeholder;
- state agencies and their capacity;
- state law and procedure;
- legislature or other required institutions where supported;
- fiscal and program relationships;
- local or regional material conditions;
- federal request, incentive, requirement, or claimed compulsion;
- state-owned decision and implementation.

A federal output cannot pre-resolve state acceptance, refusal, waiver request, lawsuit, delay, or implementation.

## 13.3 Agencies

An agency must distinguish:

- institution;
- leadership offices and assignments;
- law and delegated authority;
- program rules;
- current records and cases;
- staff/operational capacity;
- queues and deadlines;
- ministerial transitions;
- discretionary determinations;
- enforcement or guidance actions;
- review, appeal, and legal exposure.

The President may direct within available authority. The agency’s lawful obligations, professional processes, records, capacity, and independent decision loci remain real.

## 13.4 Courts

Courts participate through legal/judicial procedures, records, jurisdiction, offices, assignments, applicable law, filings, orders, and institutional compliance.

A court is not a generic opposing actor. A judge’s individualized identity is retained when it changes a supported discrete outcome, but the legal process and applicable sources remain distinct from private preference.

A ruling changes legal/judicial state within scope. It does not directly rewrite past material history or guarantee compliance.

## 13.5 White House and executive offices

Step 4 accepts only the common participation grammar for White House offices and officials.

It requires that later internal-administration design preserve:

- office versus officeholder;
- assignment and delegation;
- institution-owned records;
- private versus institutional knowledge;
- owner-specific staff, calendars, queues, and authority;
- disagreement and escalation;
- decisions staff can resolve without the President;
- decisions reserved to presidential authority;
- valid attention provenance.

It does not yet select the office roster, staffing model, appointment game, Cabinet depth, or exact presidential decision surface.

## 13.6 Player decision source

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT17]** Replacing autonomous selection with player selection for a supported decision locus changes who selects an available intent—not the authority, procedural weight, target response, material effect, or downstream causal semantics of the canonical action.

A player-selected vote, direction, veto, filing, or public claim resolves under the same world rules as the same action selected autonomously or by a deterministic test source.

The `ControlBinding` does not make related institutions or subordinates player-owned.

# 14. Firms, unions, advocacy, and civil society

## 14.1 Firms

A retained firm or business organization may own:

- organization identity;
- facilities, contracts, financial/operational resources where assigned;
- leadership and internal governance;
- current strategy;
- firm decisions and communications;
- employment or production actions at the firm-decision boundary;
- legal and political actions.

Material domains remain owners of the broader conditions produced through accepted processes, such as aggregate employment, production, prices, supply, Housing, energy, or regional economic effects.

A firm announcement is an information artifact/communication. It is not proof that a facility closed, workers separated, prices changed, or output fell.

## 14.2 Unions and worker organizations

A union may own:

- organization identity;
- membership relationship at supported resolution;
- leadership and internal decision process;
- resources;
- adopted bargaining positions;
- strike authorization or other organization decisions;
- communications and political activity;
- negotiated commitments or contracts through their declared relationship owner.

The union does not own each member’s belief, vote, household state, employment fact, or turnout.

Labor and relevant material/legal owners resolve employment, wages, work stoppages, production effects, contracts, and legal consequences according to accepted semantics.

## 14.3 Advocacy and membership organizations

Advocacy organizations may investigate, litigate, lobby, mobilize, endorse, communicate, coordinate, and allocate resources when supported.

They do not own:

- the truth of the condition they describe;
- every member’s view;
- public belief;
- law merely because they advocate for it;
- a politician’s action;
- the election result.

## 14.4 Service institutions

Hospitals, universities, schools, utilities, nonprofits, and other service institutions may require individual identity when their decisions, capacity, geography, network role, records, or public significance alter supported outcomes.

Otherwise they may remain aggregate domain entities.

Their institutional decisions remain distinct from the material/service conditions their domains own and from the people associated through Step 3.

## 14.5 Markets are not actors

A market price, unemployment rate, migration flow, disease spread, public belief distribution, or aggregate demand response is not automatically an actor decision.

Actor choices may contribute inputs. Material/social owners resolve aggregate state through their accepted processes.

The design may use aggregate behavioral models without inventing a single “market actor” or “public actor” that chooses the outcome.

# 15. Media organizations and journalists

## 15.1 Ownership chain

The accepted Step 1 information split remains controlling:

```text
underlying fact or occurrence
    → owned by material/institutional/legal/population owner

investigative, editorial, publication, briefing,
leak, correction, or distribution attempt
    → owned by acting journalist/outlet/platform/institution

story, report, claim, memo, correction, or other artifact
    → owns its content/provenance/classification

realized availability, delivery, or exposure occurrence
    → owned by InformationEnvironment

notice, trust, belief, attribution, salience, response
    → owned by recipient
```

## 15.2 Media organization

A media organization may own:

- organization identity;
- leadership and editorial governance;
- staff and investigative resources;
- access and source relationships where represented;
- editorial priorities;
- legal/financial constraints;
- publication or correction decisions;
- channels and distribution attempts.

It does not own audience belief or the truth of what it reports.

## 15.3 Journalist or reporting team

Individualize a journalist or team when identity, source trust, expertise, prior reporting, persistence, risk, or one investigative decision changes a supported outcome.

Aggregate routine reporting where individual identity adds nothing.

A journalist may receive:

- tip;
- public record;
- source communication;
- anomaly;
- leaked artifact;
- direct observation;
- competing claim.

The journalist or outlet may then choose whether to investigate, corroborate, publish, delay, correct, or abandon through bounded information and resources.

## 15.4 Investigation does not create misconduct

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT18]** An investigation may discover, fail to discover, misunderstand, or remain unable to prove an existing canonical condition or occurrence. It may not create the underlying corruption, failure, abuse, or scandal because the political term needs a story.

Evidence artifacts must arise from valid records, sources, observations, and investigative processes.

## 15.5 Publication does not guarantee reach or persuasion

An outlet may publish without broad delivery. Delivery may occur without notice. Notice may occur without trust. Trust may not produce the intended attribution or political action.

No `mediaPressure` value may directly command agencies, politicians, courts, firms, populations, or the President.

# 16. Common action-handoff contract

## 16.1 General form

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT19]** A consequential participant action follows:

```text
decision locus selects an available attempt
→ canonical attempted action or communication is recorded
→ attempt carries actor/role, type, target, time, scope,
  authority claim, relevant references, resource commitment,
  visibility, and provenance where applicable
→ receiving owner validates and admits under its rules
→ receiver may reject, transform, delay, partially accept,
  contest, ignore, or resolve
→ only receiver-owned current state changes at that boundary
→ one canonical occurrence identity and later outputs exist
```

The exact payload differs by action family. No universal runtime envelope is accepted.

## 16.2 Sender cannot prescribe receiver state

A party recommendation cannot set a senator’s vote.

A presidential request cannot set a governor’s decision.

A union announcement cannot set member belief or employer response.

A firm announcement cannot set Labor or market state.

A media story cannot set public belief.

A lawsuit cannot set a court ruling.

An agency approval cannot set material completion.

The receiver owns admissibility, timing, transformation, and result.

## 16.3 Identity and idempotence

A canonical attempt or occurrence must have stable identity sufficient to prevent accidental repeated application.

Several workstreams, reports, actors, or domains may reference the same act. Reference multiplicity does not make the act happen several times.

## 16.4 Failure and partial success are real outcomes

A meaningful attempt may produce:

- rejection;
- no response;
- delay;
- partial compliance;
- counteroffer;
- amendment;
- litigation;
- implementation without desired material effect;
- public attention without belief change;
- breach;
- unintended consequence.

Those are not errors if they follow accepted owner state.

# 17. Time, cadence, concurrency, and deterministic continuation

## 17.1 No universal actor turn

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT20]** Actors and institutions do not all reevaluate on one daily, weekly, or monthly turn merely because the player advances time at that cadence.

Evaluation may be triggered by:

- exact occurrence;
- receipt;
- deadline;
- scheduled duty;
- queue position;
- material threshold;
- relationship change;
- periodic review;
- bounded proactive monitoring;
- mixed semantics.

## 17.2 Concurrent initiatives

Several actors and institutions may act during the same period without waiting for the President or for one workstream to finish.

Their resource conflicts, procedural dependencies, communications, and consequences remain owner-specific.

## 17.3 Opportunity windows and expiration

An available action may have:

- earliest attempt time;
- deadline;
- expiration;
- reconsideration time;
- response window;
- standing default;
- conditional availability.

Delay can change feasibility, information, relationships, costs, or who acts first.

## 17.4 Same-time ordering

If same-time ordering changes an outcome, the dependency must be explicit.

Independent actions use stable deterministic tie-breaking where order is semantically irrelevant.

Container order, registration order, rendering, or file order may not decide political reality.

## 17.5 Stochastic decisions

Probabilistic actor or aggregate behavior, where later accepted, must use deterministic causal randomness bound to explicit identities and context.

Identical canonical inputs and causal identities may not yield different outcomes because of:

- UI stepping;
- global RNG consumption;
- save/load;
- scheduler reconstruction;
- unrelated actor iteration;
- container ordering.

Step 4 does not choose the RNG or decision algorithm.

## 17.6 Time-chunk invariance

Equivalent advancement over the same dated canonical inputs and decisions must produce equivalent participant actions and world results.

Advancing one day repeatedly may not silently give actors more opportunities than advancing to the same date in one supported chunk.

# 18. History, memory, turnover, and generated actors

## 18.1 Persistent identity

Actors and organizations retain identity across:

- office changes;
- elections;
- leadership turnover;
- policy disputes;
- commitments;
- public actions;
- investigations;
- periods of inactivity.

A role change does not reset biography or relationships.

## 18.2 Purpose-bounded memory

Actor memory may retain facts needed for later supported decisions, such as:

- prior negotiation;
- fulfilled or breached commitment;
- public attack or endorsement;
- trusted or unreliable source;
- institutional conflict;
- electoral consequence;
- prior legal or administrative dispute.

Memory is recipient-owned state referencing canonical occurrences or artifacts. It is not a second history store and need not preserve every past detail.

## 18.3 Turnover and succession

Leadership or office turnover must separately resolve:

- office assignment;
- institutional records;
- delegated authority;
- active queues and cases;
- unresolved commitments and contracts;
- organization strategy;
- private knowledge;
- access rights;
- relationships tied to person versus office;
- public expectations;
- current actions already in flight.

The incoming leader does not inherit all private state. The institution does not lose all continuity.

## 18.4 Organization dissolution, merger, and split

A later supported organization transition must preserve:

- occurrence identity;
- predecessor/successor lineage;
- assets and liabilities;
- memberships;
- contracts and commitments;
- records;
- legal status;
- ongoing actions;
- unresolved disputes;
- public artifacts.

Exact formulas remain downstream.

## 18.5 Generated actor history

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ACT21]** Generated actors may receive generated or reconciled personal histories consistent with the accepted world. They may not be falsely assigned real pre-divergence individual votes, statements, orders, rulings, crimes, employment decisions, or other personal acts.

Real institutions, laws, offices, aggregate outcomes, and records may be inherited at a historical seam. Fictional personal attribution requires generated history or an explicitly fictionalized scenario.

## 18.6 No retroactive exactness

If an earlier aggregate process did not retain exact individual identity, later individualization cannot fabricate which exact entity made every past decision.

The design must use:

- prospective identity;
- modeled or bounded reconstruction;
- aggregate history;
- explicit unknown;
- or another honest representation.

# 19. Presidential attention and administration receipt

## 19.1 Actor conduct does not directly interrupt the player

A consequential actor action, institutional delay, firm closure, union dispute, local investigation, court filing, state refusal, or agency problem may exist without immediate presidential awareness.

The accepted attention chain remains:

```text
canonical occurrence or condition
→ observable artifact, communication, record, or report
→ administration receipt through a valid channel
→ bounded staff or office interpretation
→ escalation, delegation, monitoring, or no escalation
→ possible presidential decision surface
```

## 19.2 Escalation must be owned

A later staff or office escalation must identify:

- role and delegated basis;
- received artifact or record;
- assessment;
- confidence and uncertainty;
- why presidential authority or judgment may be required;
- deadline or expiration;
- forecasted consequence of delay or inaction;
- what the office can handle independently.

No senior-adviser object may hide a narrative director.

## 19.3 Proactive review remains possible

The President or administration may proactively inspect accessible country conditions, records, actors, organizations, or institutions through valid information surfaces.

Proactivity does not reveal debug truth. It may commission analysis, request records, direct monitoring, or assign investigation within authority and capacity.

## 19.4 Serious but unpoliticized actor paths are valid

A state agency may quietly accumulate a backlog. A union may fail to organize affected workers. A local outlet may lack investigative resources. A firm may make incremental cuts without a public announcement. A committee may never schedule a bill.

These paths may remain consequential without becoming national drama.

The current accepted implementation and architecture already demonstrate several Step 4-compatible principles worth retaining:

Living Country requires later extension toward:

# 23. Hostile proof cases

A detached audit should attempt to falsify the candidate with at least the following cases.

## ACT-A — Party recommendation versus senator vote

A party recommends support. A senator receives the recommendation, has a conflicting constituency estimate and prior commitment, and votes against the party.

**Required:** party action, membership, actor belief, commitment, vote, procedure result, and later organization reaction remain separately owned.

**Forbidden:** party posture directly writes the vote.

## ACT-B — Presidential request versus governor response

The President requests state participation in a program. The governor can accept, refuse, negotiate, delay, request a waiver, or litigate depending on state law, records, politics, capacity, and terms.

**Required:** federal attempt does not own state action or implementation.

## ACT-C — Agency leader, staff process, and legal duty

An agency head prefers approval, but the record is incomplete and a rule-bound review requires return for additional evidence.

**Required:** leader preference, delegated authority, program record, staff/ministerial process, decision, and appeal route remain distinct.

**Forbidden:** visible leader preference bypasses the accepted procedure.

## ACT-D — Firm closure announcement

A firm announces a planned plant closure.

**Required:** announcement artifact, actual operational decision, later facility activity, worker separations, household effects, Labor state, regional material effects, and political attribution resolve through their owners.

**Forbidden:** announcement directly sets unemployment or presidential attention.

## ACT-E — Union strike authorization

A union authorizes a strike after an internal process. Some members oppose it; actual participation is incomplete; the employer responds; material output changes later.

**Required:** organization action does not own every member’s belief or actual participation, and Labor/Industry owners resolve material consequences.

## ACT-F — Investigation without scripted scandal

A journalist receives a weak tip concerning an agency contract. The outlet can investigate, seek records, corroborate, publish cautiously, abandon, or be preempted by another outlet.

**Required:** misconduct exists or does not exist independently; evidence quality and resources shape publication; no hidden director creates guilt.

## ACT-G — Court filing versus ruling and compliance

An advocacy group files suit. The court accepts or rejects under jurisdiction and procedure; an order may issue; the agency may comply, appeal, or contest within supported law.

**Required:** filing, ruling, order, compliance, appeal, material history, and public reporting remain distinct.

## ACT-H — Quiet non-action

A regional problem is known to a weak local organization but lacks resources and a viable national route. The organization does nothing. No national outlet investigates. The White House receives no valid alert.

**Required:** the condition and local consequences continue without drama generation.

## ACT-I — Decision-source substitution

The same available executive or legislative intent is selected once by autonomous logic and once through a player/test source from identical state.

**Required:** downstream authority, procedure, target response, and material semantics are identical.

## ACT-J — Bounded knowledge

A governor’s private intention changes after a confidential state briefing unavailable to the White House.

**Required:** the administration may infer but not read the intention or briefing from canonical truth.

## ACT-K — Universal influence exploit

An organization has a high public “influence” summary.

**Required:** that summary cannot command unrelated legislators, courts, agencies, firms, populations, or media. Each response needs a valid action and receiver process.

## ACT-L — Individualization without double count

One firm is extracted from a regional small-firm aggregate because its closure becomes consequential.

**Required:** employment, resources, production, and actions reconcile between aggregate and individual representation.

## ACT-M — Individual actor versus population

A named senator is represented as an actor while remaining one ordinary person in national population accounting.

**Required:** actor identity adds no extra population weight and does not shadow residence, household, or demographic truth.

## ACT-N — Delegation and principal-agent divergence

A White House office delegates a bounded negotiation to an official. The official receives new information and chooses a permitted compromise the principal dislikes.

**Required:** delegation scope, delegate discretion, action, principal reaction, and later revocation or acceptance remain causal.

## ACT-O — Succession and information transfer

An agency head leaves office while a case, confidential source, public record, queue, and personal relationship remain active.

**Required:** institutional records and case persist; private knowledge and personal trust do not automatically transfer; authority follows the new assignment.

## ACT-P — Iteration-order and time-chunk invariance

Several independent actors reconsider at the same timestamp. Container order and one-day versus multi-day advancement are changed.

**Required:** identical causal inputs produce equivalent canonical outcomes.

## ACT-Q — Action without presidential escalation

A state agency delays implementation, a local outlet reports it, and a regional organization complains. The matter never reaches a valid White House channel.

**Required:** all actions and consequences remain real without a presidential prompt.

# 24. Anti-ontology and anti-overbuilding tests

Every later actor/institution candidate should survive these tests.

## 24.1 Universal-mind test

Could the design be reduced to one generic utility vector and decision loop without losing a supported institutional difference?

If yes, the common model may be acceptable only if the lost distinction truly does not matter. If the reduction erases law, procedure, professional role, organization governance, information, or resource differences, reject it.

## 24.2 Hive-mind test

Does an organization’s position automatically become every member’s action or belief?

If yes, reject.

## 24.3 Leader-puppet test

Does one visible leader directly own every institutional outcome?

If yes, identify the actual decision loci, records, rules, staff discretion, and procedures.

## 24.4 Universal-power test

Does one influence, legitimacy, capacity, or pressure score resolve unrelated receivers?

If yes, decompose into owner-specific relationships, resources, actions, and receiver responses.

## 24.5 Omniscience test

Could the actor make the same choice only because the engine gave it hidden canonical truth?

If yes, require a valid record, artifact, communication, experience, belief, or modeled uncertainty.

## 24.6 Narrative-director test

Would the actor still act if the designer did not need conflict or content at that moment?

If no, the initiative lacks causal provenance.

## 24.7 Decision-locus test

Can the design answer who or what actually chose?

If no, “institution acted” is hiding an ownership gap.

## 24.8 Receiver-ownership test

Does the action type prescribe the target’s state change?

If yes, restore independent admission and resolution.

## 24.9 Double-count test
