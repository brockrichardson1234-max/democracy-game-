## Bottom line

The implementation history proves a substantial causal simulation foundation. It does not prove a deep presidential strategy game.

The roadmap was optimized to answer structural and causal questions: who owns a fact, whether autonomous actors remain autonomous, whether state survives save/load and succession, whether the U.S. can be configured without hardcoding, whether legislation can lead to fiscal/administrative/material consequences, and whether one complete U.S. route is production-reachable.

Those are legitimate achievements. But they are different from proving strategic density, recurring tradeoffs, national legibility, replayability, or fun.

The central distinction is:

> **System present does not mean gameplay present.**

A domain may have canonical state, autonomous resolution, tests, persistence, and player-facing records while still producing little or no recurring strategic choice.

## Two implementation programs

### Synthetic GL0 program

The synthetic walking skeleton asked whether one complete causal governing loop could execute without collapsing law, finance, administration, states, Housing, information, voters, courts, elections, and succession into one another.

It deliberately used a tiny content surface and utilitarian UI. Its success proves architecture and causality, not commercial breadth.

### U.S. I1–I10 program

The U.S. vertical asked whether the generic architecture could be instantiated at recognizable United States scale using researched structure/data, then connected into the default production runtime.

The program moved roughly from configuration separation, to U.S. topology, to Congress/political actors, geography/population, elections/succession, public finance/HOME/federalism, Housing, information/public response, courts, and finally I10 runtime convergence.

This is an effective simulation-productionization sequence. It is not sufficient by itself as a product-design sequence.

## Synthetic GL0 proof limits

### Runtime bootstrap

Proved headless simulation/application/UI boundaries, deterministic transition infrastructure, CI, and player-safe projection separation.

Did not prove political gameplay, a meaningful decision, a term, real U.S. content, or fun.

### Legislation, law, administration, federalism

Proved proposals versus enacted law, individual actor votes, fiscal/administrative authority separation, and independent state/federal decisions.

Did not prove recurring congressional strategy, committees, leadership, multiple bills, rich bargaining, durable relationships, or competing national priorities.

### Finance, recipients, Housing, implementation

Proved authorization/appropriation/allocation/award/obligation/payment and material project progression could remain separate; completion/usability could differ; local delayed Housing consequences could emerge.

Did not prove a federal budget game, a national Housing strategy, competing recipients/uses of funds, repeated implementation triage, or broad economic interaction.

### Measurement, claims, public response, election, succession

Proved lagged measurement, information artifacts, competing claims, exposure, belief, attribution, salience, preference, turnout, election, office transfer, and persistent-world succession could remain separate.

Did not prove a news ecosystem, competing issue salience, campaign gameplay, opposition strategy, coalition development, or an election summarizing a rich four-year governing history.

### Contested executive authority

Proved a disputed executive attempt, legal challenge, scoped judicial order, separate compliance, appeal/stay, and persistent legal history.

Did not prove a broad recurring judicial-strategy game.

### Final synthetic composition

Proved the complete causal skeleton could coexist deterministically in one persistent world.

Did not prove real U.S. scale, strategic density, replayability, production interaction quality, or a coherent commercial presidency.

## U.S. implementation proof limits

### I1 — configuration boundary

Proved the United States could be a named content/configuration package rather than hardcoded generic-engine identity. Synthetic scenario behavior was moved out of generic runtime ownership.

Did not prove a playable United States.

### I2 — U.S. governmental topology

Proved researched 50-state/DC identity, 435 House seats/districts, 100 Senate seats/classes, apportionment, topology, and provenance.

Did not prove functioning Congress, political behavior, parties, committees, bargaining, or elections as gameplay.

### I3 — legislature and political organizations

Proved hundreds of individual political actors, assignments, individual votes, one proposal route, sponsorship, political organizations, commitments, bounded staff outlook, and autonomous legislative actions outside player control.

Did not prove recurring congressional gameplay, multiple bills, committees, leadership agendas, faction strategy, logrolling, constituency pressure, durable relationships, or a satisfying negotiation grammar.

Important distinction: 535 political actors are structural simulation scale; they are not automatically 535 meaningful political characters.

### I4 — geography and aggregate population

Proved state/district geography, 51 population controls representing roughly 341.8 million people, 106 weighted cohorts, deterministic split/merge with population conservation, and Electoral College topology.

Did not prove macroeconomy, employment/unemployment, wages/income, poverty/inequality, health, education, crime, immigration, energy/environment, demographic evolution, state political cultures, or a broad national-condition model.

The represented population scale is genuine, but it does not mean the game already simulates the economic/social lives of 341 million people.

### I5 — elections, rollover, succession

Proved population-backed state presidential results, elector allocation/process, office entitlement/assignment, congressional term rollover, new administrations, ControlBinding transfer, and persistent-world succession.

Did not prove campaign gameplay, candidate emergence/nomination, parties, opposition strategy, polling, state coalition politics, midterm strategic transformation, or a rich governing record feeding election choice.

### I6 — public finance, HOME, recipients, federalism, BABA

Proved enacted terms can create downstream fiscal/program rules while allocation, award, obligation, recipient commitment/activity/draw/payment remain separate owner-resolved records. Proved selected intergovernmental relationships and BABA waiver outcomes can generate typed implementation inputs without directly mutating Housing.

Did not prove a federal-budget game, taxation/revenue/deficit/debt, broad agency management, nationwide recipient/state dynamics, recurring implementation prioritization, or that each administrative request deserves a player click.

BABA is a strong example of mechanically present capability that was not yet production gameplay at I10.

### I7 — canonical Housing material route

Proved validated government inputs feed Housing without direct policy-to-outcome mutation; compliance holds can block projects; physical completion and usability differ; project effects are local and state summaries derive from regional truth.

Did not prove a national Housing market, migration/wages/rates/construction-cost interaction, zoning/land-use politics, large project portfolios, multiple Housing-policy approaches, or national-scale strategic effect.

### I8 — information, measurement, claims, public response

Proved observation, measurement, release, claim, delivery/exposure, and population response are separate; measurements can lag/error; direct experience can differ from mediated information; claim framing can affect response without changing truth; election intake consumes Population-owned political state.

Did not prove journalism/media organizations, several concurrent issues/claims, presidential and opposition communication strategy, public-attention competition, scandals/leaks, or a politically inhabited national mood.

### I9 — legal contest

Proved filing, docketing, interim request, ruling, interpretation, order, notice, compliance, stay, appeal, and administrative consequences are separate and deterministic; legal relief does not rewind completed material history.

Did not prove a broad judiciary, judicial appointments, multiple simultaneous cases, doctrine as recurring strategic risk, court composition, or political reactions to courts.

### I10 — production runtime convergence

Proved the accepted U.S. simulation became the default production application rather than a headless fixture; the normal factory boots the U.S. configuration; GL0 synthetic content is quarantined; the complete I3–I9 route can be traversed through production vocabulary and player-safe projections; autonomous owners resolve their own actions; save/load and deterministic time work; opposition succession ends outgoing control while preserving the world.

Repairs correctly removed player control over autonomous acts such as sponsor introduction, organization coordination outcomes, fiscal recognition, and court-owned resolution.

I10 did not prove that the game was understandable, presidential, strategically dense, replayable, fun, or commercially viable.

## Actual I10 player action surface

The accepted runtime contains far more simulation state than meaningful player choice.

### Agenda

The opening action is a fixed `Set a balanced delivery agenda` operation that submits a preconfigured multidimensional Housing package. The engine has proposal dimensions, but the production route exposes almost no real agenda-setting choice.

### Sponsorship

The player can begin a sponsor search and approach a few eligible members. The member independently accepts/declines. This is a legitimate bounded choice, but lacks enough political history, constituency, committee, faction, relationship, or broader-agenda context to be deeply strategic.

### Political organizations

The player can request organization support/coordination; the organization resolves independently. This preserves agency but exposes little substantive bargaining, concession, commitment, issue trade, or relationship gameplay.

### Amendments

The underlying engine supports versions/amendments/conditions, but the normal route largely exposes `Close amendment negotiations` rather than a meaningful set of amendment tradeoffs.

### Presentment

Sign/veto is a real constitutional choice, but its strategic value is limited when the route contains one administration-driven proposal and insufficiently legible compromise history.

### Implementation

The player requests apportionment, one selected award, one selected recipient commitment/activity/draw/payment chain, and related steps. These requests correctly enter autonomous owner boundaries, but the player mostly asks for the next necessary step in a fixed route. There are few competing recipients, uses of funds, project portfolios, capacity allocations, or alternative implementation strategies.

### Legal response

Comply/delay/contest/noncompliance/stay is the strongest genuine decision seam in the production route. Different choices can change future legal/administrative state while preserving court independence.

### Time advancement

Automatic world advancement is architecturally correct and prevents procedural click-spam. It becomes strategically interesting only when autonomous activity repeatedly creates new dilemmas; in I10 it often merely reveals the next required request in the same chain.

## Why prior audits could pass

The acceptance gates mostly tested structural correctness:

- correct owner mutations;
- autonomous actors not player-commanded;
- causal reachability;
- save/load persistence;
- time-chunk invariance;
- U.S. content/configuration separation;
- succession continuity.

They did not primarily test whether the player chooses among competing priorities, understands Congress/country context, develops a strategy, experiences a rich four-year political history, or wants to play another run.

Thus the gates were highly capable of detecting causal cheating, ownership violations, nondeterminism, and unreachable routes, but not an experience dominated by advancing and requesting the next step.

## Autonomy versus gameplay

Removing illegitimate player control was correct. But autonomous ownership alone does not create a game. The missing layer must surround autonomous actors with presidential decisions such as whom to approach, what to concede, which issue/bill to prioritize, what opportunity to sacrifice, which implementation failure to tolerate, which state conflict to escalate, which legal risk to accept, and what message to emphasize.

## Real scale versus strategic breadth

The U.S. runtime contains real structural scale—hundreds of congressional actors, national geography, population controls/cohorts, Electoral College topology, Housing regions, and researched government artifacts—but only a very small number of instantiated strategic workstreams.

Structural scale therefore increased credibility and causal coverage without automatically creating strategic breadth.

## Did implementation narrow the original game?

Conceptually, mostly no: executive boundedness, independent actors, law/implementation separation, material causality, imperfect information, population-owned response, elections/succession, and U.S.-as-configuration were preserved.

Practically, yes: the visible production product became much narrower because one Housing-centered policy agenda and one principal causal route remained the primary instantiated content and decision surface.

Refined hypothesis:

> **The architecture did not conceptually narrow into a Housing game. The production content and decision surface remained bounded to the Housing proof for so long that the proof became indistinguishable from the product in actual play.**

Its exact wording must not be represented as repository authority unless separately supplied/preserved. The repository's action surface and acceptance criteria independently support much of its general conclusion.

Likewise, the accepted Functional Gameplay UI direction and detailed Stage 1 product specification were not found as committed docs in the frozen candidate; Stage 1 contains implementation/test/screenshots but no corresponding committed product-authority document.

## Step 3 conclusion

The implementation program successfully proved that the simulation can support a political strategy game. It did not prove that the political strategy game itself had been sufficiently designed or instantiated.

The next assessment work should therefore evaluate the frozen baseline domain-by-domain across causal integrity, breadth, generality, production reachability, legibility, strategic agency, autonomous opposition, feedback continuity, and fixture dependence before proposing new implementation work.
