# 2. What a calibration seam is

## 2.1 Seam definition

A calibration seam is the declared boundary package at which:

- inherited real-world institutional, legal, fiscal, population, geographic, material, and evidentiary state is initialized and frozen;
- generated actors/backstory have already been created and reconciled;
- live alternate-history generation assumes responsibility for future transitions;
- later real-world events cease to update the run.

The seam may include several coordinated transition times.

It need not be one universal timestamp at which every institution starts a new cycle.

## 2.2 Transition package rather than magic instant

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-CAL01] A U.S. calibration seam may be a bounded transition package containing several owner-specific constitutional, statutory, fiscal, judicial, administrative, and evidentiary boundaries. It may not pretend one date resets every process.**

The package must identify:

- primary world-state handoff time;
- straddling processes;
- later owner-resolved transition times;
- pre-seam generated-backstory occurrences;
- source/reference vintages;
- evidence available to actors at each time.

## 2.3 Seam versus player start

The calibration seam is where alternate history begins.

The player may assume office later.

Potentially:

```text
Early-2025 calibration seam
→ generated prehistory
→ 2029 player inauguration
```

or:

```text
Early-2025 calibration seam
→ longer generated prehistory
→ 2033 player inauguration
```

Step 11 does not choose between those player starts.

## 2.4 Seam versus current technical fixture

The accepted August 22, 2026 U.S. configuration remains a valuable technical and causal proof.

It does not automatically define the product's historical seam.

# 3. Hard disqualifiers

A candidate seam fails Step 11 if any of the following is unavoidable.

## 3.1 Future-real-world dependency

The package cannot be frozen without waiting for future real-world political, legal, fiscal, geographic, or data outcomes that the product schedule cannot reliably absorb.

## 3.2 Hidden post-seam import

The package requires actual post-seam real-world events to appear as inherited facts instead of generated history.

## 3.3 False actor attribution

The seam cannot support fictional/generated actors without assigning them real individuals' votes, statements, appointments, cases, promises, or other acts.

## 3.4 Unclosed institutional transition

The seam leaves office entitlement, assignment, election certification, term boundaries, or governing authority contradictory or undefined.

## 3.5 Unrepresentable straddling state

The seam requires ongoing laws, appropriations, obligations, cases, programs, measurements, contracts, elections, projects, or administrative queues to be silently reset or treated as completed.

## 3.6 Mixed-vintage laundering

The package must combine sources with different reference times or geographic/legal vintages but cannot preserve and reconcile those differences honestly.

## 3.7 Noncausal shortcut to desired player start

The candidate is called a “calibration seam” even though its state can only be produced by generating an earlier period that the candidate hides.

## 3.8 Rolling real-world overwrite

Later real-world updates would continue mutating an existing run.

## 3.9 Unresolved 2032 electoral geography

For any proposed post-2032 player start, the design refuses to generate or reconcile the 2030 census, apportionment, redistricting, and 2032 election geography.

This disqualifies that **player-start pairing**, not necessarily the earlier calibration seam itself.

# 13. Controlling Early-2025 package

## 13.1 Primary world-state handoff

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-CAL02] The leading package anchors live alternate-history generation at noon Eastern Time on January 3, 2025, when the 119th Congress begins.**

This date gives the generated world:

- new House and Senate terms;
- new chamber organization;
- a new Congress capable of counting electoral votes;
- a bounded transition interval before the executive transfer.

## 13.2 Frozen pre-seam election/backstory package

Before the January 3 handoff, the generator must have created and frozen enough synthetic history to support:

- candidate identities and eligibility;
- the 2024 presidential election;
- state results and elector appointments;
- congressional elections and office entitlement;
- campaign commitments and bounded mandate evidence;
- transition-team state;
- outgoing and incoming actor relationships where retained.

The backstory may be compressed.

It cannot be a list of desired seat totals and personalities without causal support.

## 13.3 January 6 live transition

The 119th Congress owns the electoral-count process.

The result is not treated as already completed on January 3.

If the generated election is ordinary, the count may resolve routinely.

If a generated candidate history permits a lawful dispute, the applicable procedure resolves it.

The design does not require every run to create a contested count.

## 13.4 January 20 live transition

At noon January 20:

- the outgoing presidential and vice-presidential terms end;
- the incoming terms begin if constitutional/procedural conditions are satisfied;
- office assignments and administration control transition;
- outgoing institutional records, obligations, queues, cases, and programs persist;
- private knowledge and campaign records transfer only through valid routes.

This is the first full presidential administration of forward prehistory.

## 13.5 Why Jan. 3 rather than Jan. 20

Using January 20 as the sole seam would require the package to baselineize:

- 17 days of the new Congress;
- the January 6 count;
- transition activity;
- any intervening legal, fiscal, institutional, or informational occurrences.

That is possible but less clean.

Starting January 3 lets the generator own those transitions directly.

## 13.6 Why not November 5

Starting on Election Day creates additional campaign and outgoing-Congress simulation burden without materially improving the Living Country foundation.

The 2024 election remains causally real through generated backstory and retained occurrences.

# 14. Three-clock source policy

## 14.1 World-state seam

The primary dynamic handoff is January 3, 2025, noon ET.

## 14.2 Source-compilation cutoff

**[UQ]** Step 11 does not fix the exact source-compilation cutoff.

It requires a versioned package freeze before world generation and release.

A source may be published after January 3 and still be admissible for a pre-seam reference period when Step 10's retrospective-source rules are satisfied.

## 14.3 Actor-knowledge time

Actors receive only information available through valid in-world channels at the applicable time.

Examples:

- the December 2024 Employment Situation was released January 10, 2025;
- the December 2024 CPI was released January 15, 2025;
- the advance estimate for fourth-quarter/year-2024 GDP was scheduled for January 30, 2025;
- the 2024 ACS 1-year estimates were released in September 2025;
- the 2020–2024 ACS 5-year estimates were released in January 2026.

A later source package may use those products to improve hidden baseline estimation of their declared reference periods.

The outgoing administration, incoming transition team, Congress, media, and public at the start may not possess them before their actual release or another valid earlier estimate.

## 14.4 Reference-period gate

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-CAL03] A post-seam-published source may inform hidden pre-seam state only when its reference period, transformation, and leakage limits are declared. It may not import a completed post-seam political, legal, fiscal, institutional, material, or informational outcome into the generated world.**

## 14.5 Source-freeze rule

Once the scenario package is frozen and a candidate world is generated:

- later real revisions create a new package version if adopted;
- existing runs remain pinned;
- no rolling source update modifies the run;
- actor knowledge remains tied to in-world release and receipt chronology.

# 17. Fiscal and legal cleanliness assessment

## 17.1 No January seam aligns with fiscal year

The federal fiscal year runs October 1 through September 30.

January 2021, 2025, 2027, and 2029 are all mid-fiscal-year.

Therefore “fiscal cleanliness” cannot distinguish these January candidates by reset alignment.

The correct criterion is reconstructability of:

- opening balances;
- authority;
- obligations;
- receipts/outlays;
- debt;
- program execution;
- budget process and deadlines.

## 17.2 Legal state is reconstructable by vintage

The Federal Register provides daily official rules, proposed rules, notices, and presidential documents.

The annual CFR provides dated codification vintages.

The eCFR exposes continuously updated and historical regulatory text while remaining distinct from the official annual edition.

This makes an early-2025 legal package feasible, though not trivial.

## 17.3 Ongoing cases are expected

The Supreme Court term begins on the first Monday in October and is underway in January.

A January seam must inherit active cases and lower-court proceedings.

This is not a defect.

It is part of the Living Country.
