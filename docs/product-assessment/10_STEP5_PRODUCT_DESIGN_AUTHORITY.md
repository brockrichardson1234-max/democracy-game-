# 2. Accepted game definition

The game is a systemic United States presidential strategy game organized around:

> **Presidential prioritization under concurrent institutional pressure in a dated, autonomous, persistent political world.**

The player leads the strategic decision surface of a constitutionally and institutionally bounded executive administration.

The country, Congress, states, courts, agencies, political organizations, parties/factions, population, material domains, information environment, elections, and external actors continue to pursue their own processes and objectives whether or not the President is focused on them.

The player's core responsibility is:

> **Decide what deserves presidential intervention, what is delegated, what waits, what compromise is acceptable, and what opportunity is sacrificed while the country continues moving.**

---

# 3. Accepted recurring gameplay loop

```text
inherit and observe a moving country
→ triage concurrent workstreams and presidential attention
→ select objective, priority, and governing route
→ bargain, commit, appoint, direct, delegate,
  communicate, litigate, or deliberately wait
→ advance dated world while autonomous owners continue
→ receive bounded information and escalation
→ act, delegate, or accept expiration/default
→ observe later legal, fiscal, material, political,
  informational, and electoral consequences
→ reprioritize
```

A policy or conflict may pass through this loop repeatedly. The player does not finish one complete issue pipeline before another issue can matter.

---

# 4. Accepted concurrency and opportunity-cost model

## 4.1 Soft concurrency

The player may attempt many initiatives simultaneously. The game must not block a fifth initiative through a universal slot count merely because a configured maximum was reached.

## 4.2 Concrete contention

Opportunity cost arises through owner-specific contention such as:

- congressional committee and floor opportunities;
- leadership scheduling and priorities;
- White House staff assignments and deadlines;
- agency and OMB queues;
- presidential calendar conflicts;
- commitments and remembered credibility;
- fiscal authority, obligations, receipts, and debt-service pressure;
- legal authority and deadlines;
- state participation;
- communication exposure and competing claims;
- election timing;
- crisis displacement.

## 4.3 Universal-resource prohibition

No universal or cross-domain aggregate Political Capital, Presidential Capacity, attention, agenda-slot, or overextension value may resolve unrelated institutional outcomes.

Owner-scoped calendars, assignments, queues, throughput, dates, personnel, dollars, and legal limits are permitted when their ownership, competing uses, and consequences remain visible and causal.

---

# 5. Accepted workstream model

A workstream is a player-facing coordination projection over canonical state.

It may summarize:

- governing objective;
- responsible administration lead;
- active institutional owner;
- status and history;
- relevant actors;
- deadlines and opportunities;
- legal/fiscal/administrative constraints;
- commitments;
- condition trends;
- implementation;
- public information and political interpretation;
- what can proceed autonomously;
- what specifically requires presidential action.

A workstream never owns or directly mutates votes, laws, money, court rulings, state decisions, material conditions, beliefs, or election results.

---

# 6. Accepted attention, delegation, and inaction model

## 6.1 Information-bounded attention

A presidential attention item must follow:

```text
canonical occurrence
→ observable artifact or communication
→ administration receipt
→ bounded interpretation and escalation
→ presidential decision surface or review
```

Canonical truth may not interrupt the player before the administration has a valid way to know or infer it.

Every attention packet must identify:

- who or what nominated it;
- the received artifact/communication;
- what the administration knows;
- uncertainty;
- why presidential involvement is requested;
- deadline/expiration where applicable;
- what happens without presidential action.

## 6.2 Delegation

Delegation requires:

- objective;
- responsible owner;
- available authority;
- priorities/constraints;
- reporting thresholds;
- deadlines;
- decisions the subordinate owner may make independently.

Delegation is not automatic quest completion.

## 6.3 Inaction

No decision may freeze the world indefinitely.

Every decision surface has an expiration or default, such as:

- standing policy continuing;
- delegated staff/agency action;
- an offer lapsing;
- a legal filing expiring;
- another actor proceeding;
- no public response;
- an existing rule remaining operative.

Deliberate inaction is a strategic choice with canonical consequences.

# 7. Accepted situation-to-decision provenance

Decision discovery, option derivation, and outcome resolution remain separate.

## 7.1 Discovery

```text
owner changes state or autonomous actor acts
→ observable record exists
→ administration receives it
→ bounded actor evaluates authority, delegation,
  objectives, deadlines, and uncertainty
→ President is asked or player proactively opens review
```

## 7.2 Option derivation

Each option must have provenance for:

- authority;
- institutional route;
- target;
- timing;
- prerequisites;
- known constraints;
- known uncertainty;
- default/inaction.

No option exists merely to create a balanced menu.

## 7.3 Resolution

The presidential attempt becomes a canonical record. Relevant lawmakers, agencies, courts, states, organizations, populations, material domains, and other actors resolve their own portions independently.

The presidential attempt may fail, partly succeed, be transformed, or create consequences whose later political interpretation differs from the material result.

---

# 8. Accepted autonomous-actor model

Important political actors must initiate action from their own bounded state rather than functioning primarily as responders to the President.

A load-bearing non-player initiative follows:

```text
actor/institution observes or receives bounded state
→ interprets through role, jurisdiction, objectives,
  constituency, beliefs, relationships, commitments,
  risk, and opportunities
→ derives institutionally available attempts
→ chooses action, delay, delegation, or no action
→ records attempt canonically
→ target/procedure resolves independently
→ administration learns through valid channels
```

This applies to:

- lawmakers, committees, and congressional leadership;
- governors and state coalitions;
- parties, factions, and organizations;
- opposition actors;
- agencies and institutions;
- courts through their legal processes;
- external actors when modeled.

No global dramatic director may insert an initiative because the term needs pressure.

Senior staff likewise cannot hide such a director: discretionary escalation requires role, receipt, delegated basis, assessment, confidence, uncertainty, forecasted consequence, and a recorded escalation/non-escalation.

---

# 9. Accepted systemic-content doctrine

The game is systemic/generative rather than primarily authored political narrative.

The preferred direction is:

```text
world and institutional state
→ actor observation and autonomous action
→ information, measurement, reporting, and claims
→ bounded presidential situation
→ presidential attempt or inaction
→ autonomous resolution
→ persistent consequences
```

Configured primitives and bounded shock families are legitimate.

A hidden event/story director may not select unemployment, scandals, hurricanes, congressional offers, or faction revolts merely because the presidency needs drama.

Illustrative traces and world-shape exemplars remain design tools, not proof that a generator already produces them.

---

# 10. Accepted political issue and coalition concepts

## 10.1 Domains, conditions, and issues

- **Durable domains** exist whether politics is focused on them or not.
- **Conditions** are canonical state within those domains.
- **Political issues** are emergent, perspectival projections formed through conditions, trends, exposure, institutional conflict, actor attention, organization activity, information, claims, attribution, salience, and opportunity.

There is no canonical Top Five Issues object whose ranking causes actors to care.

## 10.2 Distinct coalition concepts

The design distinguishes:

- electoral coalition;
- party coalition;
- issue-specific governing coalition;
- current public-support coalition.

These may overlap but do not collapse into one support or mandate meter.

---

# 11. Accepted political grounding and player setup concept

The first product remains United States-first, with real U.S. institutions and recognizable major-party structure while political officeholders, histories, factions, relationships, and administrations may be generated.

The standard governing setup may construct which plausible victorious campaign history the player inhabited.

It must:

- begin from viable generated nominee histories;
- maintain at least one plausible victorious history after every finalized setup choice;
- require revision when a combination eliminates the final path;
- preserve opponent, electorate, and prehistory rather than manufacture a miracle;
- let choices alter commitments, coalition composition, margin, vulnerabilities, relationships, and governing expectations;
- resolve House and Senate elections separately.

A future loss-capable full campaign is outside this authority.

---

# 12. Accepted time model

The world retains exact dated deterministic time.

The leading player-facing relationship is accepted:

> **Daily dated continuity plus event-driven presidential attention.**

The player does not take one turn per calendar day.

Simulation pauses for active deliberation where required. The default advance behavior may run routine days and autonomous processes until a valid decision, expiring opportunity, authority-required action, major bounded review, or player-requested threshold reaches the President.

Secondary time controls and final UX remain unresolved.

Attention density must emerge from observable conflicts and deadlines rather than a fixed monthly quota.

No exact four-year decision count is accepted.

---

# 13. Accepted term continuity and evaluation

The first four-year term must form a coherent arc, but reelection may continue into a second term in the same world.

Losing ends the player's presidential ControlBinding at lawful transfer while the world and history persist.

The Presidency receives a multidimensional historical record rather than one authoritative score.

The record may separately show:

- campaign commitments;
- governing acts;
- material/social condition trajectories;
- attribution uncertainty;
- implementation inheritance;
- fiscal inheritance;
- institutional changes and disputes;
- coalition history;
- election results;
- crisis and national-security record;
- unfinished work.

Different actors may interpret the same record differently.

---

# 14. National security and foreign affairs

National security and foreign affairs remain part of the eventual presidential fantasy and may create domestic fiscal, legal, material, informational, and political consequences.

Domestic governing remains the initial design center.

No depth, external-world model, conflict system, or Early Access scope is accepted here.
