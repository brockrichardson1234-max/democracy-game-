# 2. Direct comparison: B1 versus B2

The original assessment identified early 2025 correctly but did not adequately compare two legitimate subvariants.

This repair performs that comparison.

## 2.1 B1 — January 3 live handoff

Structure:

```text
frozen generated 2024 election/backstory
→ January 3, noon ET live handoff
→ new Congress begins under outgoing President
→ January 6 electoral count resolves live
→ January 3–20 outgoing-administration and transition activity resolves live
→ January 20 executive transfer
→ first complete generated presidential term begins
```

### Strengths

- the new Congress begins directly inside live generated prehistory;
- January 6 and January 20 are ordinary future transitions rather than pre-seam records;
- every consequential act during the 17-day interval can emerge through the same forward-generation process;
- the package minimizes the amount of post-election transition history classified as generated backstory.

### Burdens

- live generated prehistory begins under the outgoing administration rather than the administration whose full term supplies the first major historical arc;
- the initial live interval is only 17 days and crosses a change in presidential office, control, White House staff, Cabinet leadership, agency political leadership, access, private knowledge, transition-team status, and administration priorities;
- the generator must instantiate and route an outgoing administration, incoming transition apparatus, newly seated Congress, pending electoral count, and January 20 transfer before reaching its first complete presidential term;
- the first forward-generation checkpoint is a transition fragment rather than a stable administration boundary;
- it complicates reasoning about which initial administration records, queues, appointments, policy objectives, and private political relationships belong to the outgoing administration versus the incoming administration;
- no currently identified later product consumer requires every routine January 3–20 act to be simulated live rather than retained as bounded generated transition history.

## 2.2 B2 — January 20 live handoff

Structure:

```text
frozen generated 2024 election/backstory
→ January 3 new Congress begins as generated pre-seam transition history
→ January 6 electoral count resolves as generated pre-seam transition history
→ January 3–20 load-bearing congressional/transition activity retained
→ January 20, noon ET live handoff
→ incoming generated administration begins complete first term
```

### Strengths

- forward-generated prehistory begins at the constitutional start of the first complete presidential administration;
- the first live administration has one coherent President, Vice President, White House, Cabinet/acting structure, control environment, and governing mandate;
- the two potential pre-player presidential terms become cleanly comparable term-length arcs;
- January 3 congressional legitimacy and January 6 presidential entitlement still possess exact generated occurrence history;
- any consequential January 3–20 action can still be retained with actor, authority, receipt, procedure, and consequence provenance;
- routine transition detail can be compressed without losing later-required rights, obligations, knowledge, or relationships;
- the live generator avoids a short outgoing-administration segment whose main purpose is to hand off immediately;
- the selected seam better matches the presidency-centered product while preserving Congress as independently seated before the President;
- a later 2029 player-start fallback becomes exactly one complete generated presidential term after the live seam;
- a later 2033 candidate becomes exactly two complete generated presidential terms after the live seam.

### Burdens

- January 3–20 history must be generated and frozen before the live handoff;
- the package must explicitly retain congressional organization, electoral-count, transition, information, legal, fiscal, and other load-bearing occurrences from that interval;
- any consequential variation in those 17 days must be generated through the backstory/transition package rather than discovered after the live handoff;
- an invalid or changed transition package requires regeneration or migration before forward prehistory begins.

## 2.3 Current-state ownership comparison

Both variants can produce a valid January 20 current state.

B1 reaches it through 17 days of live generated transitions.

B2 initializes it from:

- inherited pre-January-3 baseline facts;
- frozen generated 2024 election/backstory;
- generated January 3–20 occurrence history;
- owner-reconciled January 20 current state.

Under Step 10, B2 is valid because generated backstory and transition history may contain occurrence-required facts when they are produced before forward generation, frozen, and reconciled.

B2 does not replace occurrences with traits.

## 2.4 Office and election legitimacy comparison

Both variants require:

- generated 2024 presidential and congressional elections;
- state results and elector appointment;
- January 3 congressional office entitlement/assignment;
- January 6 electoral count;
- January 20 presidential entitlement/assignment;
- separate House, Senate, presidential, electoral, certification, assignment, and control facts.

B2 retains those as generated pre-seam occurrences.

Therefore B1 has no unique legitimacy advantage.

## 2.5 Administration and control comparison

B1 requires a live outgoing administration and live incoming transition apparatus before the first complete generated administration.

B2 begins live forward prehistory after:

- outgoing office tenure has ended;
- incoming entitlement has resolved;
- office assignment has changed;
- transition records and permitted knowledge transfers have resolved;
- inherited institutional queues and obligations have been reconciled;
- new administration objectives and appointments remain available for forward resolution.

B2 is cleaner for the accepted internal-administration contract because the live period begins with one active presidential administration rather than a near-immediate succession boundary.

## 2.6 Congress and executive alignment comparison

B1 gives Congress 17 live days before executive transfer.

B2 gives Congress the same historical 17-day interval but freezes it before forward prehistory.

The 119th Congress remains:

- separately elected;
- separately seated;
- capable of organizing and acting;
- the owner of the January 6 count;
- independent of the incoming President.

B2 does not make Congress begin on January 20.

It only moves the primary live history handoff to the executive-term boundary.

## 2.7 Open-process comparison

Both variants inherit ongoing:

- fiscal authority, obligations, payments, debt, and budget work;
- cases, orders, rules, and legal deadlines;
- grants, contracts, programs, queues, and investigations;
- material and population processes;
- measurements, releases, journalism, and organization activity.

B2 must reconcile any changes to those processes between January 3 and January 20.

That is a bounded 17-day generated transition problem, not an argument for treating the interval as nonexistent.

## 2.8 Actor-knowledge comparison

Both variants preserve exact release, receipt, access, memory, and transition chronology.

B2 cannot grant the incoming administration knowledge merely because a report existed before January 20.

It must distinguish:

- public information;
- outgoing institutional records;
- transferable agency/office records;
- transition-team access;
- private outgoing-administration knowledge;
- classified, privileged, or restricted information;
- information released after January 20.

No epistemic advantage belongs automatically to either variant.

## 2.9 Later explanation comparison

B2 produces a cleaner explanation:

> The 119th Congress took office on January 3. It counted the generated electoral votes on January 6. Transition activity and any consequential congressional acts were retained. The incoming administration assumed office on January 20, when forward generated prehistory began.

This remains causally complete and avoids implying that January 20 created Congress.

## 2.10 Named-consumer test

No currently accepted product contract requires routine January 3–20 activity to be simulated as open-ended forward history.

The later product may consume:

- congressional organization and leadership;
- committee and chamber assignment;
- electoral count and entitlement;
- outgoing/incoming transition records;
- transferred institutional queues and obligations;
- January releases and receipts;
- consequential promises, contacts, hearings, votes, legal acts, or crises if generated.

B2 can retain all of those through exact occurrences or bounded transition bundles.

The 17-day interval does not need to remain live merely to prove that it existed.

# 4. Calibration controls versus generated election outcomes

## 4.1 Core distinction

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-CALR02] Every political aggregate or outcome fixed before synthetic 2024 election generation must be declared as an external calibration control or scenario constraint. It may not later be presented as an independently emergent validation of the generator.**

## 4.2 Permitted generation families

A later design may choose among or combine declared approaches such as:

### Fully generated within plausibility constraints

The generator resolves candidates, campaigns, state outcomes, Congress, and mandate evidence from configured institutions and conditions without fixing the final aggregate result.

### Conditioned generation

The package declares some controls before generation, such as:

- incoming President's party;
- broad national margin range;
- chamber-control ranges;
- state-result plausibility constraints;
- divided/unified government family.

The generator constructs a compatible history.

### Aggregate-calibrated synthetic election

The package fixes selected real or configured aggregate outcomes while replacing individual politicians and generating a compatible campaign and actor history.

The result is calibrated, not independently predicted.

### Scenario-family selection

The product offers several separately declared starting families, each with its own political controls and generated history.

## 4.3 Control declaration

A fixed control identifies:

- source or design authority;
- exact semantic meaning;
- time and geography;
- support/status;
- whether real aggregate, modeled range, or authored scenario constraint;
- why it is fixed;
- what remains autonomous;
- what claims may and may not be made about generation.

## 4.4 No double use as proof

Forbidden:

```text
configure incoming party and chamber balance
→ generate compatible election
→ cite compatible result as proof
  that election simulation independently produced them
```

The generator may still be tested on:

- internal state consistency;
- feasible campaign/election path;
- geographic and turnout reconciliation;
- actor/candidate eligibility;
- mandate and coalition plausibility;
- variation within controls;
- no miraculous or contradictory result.

## 4.5 No impossible microhistory

Aggregate controls do not license impossible individual histories.

State outcomes, candidate histories, House/Senate results, elector appointments, and office assignments must reconcile within the declared controls.

A desired chamber balance cannot silently backsolve mutually impossible district or Senate contests.

# 5. Retrospective-source interval admissibility

## 5.1 Core invariant

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-CALR03] A source published after the January 20 live seam may initialize or refine hidden opening state only for the portion of its reference universe and interval that is legitimately attributable to pre-seam state under a declared transformation. It may not import realized post-seam outcomes into the opening root.**

## 5.2 Reference-interval classes

A later source may concern an interval that:

1. ends before January 20;
2. straddles January 20;
3. begins on or after January 20;
4. aggregates several years or changing universes;
5. provides a later revision to an earlier reference period.

These require different treatment.

## 5.3 Pre-seam interval

A later-published source whose reference period ends before the seam may inform hidden opening state when:

- reference period and publication time are retained;
- the source does not disclose post-seam actor decisions or outcomes;
- actor knowledge remains governed by actual release/receipt;
- transformation from period estimate to seam-time state is explicit where needed.

## 5.4 Straddling interval

A source whose reference interval straddles January 20 may not be imported wholesale as an opening fact.

Permitted treatments include:

- decomposing the pre-seam portion when support exists;
- using a model with declared assumptions and uncertainty;
- using bounds;
- using the source only for later measurement validation;
- rejecting it for opening initialization.

## 5.5 Post-seam interval

A source measuring outcomes entirely after January 20 belongs to generated history or later evidence.

It cannot initialize the opening world.

## 5.6 Multi-year estimates

A multi-year estimate is a period estimate under its declared methodology and geography.

It is not an exact January 20 point-in-time observation.

It may support a modeled opening allocation or distribution only with explicit transformation and uncertainty.

## 5.7 Revision

A later revision to a pre-seam reference period may improve the developers' estimate of hidden opening state.

It does not become earlier actor knowledge or rewrite what actors received before the revision.

## 5.8 Political/legal/fiscal exclusion

Later sources may never be used to import completed post-seam:

- elections;
- appointments;
- laws;
- court results;
- appropriations;
- payments;
- agency actions;
- program outcomes;
- political campaigns;
- media/public developments;
- crises.

Their reference labels do not override their actual temporal content.
