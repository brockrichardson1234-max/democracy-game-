# 2. R10-01 — BLOCKING: generated-backstory roots lack a closed admissibility and generation-order contract

## 2.1 The gap

The candidate defines generated backstory as synthetic pre-seam or seam-time state used to reconcile actors, relationships, organizations, reputations, careers, and contextual history.

It also states:

- a relationship may begin as a generated baseline condition when the product does not consume its detailed origin;
- specific betrayals, favors, promises, votes, appointments, scandals, or alliances require supporting occurrences when consumed;
- forward-generated history may not backfill a desired opening story.

Those are good constraints, but they do not fully close the classification boundary.

The generator can still decide that the product consumes only the **current relationship**, not the detailed origin, and therefore assert almost any desired political state as generated backstory.

## 2.2 Exploit

A 2033 opening generator wants:

- a hostile Senate faction;
- three senators indebted to one governor;
- a party reputation for fiscal irresponsibility;
- an organization expecting repayment for campaign support;
- a Cabinet nominee carrying a remembered betrayal;
- a public belief that one party abandoned industrial regions.

Instead of generating the relevant post-divergence elections, promises, votes, appointments, communications, program failures, evidence, exposure, and recipient updating, it does this:

```text
relationship = generated-backstory root
commitment = generated-backstory root
party reputation = generated-backstory root
actor memory = generated-backstory root
public issue association = generated-backstory root
```

Every fact has a declared root.

The opening state may reconcile.

No false real-world attribution occurs.

Yet the generated 2025–2032 history has been bypassed.

The backstory root has become a teleological patch layer.

## 2.3 Temporal exploit

The candidate also does not state strongly enough that generated-backstory roots must be fixed before the forward-generated interval begins.

A generator could run most of prehistory, inspect the desired or produced opening state, then add a new `pre-seam` rivalry, reputation, or promise to explain a current coalition.

The root is dated earlier, but the generation act happened later and was selected because of downstream state.

That is retroactive fabrication even if the generated-world timestamp looks valid.

## 2.4 Why this is blocking

The contract's core claim is that post-divergence political and material history must arise through owner-resolved causes rather than desired-opening backfill.

That claim fails if `generated backstory` can absorb any politically useful state for which the generator chooses not to retain an origin.

The loophole would permit:

- unexplained coalition structure;
- invented actor memory;
- commitments without offers or receipt;
- public reputation without evidence or recipient history;
- party/faction development without political action;
- office legitimacy without an election or appointment route;
- post-divergence state relabeled as pre-seam context.

This fails gate items 2, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, and 18.

## 2.5 Required repair

The repaired contract must add a closed admissibility rule.

At minimum:

### A. Backstory generation order

Generated backstory is created, reconciled, versioned, and frozen before forward-generated prehistory begins.

It may not be added or reclassified after observing forward-generated outcomes in order to repair or enrich them.

### B. Temporal scope

A generated-backstory root may cover only facts whose effective origin is at or before the configured divergence/prehistory boundary assigned to that root class.

Any load-bearing fact created or materially changed after that boundary belongs to forward-generated prehistory or player-era history.

### C. Admissible root facts

A bare generated-backstory root may terminate causal recursion for synthetic background facts whose detailed origin no supported consumer requires, such as bounded identity, education/profession, general ideological disposition, non-event-specific affinity, or broad prior reputation when its synthetic and bounded status is explicit.

### D. Occurrence-required facts

A current fact requires a compressed or full generated occurrence/activity chain when it semantically asserts or depends on a particular:

- election, appointment, office transfer, vote, law, ruling, payment, program, or institutional act;
- promise, commitment, threat, bargain, betrayal, favor, endorsement, or quid-like expectation;
- relationship change caused by identifiable conduct;
- public reputation or party competence/association produced by historical performance, evidence, communication, or audience response;
- actor memory of a specific event;
- organization right, obligation, resource, or expectation;
- legal, fiscal, material, evidentiary, or procedural consequence.

### E. Root-admission ledger

Every load-bearing generated-backstory fact must declare:

- why it is admissible as a root rather than an occurrence chain;
- effective interval;
- generation order/version;
- support and knowledge status;
- consumers;
- what exact historical claims are prohibited;
- whether later deepening can remain modeled/bounded rather than fabricate exact origin.

### F. No relabeling

A forward-generated or player-era fact may not be reclassified as generated backstory to satisfy reconciliation or compression.

# 3. C10-02 — world seam, source-compilation cutoff, and actor-knowledge time must be separated

The candidate refers to a divergence boundary and source-cutoff policy, but later calibration can require three different times:

1. **world-state/divergence seam**
   - when the alternate world begins its generated continuation;
2. **external source-compilation cutoff**
   - the latest real-world publication/revision the scenario author used to estimate or construct the baseline;
3. **in-world actor-knowledge availability**
   - what actors could actually have received by the seam or later dates.

These times need not be identical.

Example:

- world seam: January 2025;
- a benchmark revision published in 2026 improves the developer's estimate of an economic condition as of late 2024;
- actors in January 2025 could not possess that 2026 revision.

A later calibration package may use retrospective evidence to improve hidden opening state if declared and if it does not import post-seam occurrences or outcomes.

It may not grant that later evidence to historical actors or use it to determine post-seam decisions.

Required clarification:

- declare all applicable cutoffs/times;
- preserve source publication and reference periods;
- distinguish retrospective baseline estimation from in-world evidence availability;
- prohibit future-outcome leakage;
- ensure `historically closed` does not ambiguously mean all three clocks are the same.

# 4. C10-03 — composite current facts require contribution/root coverage, not one convenient ancestor

`LC-HIST01` says every load-bearing fact must trace to at least one root.

For a simple identity fact, one root may be enough.

For a composite stock, balance, population total, debt position, program caseload, or material condition, `at least one root` could be read too weakly.

Exploit:

```text
2033 debt
→ references one inherited opening balance root
```

while generated deficits, repayments, revaluations, and residuals are omitted.

Required clarification:

A composite fact must reconcile across the complete contribution basis required by its owner:

```text
opening stock/root
+ admitted inflows
- admitted outflows
± revaluation/reclassification
± modeled residual/uncertainty
= current stock
```

The root need not enumerate every microscopic cause.

It must cover the full semantic basis at the accepted resolution, including declared residuals and unknowns.

Step 6 contribution-lineage and accounting rules remain controlling.

# 5. C10-04 — compressed historical bundles cannot masquerade as one exact occurrence

The candidate correctly says summaries are projections and cannot replace retained occurrence identities.

The final authority should additionally distinguish:

- one canonical occurrence;
- a compressed interval/process bundle containing several occurrences;
- an aggregate count/flow;
- a historical summary projection.

A statement such as:

```text
Administration A conducted 47 state consultations during 2029
```

may be a supported aggregate or compressed bundle.

It is not one consultation occurrence and cannot be used as the exact referent for:

- a particular senator's memory;
- a legal notice;
- a specific commitment;
- one organization's receipt;
- a deadline;
- a causal claim about one decision.

When later gameplay requires a specific occurrence, that occurrence must be retained or remain modeled/bounded/unsupported.

A summary cannot be unpacked into invented exact events.

# 6. C10-05 — preselection repair must not mutate owner outcomes by editorial fiat

The candidate states that worlds failing reconciliation are rejected or explicitly repaired before canonical selection.

`Repaired` is potentially too broad.

A generator could:

```text
Congress result conflicts with office assignment
→ edit office assignment
```

or:

```text
fiscal total does not reconcile
→ alter one program outlay
```

without rerunning or recording the owner process.

Required clarification:

Before canonical selection, a failed generated world may be:

- rejected and regenerated;
- resumed from a valid checkpoint through owner-resolved transitions;
- migrated through an explicit generation repair that preserves owner authority, identities, times, and occurrence lineage;
- marked unresolved when the accepted abstraction permits.

It may not be patched by directly editing a consequential owner output while leaving the incompatible history intact.

Any correction that changes generated history creates a new candidate world/version. The abandoned candidate remains noncanonical developer evidence.
