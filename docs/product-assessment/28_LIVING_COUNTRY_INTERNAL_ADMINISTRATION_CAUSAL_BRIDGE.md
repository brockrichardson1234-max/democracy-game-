# 1. Executive design verdict

## 1.1 Central answer

**[DI]** The internal administration should be represented as:

> **A routed federation of persistent offices, institutions, officeholders, records, assignments, queues, delegations, and decision processes—not one staff actor, one Cabinet hive mind, one competence score, or one presidential inbox.**

Its product purpose is to transform the enormous, partially observed country into a bounded presidential decision surface while allowing most government work to proceed without presidential clicks.

The administration must support this causal chain:

```text
country, institution, actor, legal, fiscal, or external state changes
→ an observable record, communication, direct experience, or scheduled duty exists
→ one or more administration offices legitimately receive it
→ responsible offices investigate, coordinate, forecast, disagree, act, or delegate
→ routine matters resolve under valid authority and standing instructions
→ only unresolved presidential authority, cross-office conflict,
  major commitment, expiring opportunity, or configured reporting threshold escalates
→ a decision package identifies sources, uncertainty, options, dissent,
  deadlines, defaults, and independent resolvers
→ President acts, delegates, asks for revision, or deliberately does nothing
→ offices, agencies, Congress, states, courts, firms, organizations,
  media, material domains, and other owners resolve their portions
→ records, queues, relationships, and consequences persist
```

The administration is therefore neither:

- an invisible automation bonus;
- nor a second player character that makes every national decision;
- nor a list of advisers who supply colored modifiers;
- nor a daily task-management game.

## 1.2 Core distinction

The controlling design distinction is:

> **The office persists; the officeholder interprets and acts.**

An office or institution can own:

- mandate and jurisdiction;
- institutional identity;
- standing procedures;
- access rights attached to role;
- official records;
- active assignments and queues;
- delegated authority;
- deadlines and obligations;
- owner-scoped staff, budget, and operational capabilities.

An officeholder can own:

- private beliefs and uncertainty;
- interpretation of evidence;
- priorities within discretion;
- relationships and trust;
- management choices;
- willingness to escalate, dissent, delay, comply, resist, resign, or take risk;
- actor memory not converted into an institutional record.

Turnover changes the interpreter and decision-maker. It does not automatically erase the office, its lawful duties, records, queues, deadlines, delegations, or unresolved obligations.

## 1.3 Functional closure, not one frozen org chart

**[DI]** The game should require a **functional closure set** rather than one immutable list of White House office names.

Every playable administration must possess valid ownership and routing for the following functions when relevant:

1. presidential operations, access, scheduling, and cross-office coordination;
2. presidential-paper and decision-package assembly;
3. domestic-policy coordination;
4. economic-policy and empirical analysis;
5. national-security and external-affairs coordination;
6. budget, management, and regulatory/fiscal review;
7. legal advice, presidential-action review, and executive-branch legal coordination;
8. congressional liaison;
9. state, local, tribal, territorial, and intergovernmental liaison;
10. external stakeholder/public engagement;
11. communications and press strategy;
12. official political/coalition assessment kept distinct from campaign operations;
13. presidential personnel, nominations, appointments, and vacancy management;
14. Cabinet and agency leadership;
15. career institutional continuity and program operations;
16. presidential and agency records appropriate to their legal owners.

One administration may place several functions under one deputy or office. Another may split them. A later country configuration may use different names.

The organization is valid only if every load-bearing function has:

- one declared current owner or coordinated ownership rule;
- a decision locus;
- an information route;
- authority and delegation semantics;
- a queue or routine process where needed;
- records and succession behavior;
- no duplicated resources or actions.

## 1.4 Product deletion rule

A staff office, title, actor, queue, or record family should be retained only when deleting it would make at least one supported claim materially less coherent by removing:

- a distinct information route;
- a real authority or legal-review boundary;
- a consequential assignment or resource collision;
- a persistent relationship or bargaining channel;
- a meaningful disagreement;
- a delegation boundary;
- a nomination, confirmation, vacancy, or succession path;
- a presidential decision that cannot be represented honestly otherwise.

Historical realism alone is not enough to require individual simulation.

# 3. Administration taxonomy and ownership

## 3.1 Administration

**[DI]** An administration is a time-bounded governing configuration associated with the President, Vice President, appointed leadership, White House organization, delegations, priorities, and records.

It may reference:

- the President and Vice President;
- White House/EOP offices and assignments;
- Cabinet and agency leadership assignments;
- standing delegations and instructions;
- administration-wide objectives and commitments;
- current organization topology;
- official decision and coordination records.

The administration is not one autonomous actor and does not own every action of its members or agencies.

## 3.2 Office

An office is a persistent constituted role or organizational function with a declared mandate, scope, records, and possible authority.

Examples:

- President;
- Vice President;
- Chief of Staff;
- Staff Secretary or executive secretariat;
- Legislative Affairs lead;
- OMB Director;
- White House Counsel;
- Cabinet secretary;
- agency administrator;
- Press Secretary.

The office remains distinct from its holder.

## 3.3 Office assignment

An office assignment is the dated relationship placing an actor into an office.

Assignment state may include:

- nominated;
- pending confirmation;
- confirmed;
- commissioned or appointed;
- acting;
- delegated;
- recessed or another later lawful route if supported;
- resigned;
- removed;
- expired;
- superseded;
- vacant.

These statuses do not all grant the same authority, access, tenure, or political legitimacy.

## 3.4 White House office versus executive agency

A White House/EOP office primarily advises, coordinates, routes, schedules, reviews, communicates, or supports presidential action according to its accepted function.

An executive department or agency owns program operations, statutory duties, regulatory or enforcement processes, field capability, records, and institutional continuity appropriate to its legal role.

A White House office may coordinate an agency. It does not automatically absorb the agency’s authority, staff, program state, or material outcomes.

## 3.5 Political leadership versus career institution

A department or agency may contain:

- politically appointed leadership;
- acting leadership;
- deputies and subordinate appointed offices;
- career executives and managers;
- career professional and operational units;
- inspector-general or independent components where applicable;
- standing procedures and records.

The political head may set priorities within authority, make delegated decisions, allocate owner-scoped resources, and advise the President.

Career units preserve institutional knowledge, legal duties, ongoing cases, operational capacity, and records across turnover. They are not automatically politically neutral in every behavior, perfectly competent, or immune from leadership—but neither are they erased by a new administration.

## 3.6 Coordination body

A council, task force, working group, or interagency process is a coordination relationship or temporary institution with:

- mandate;
- chair;
- membership;
- information access;
- delegated scope;
- assignments;
- records;
- reporting route;
- effective interval or sunset.

It gains no authority merely because it exists. Its members retain their own institutional ownership.

## 3.7 Decision package and briefing packet

A decision package is a player-facing and institution-facing projection assembled from canonical artifacts, assessments, disagreements, options, deadlines, and defaults.

It does not own:

- underlying truth;
- legal authority;
- fiscal state;
- agency capability;
- actor positions;
- presidential choice;
- downstream outcome.

Its assembly and clearance status may be owned by an executive-secretariat process.

# 4. The office persists; the officeholder interprets and acts

## 4.1 Office-owned continuity

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM01]** Every persistent office or institution must distinguish its continuing state from holder-private state.

Office/institution continuity may include:

- mandate and jurisdiction;
- official records and correspondence;
- active assignments;
- queues and deadlines;
- lawful delegations that survive according to their terms;
- standing instructions and procedures;
- owner-scoped resources;
- institutional access rights;
- unresolved obligations;
- official relationships and coordination memberships.

## 4.2 Holder-owned interpretation

The holder may own:

- private beliefs and forecasts;
- relationships and trust;
- judgment;
- leadership priorities within lawful discretion;
- willingness to dissent or escalate;
- private notes and memories according to applicable record rules;
- management style as reflected in concrete decisions;
- risk tolerance and political ambition.

A holder does not own the office’s legal authority as personal property.

## 4.3 Turnover

When an officeholder changes:

- the office persists;
- official records remain according to their owner and access rules;
- active assignments and deadlines remain unless validly changed;
- delegations continue, expire, revoke, or require reissuance according to their own terms;
- the incoming holder receives only records and access legitimately available;
- private relationships, beliefs, source trust, and undisclosed knowledge do not automatically transfer;
- the new holder may reprioritize discretionary queues through valid acts;
- prior occurrences are not rewritten.

## 4.4 No automatic competence reset

A change in holder can alter task performance through actual differences in:

- relevant experience;
- substantive understanding;
- relationships;
- management decisions;
- tolerance for uncertainty;
- willingness to use career expertise;
- delegation choices;
- staffing and resource allocation.

It cannot directly apply a universal `OfficeEfficiency +12%` modifier.

# 5. Minimum functional closure set

## 5.1 Closure, grouping, and naming

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM02]** A playable administration must close every supported administration function through declared offices, institutions, procedures, or delegations. Names and groupings may vary; semantic ownership may not disappear.

An administration can combine functions when one office legitimately possesses the relevant mandate, information, personnel, and procedure.

An administration can split functions when the owners, relationships, and handoffs remain explicit.

A combined office may not double-count staff, records, access, or resources. A split office may not create contradictory copies of one queue or decision.

## 5.2 Required function families

### A. Constitutional principals

- President;
- Vice President.

### B. Presidential operations and decision flow

- Chief of Staff/operations/access/scheduling function;
- Staff Secretary/executive-secretariat/presidential-paper function;
- records-management support where required.

### C. Policy coordination

- domestic-policy coordination;
- economic-policy coordination and analysis;
- national-security/external-affairs coordination;
- specialized science, technology, climate, cyber, drug, trade, or other advice where supported.

### D. Constraint and review

- OMB or equivalent budget/management/regulatory review;
- White House legal advice;
- DOJ/OLC and agency-counsel relationship where applicable;
- security, privilege, classification, and records review at bounded resolution.

### E. Institutional liaison

- congressional liaison;
- intergovernmental liaison;
- external/public engagement;
- communications/press;
- official political/coalition assessment separated from campaign ownership.

### F. Personnel and succession

- presidential personnel;
- nominations and appointments;
- vacancy and acting-service management;
- removals, resignations, and replacement processes.

### G. Cabinet and agency execution

- department/agency leadership;
- deputies or acting routes where needed;
- career institutional continuity;
- program and operational units.

## 5.3 Not every function is equally deep

A function may be:

- a persistent individualized office and actor;
- a persistent office with aggregated staff;
- a rule-bound process;
- a coordination relation;
- a bounded aggregate service;
- omitted when no supported process requires it.

The representation level must be justified by later presidential decisions or independent institutional consequences.

# 6. President and Vice President

## 6.1 President

The President owns or selects only decisions constitutionally, legally, politically, or organizationally attributable to the office or controlled administration surface.

The President may:

- set administration objectives;
- appoint, nominate, retain, remove, or request resignation where applicable;
- delegate within authority;
- decide matters reserved to the office;
- resolve cross-office disputes when presidential judgment is needed;
- choose personal calendar intervention;
- request analysis or reconsideration;
- issue presidential actions or communications;
- deliberately allow defaults.

The President does not own every subordinate choice.

## 6.2 Vice President

**[DI]** The Vice President should be a distinct office and actor, not a passive bonus.

Potential functions include:

- constitutional Senate role;
- tie-breaking where applicable;
- delegated policy portfolio;
- congressional, state, or stakeholder relationships;
- representation of the administration;
- crisis or task-force leadership;
- electoral-coalition and succession significance.

A delegated Vice-Presidential portfolio must identify authority, scope, staff, reporting, records, and reserved presidential decisions.

The Vice President may disagree, underperform, overreach, build independent relationships, or become politically consequential without becoming an alternative player-controlled President.

# 7. Chief of Staff and White House operations

## 7.1 Core function

**[DI]** The Chief of Staff/operations function should own or coordinate, at the accepted abstraction:

- access to the President;
- presidential schedule and meeting conflicts;
- cross-office coordination rules;
- assignment of lead and supporting White House offices when not otherwise fixed;
- enforcement of presidential process requirements;
- triage of matters that reach the presidential decision process;
- follow-up on presidential decisions;
- staff-level conflict requiring escalation;
- organization of senior meetings.

The Chief of Staff does not own every substantive issue or decide what is objectively important.

## 7.2 Access models are concrete process choices

An administration may use:

- centralized gatekeeping;
- more open access;
- strong policy-council filtering;
- Cabinet-centered access;
- Vice-Presidential or deputy-heavy coordination.

These are not personality labels. They arise through rules about:

- who may nominate a presidential item;
- who sees which records;
- required clearance;
- meeting and calendar ownership;
- delegated thresholds;
- whether dissent may travel outside the lead office;
- how unresolved items are bundled.

## 7.3 Gatekeeping limits

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM03]** The operations function may route, schedule, bundle, return, or defer items only through recorded process. It may not silently erase legally mandatory notices, deadlines, direct presidential communications, or another institution’s canonical action.

Improper suppression, manipulation, or concealment can exist as a canonical staff act when supported. It cannot be an invisible UI filter with no owner or history.

## 7.4 Calendar ownership

The scheduling process may own:

- proposed meetings and events;
- reserved blocks;
- travel and campaign conflicts;
- decision deadlines requiring presidential time;
- accepted schedule;
- cancellation or substitution records.

It does not own external deadlines or actor willingness. Congress, courts, states, agencies, media, crises, and campaigns retain their own clocks.

# 8. Staff Secretary and executive secretariat

## 8.1 Core function

**[DI]** The Staff Secretary/executive-secretariat function should own the administrative assembly and routing of presidential paper at the chosen abstraction:

- receipt of candidate decision materials;
- identification of lead and required consult offices;
- clearance requests and responses;
- version lineage;
- source-artifact references;
- missing-input status;
- dissent attachment;
- deadline and default metadata;
- final packet assembly;
- presidential disposition receipt;
- return-for-revision routing;
- official decision-record handoff.

It does not determine the substantive truth or secretly choose the best option.

## 8.2 Packet assembly is not decision ownership

The executive secretariat can determine that a packet is:

- incomplete;
- awaiting legal review;
- awaiting fiscal review;
- awaiting agency analysis;
- awaiting dissent;
- superseded;
- ready for presidential review;
- returned for revision;
- resolved by presidential action or default.

It cannot convert an unsupported option into a valid one by including it.

## 8.3 Time-sensitive routing

Packet urgency must derive from:

- external deadline;
- presidential instruction;
- valid reporting threshold;
- institutional opportunity;
- expiring authority;
- scheduled meeting or decision;
- operational need communicated by a responsible owner.

No hidden drama score may place a packet on top.

# 9. Domestic, economic, and national-security policy coordination

## 9.1 Coordination councils do not own domains

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM04]** A domestic, economic, national-security, science, technology, climate, cyber, or other policy council coordinates advice and interagency process. It does not own the underlying material domain, agency authority, budget, law, or external actor outcome.

## 9.2 Domestic-policy function

A domestic-policy coordination function may:

- frame cross-agency domestic objectives;
- convene responsible agencies;
- identify legal, fiscal, state, delivery, and political dependencies;
- commission analysis;
- compare routes;
- track presidential commitments;
- maintain policy workstream coordination;
- escalate unresolved cross-agency disputes.

It cannot order an agency outside applicable authority merely by labeling something a presidential priority.

## 9.3 Economic-policy function

An economic-policy coordination function may integrate:

- labor and macroeconomic measurements;
- industry, trade, energy, household, and fiscal analysis;
- agency forecasts and disagreements;
- policy effects and distributional uncertainty;
- congressional and market context.

The Council of Economic Advisers or another empirical-analysis office may provide evidence-based economic analysis and forecasts. The coordination function does not own actual GDP, employment, prices, wages, market behavior, or official statistical releases.

## 9.4 National-security function

A national-security coordination function may:

- receive intelligence and agency reporting according to access;
- convene departments and agencies;
- coordinate policy options;
- identify domestic, legal, fiscal, diplomatic, military, cyber, energy, trade, and communications consequences;
- preserve dissent and uncertainty;
- escalate decisions reserved to the President.

It does not own intelligence truth, military operations, diplomacy, agency compliance, foreign actor choices, or domestic material consequences.

## 9.5 Specialized offices

Specialized offices such as science/technology, cyber, climate/environment, trade, drug policy, or another configuration-specific function should be retained individually only when they supply distinct:

- expertise;
- statutory duty;
- coordination authority;
- information access;
- budget review role;
- presidential decision or recurring cross-domain conflict.

Otherwise, they may be aggregated under a broader coordination function.

# 10. OMB, fiscal control, management, and regulatory review

## 10.1 OMB is not the economy or Congress

**[DI]** OMB or an equivalent institution may own, at the selected abstraction:

- executive budget-development processes;
- agency budget-submission review;
- apportionment and execution review where applicable;
- management and performance review;
- interagency legislative-reference process;
- regulatory review where applicable;
- cross-agency fiscal estimates and assumptions;
- review queues, deadlines, and clearance records;
- official recommendations to the President.

OMB does not own:

- congressional appropriations;
- statutory budget authority merely because it reviews execution;
- agency program operations;
- Treasury cash or debt operations unless separately assigned;
- material outcomes;
- macroeconomic truth;
- legal validity by itself.

## 10.2 OMB queue integrity

An OMB task must identify:

- requesting or initiating owner;
- legal/fiscal review basis;
- affected agency or program;
- source assumptions;
- due date;
- dependencies;
- assigned review unit;
- resource conflicts;
- status;
- uncertainty;
- disposition and record.

Urgent presidential labeling does not erase statutory work already in the queue.

## 10.3 Fiscal disagreement

OMB may disagree with:

- policy councils over cost or implementation;
- agencies over assumptions, resources, or management capacity;
- communications staff over public claims;
- legislative affairs over concessions;
- economic advisers over forecast assumptions;
- Counsel over authority or required procedure.

The disagreement must identify the conflicting facts, assumptions, duties, or objectives. It cannot be generated from an abstract need for internal drama.

# 11. White House Counsel, agency counsel, DOJ, and OLC

## 11.1 Distinct legal roles

The administration’s legal function must distinguish:

- White House Counsel advising the President and White House process;
- agency general counsel advising an agency under its legal responsibilities;
- Department of Justice litigation and legal functions;
- Office of Legal Counsel or an equivalent authoritative executive-branch legal-advice function;
- courts as independent legal resolvers;
- law and legal authority as separate canonical state.

## 11.2 Counsel does not own legal truth

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM05]** Legal advisers own advice, analysis, clearance, risk assessment, and requests for authoritative resolution. They do not own statutes, court rulings, legal validity, compliance, or future judicial outcomes.

A Counsel memorandum may be:

- correct;
- incomplete;
- disputed;
- superseded;
- based on uncertain facts;
- contrary to another legal office;
- later rejected by a court.

## 11.3 Presidential-action review

A proposed executive order, memorandum, proclamation, veto message, privilege assertion, or other formal action may require:

- source draft;
- policy lead;
- agency consultation;
- Counsel review;
- OLC or DOJ review where applicable;
- OMB or fiscal review where applicable;
- Staff Secretary clearance;
- presidential signature or disposition;
- publication/delivery.

No one review step creates the downstream legal or material result.

## 11.4 Interagency legal disputes

An unresolved dispute among executive agencies may:

- remain at agency-counsel level;
- escalate to White House Counsel;
- be referred to DOJ/OLC under an applicable process;
- reach the President when policy or constitutional judgment remains;
- later enter court through a separate case.

The selected route depends on authority, procedure, actors, and records—not a generic legal-conflict flag.

# 12. Legislative Affairs

## 12.1 Core function

The Legislative Affairs function may own:

- White House contacts and liaison assignments;
- received communications from congressional actors;
- outreach plans;
- whip and feasibility assessments with sources and confidence;
- records of requested commitments and concessions;
- negotiation assignments;
- floor, committee, and confirmation windows communicated by Congress;
- follow-up obligations;
- administration-side congressional strategy recommendations.

It does not own votes, committee decisions, leadership scheduling, or member beliefs.

## 12.2 House, Senate, committee, and member relationships

The design may distinguish House, Senate, leadership, committee, nomination, appropriations, and issue portfolios when those distinctions change access, knowledge, or action.

A generic `CongressRelations = 72` is prohibited.

## 12.3 Assessment support

A whip assessment may combine:

- public positions;
- direct member communication;
- staff conversations;
- commitments;
- committee role;
- constituency estimates at honest support levels;
- leadership information;
- past conduct;
- uncertainty.

It remains an administration assessment, not the member’s hidden decision state.

## 12.4 Legislative disagreement inside the administration

Legislative Affairs may recommend accepting a concession that:

- policy staff believes defeats the objective;
- OMB believes is fiscally damaging;
- Counsel believes creates legal risk;
- communications staff believes is hard to defend;
- the President’s campaign commitment appears to reject.

That is a legitimate decision package when each view has provenance.

## 14.2 Appointment stages remain distinct

Potential stages include:

```text
candidate identified
→ interest/availability established
→ vetting initiated
→ relevant review completed or unresolved
→ President selects or declines
→ nomination/appointment attempt occurs
→ Senate or other procedure independently resolves where required
→ commission/assignment becomes effective
→ service continues, becomes acting, ends, or becomes vacant
```

A nomination does not equal confirmation. Confirmation does not automatically equal effective service if another required step remains. A vacancy does not automatically erase the office’s work.

## 14.3 Acting leadership

Acting leadership must identify:

- legal or institutional basis;
- effective interval;
- functions and duties available or unavailable;
- records and access;
- relationship to pending nominees;
- expiration or reporting requirements;
- agency continuity consequences.

An acting official is not merely `PermanentLeaderQuality -10`.

## 14.4 Confirmation strategy

The President may face choices about:

- whom to nominate;
- whether to spend congressional time and relationships;
- whether to retain an acting official;
- whether to withdraw and replace a nominee;
- whether a vacancy is tolerable;
- whether to prioritize judicial, agency, diplomatic, or other confirmations;
- how candidate expertise, loyalty, relationships, ideology, and confirmability trade off.

The Senate owns confirmation decisions.

# 15. Cabinet, agency leadership, and career institutions

## 15.1 Cabinet is not one actor

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM07]** The Cabinet is a coordination/advisory grouping of distinct offices and actors. It is not a hive mind and does not own one unified position unless an actual presidential or Cabinet procedure adopts one.

A Cabinet meeting may produce:

- individual advice;
- disagreement;
- assignments;
- presidential direction;
- no decision;
- a request for further work.

## 15.2 Dual position of department heads

A Cabinet secretary or agency head may act as:

- presidential adviser;
- holder of department/agency authority;
- manager of institutional operations;
- participant in interagency councils;
- political representative of the administration;
- witness or respondent to Congress;
- actor with personal beliefs, relationships, and ambitions.

Those roles overlap but do not collapse.

A secretary’s recommendation to the President is not automatically an agency action. An agency action must use the agency’s proper authority and procedure.

## 15.3 Agency leadership and career continuity

An agency should distinguish, at whatever resolution is later accepted:

- political leadership;
- acting leadership;
- career management;
- program offices;
- legal/financial/inspector functions when load-bearing;
- operational resources and queues;
- field or recipient relationships;
- statutory duties;
- institutional records.

Political leadership can change discretionary priorities through valid actions. It cannot silently eliminate statutory duties, completed records, court orders, fiscal restrictions, or material constraints.

## 15.4 Agency disagreement and resistance

Agency actors may:

- advise against a White House preference;
- request clarification;
- identify insufficient authority or resources;
- delay because prerequisites are missing;
- comply;
- partially comply;
- refuse when supported;
- resign;
- leak or report wrongdoing through later accepted routes;
- continue standing policy absent valid change.

These outcomes arise from actors, law, procedures, information, and capabilities—not an abstract `AgencyResistance` meter.

# 16. Assignment and queue contract

## 16.1 Assignment identity

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM08]** Every load-bearing administration assignment must identify:

- assignment identity;
- initiating owner or instruction;
- responsible lead office/institution;
- supporting or consulted offices;
- objective or required output;
- authority or duty basis;
- source records and inputs;
- target or consumer;
- effective start;
- requested or legal due date;
- expiration/default where applicable;
- dependencies and prerequisites;
- owner-scoped personnel, time, funds, access, or other reserved capability;
- status;
- reporting and escalation threshold;
- current uncertainty;
- completion, cancellation, supersession, or failure record.

These are semantic obligations, not one required software type.

## 16.2 Queue ownership

A queue belongs to a specific office, institution, procedure, or shared process.

Examples:

- OMB budget-review queue;
- Counsel legal-review queue;
- Personnel vetting queue;
- Legislative Affairs negotiation assignments;
- Staff Secretary clearance queue;
- agency application or enforcement queue;
- presidential decision packet queue;
- scheduling proposals.

No universal `AdministrationQueue` determines every office’s work.

## 16.3 Queue precedence

Queue precedence may derive from:

- legal deadline;
- external institutional window;
- presidential instruction;
- standing duty;
- existing reservation or commitment;
- safety/security urgency based on received information;
- delegated policy priority;
- first-in/other procedural rules;
- owner judgment with a recorded basis.

A global drama score is invalid.

## 16.4 Overlapping assignments

When assignments collide, the result must be concrete:

- one review finishes later;
- a deadline is missed;
- a packet carries higher uncertainty;
- another office is assigned;
- mandatory work displaces discretionary work;
- external action occurs before advice is complete;
- the issue escalates because subordinate owners cannot reconcile it;
- the President explicitly accepts the tradeoff.

There is no generic administration-overload penalty.

# 17. Delegation and reserved authority

## 17.1 Delegation declaration

A load-bearing delegation must identify:

- principal;
- delegate;
- source authority;
- objective or duty;
- scope and targets;
- allowed actions;
- prohibited or reserved actions;
- resources and access;
- effective interval;
- reporting thresholds;
- deadlines;
- revocation or modification process;
- succession treatment;
- record and information transfer;
- default when the delegate does not act.

## 17.2 Delegation does not transfer omnipotence

The delegate may act only within actual authority and capability.

A presidential delegation cannot:

- transfer authority the President does not possess;
- force Congress, courts, states, firms, organizations, or media to comply;
- directly change material outcomes;
- erase agency duties;
- create information access outside applicable rules.

## 17.3 Standing instructions

The President may establish standing instructions such as:

- return if estimated cost exceeds a threshold;
- escalate if a governor coalition reaches a declared condition based on valid reports;
- proceed with routine grants within enacted terms;
- appeal adverse orders under defined conditions;
- preserve a reserve unless emergency criteria are met;
- send routine press responses through the Press Secretary.

Thresholds must use observable facts available to the delegate, not hidden truth.

## 17.4 Delegated success can remain quiet

A properly delegated office or agency may resolve a matter successfully without presidential attention.

The result appears through summaries, records, or later consequences according to player settings and access. The game does not create a presidential decision merely to show that a system exists.

# 18. Information receipt, access, and classification

## 18.1 Receipt before knowledge

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM09]** No officeholder, staff office, Cabinet actor, or agency head may use a fact merely because it exists in canonical world state.

Knowledge requires one or more of:

- official role-accessible record;
- delivered communication or artifact;
- direct operational experience;
- valid briefing;
- measurement or report;
- source relationship;
- meeting participation;
- lawful or institutionally accepted access;
- retained memory.

## 18.2 Access categories

Step 5 requires only a bounded semantic distinction among categories such as:

- public;
- administration-wide official;
- office- or agency-internal;
- presidential/EOP;
- privileged legal or deliberative;
- classified or security-restricted;
- source-protected;
- personal/private;
- party/campaign separate from government.

The exact classification regime is deferred.

## 18.3 Access is not truth

Having access to an artifact does not prove:

- the artifact is true;
- the recipient read it;
- the analysis is sound;
- the source is credible;
- the forecast will occur;
- all relevant dissent was included.

## 18.4 Security and clearance

Where security access matters, an officeholder or staff actor may be unable to receive or work on certain records until a valid clearance/access condition exists.

This can change:

- who can perform an assignment;
- whether an acting official receives a full brief;
- whether a packet is complete;
- whether a deadline is met;
- whether the President receives dissent or only a partial assessment.

It should not become one global `SecurityAccess` stat.

## 18.5 Institutional versus private knowledge

A private source relationship, personal trust judgment, campaign poll, or unrecorded conversation does not automatically enter the office’s transferable knowledge.

A formal agency record, legal notice, budget submission, or signed decision does not disappear when one holder leaves.

# 19. Consultation, clearance, dissent, and decision packages

## 19.1 Lead, support, consult, and clear

For a consequential issue, offices may occupy different roles:

- **Lead:** responsible for producing the coordinated recommendation or output.
- **Support:** performs assigned analysis or operations.
- **Consult:** provides views or information without veto authority.
- **Clear:** must review because an accepted legal, fiscal, security, records, or procedural requirement applies.
- **Dissent:** records a materially different assessment or recommendation.
- **Decision owner:** holder or procedure that resolves the administration-side choice.

These roles must not be inferred merely from screen placement.

## 19.2 Decision-package minimum provenance

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM10]** A presidential decision package must be able to expose:

- initiating occurrence, request, or objective;
- lead office;
- source artifacts and dates;
- known facts and support levels;
- unresolved factual disputes;
- affected law, fiscal state, programs, institutions, actors, populations, places, and commitments;
- options and their authority/route provenance;
- implementation owner and capability assessment;
- fiscal assessment;
- legal assessment;
- congressional, state, external, and public-information assessments where applicable;
- consulted offices;
- dissenting views;
- decision deadline or opportunity window;
- default/inaction consequence;
- requested presidential act;
- independent downstream resolvers.

Not every packet needs every category. Missing applicability differs from missing required work.

## 19.3 No balanced-menu authorship

Options may not be added because the player needs three attractive buttons.

An option must be:

- meaningfully attemptable by the President or administration;
- available under current law, procedure, relationships, resources, and timing;
- supported by a real target and route;
- accompanied by known constraints and uncertainty.

A staff recommendation can advocate one option. The packet does not need false neutrality.

## 19.4 Dissent

Dissent may arise from:

- different evidence;
- different institutional mission;
- legal disagreement;
- fiscal assumptions;
- implementation knowledge;
- constituency or state information;
- time horizon;
- risk tolerance;
- professional norms;
- policy objectives;
- uncertainty.

Dissent is not a random personality event.

## 19.5 Presidential disposition

The President may:

- select an option;
- modify within attemptable bounds;
- request more analysis;
- return the packet;
- delegate the decision;
- defer;
- allow default;
- choose a disputed course;
- reject all presented routes.

A request for more analysis consumes specific time and assignments and may outlast the opportunity.

# 20. Task-specific capability, competence, error, and uncertainty

## 20.1 No global staff-quality score

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM11]** No universal `StaffQuality`, `CabinetCompetence`, `AdministrationCapacity`, or `Loyalty` value may resolve unrelated office tasks.

## 20.2 Capability sources

Task performance may depend on concrete factors such as:

- holder experience relevant to the task;
- professional or substantive expertise;
- office procedures and institutional memory;
- assigned personnel and time;
- current queue and deadlines;
- quality and support of available information;
- access or clearance;
- cooperation from agencies and other offices;
- relationship access;
- legal/fiscal complexity;
- review and verification completed;
- turnover and vacancies;
- management and delegation choices.

## 20.3 Observable consequences of weak performance

Weak performance may produce:

- delay;
- incomplete consultation;
- higher stated uncertainty;
- omitted evidence;
- inconsistent assumptions;
- missed commitment conflict;
- invalid or unavailable option reaching review and being rejected;
- poor follow-up;
- deadline expiration;
- operational confusion;
- incorrect advice later corrected;
- leak or dispute exposure where causally supported.

It does not directly produce `policyEffectiveness -15%`.

## 20.4 Loyalty and alignment

Loyalty, ideological alignment, trust, and willingness to carry out presidential preferences are relational and context-specific.

A highly aligned official may be technically weak. A skeptical career official may execute law competently. A loyal appointee may refuse an unlawful instruction. An expert adviser may leak, resign, or dissent under some conditions.

No universal loyalty score forces compliance.

## 20.5 Error and revision

Advice, forecasts, and assessments can be wrong without the underlying world changing to match them.

Corrections and revisions create new records. They do not rewrite the earlier advice or decisions made from it.

# 21. Escalation and presidential attention

## 21.1 Accepted attention chain

Step 5 preserves the accepted chain:

```text
canonical occurrence or actor action
→ observable artifact or communication
→ administration receipt
→ bounded office interpretation and coordination
→ valid escalation or routine resolution
→ presidential decision surface or review when justified
```

## 21.2 Valid escalation bases

A matter may escalate because:

1. the President alone owns the required act;
2. a standing delegation threshold is reached through observable facts;
3. a known legal, congressional, fiscal, electoral, diplomatic, operational, or calendar deadline approaches;
4. two or more responsible offices cannot resolve conflicting objectives within delegated authority;
5. the player requested monitoring at a declared threshold;
6. a senior officeholder uses bounded judgment based on role, records, evidence, uncertainty, and expected consequence;
7. a subordinate owner requests authority, resources, clarification, or risk acceptance that only a higher owner can provide.

## 21.3 Escalation record

Every presidential item must identify:

- nominating office or actor;
- initiating record or communication;
- recipient and receipt time;
- lead office;
- known support level;
- uncertainty;
- why the President is required or requested;
- deadline/window;
- default without action;
- unresolved dissent;
- requested decision.

## 21.4 Staff judgment is fallible

A senior official may:

- miss a matter;
- overstate urgency;
- underestimate a risk;
- delay escalation;
- suppress dissent through a canonical act;
- escalate for political or institutional reasons;
- revise an assessment.

Those outcomes require bounded knowledge and actor/office conduct. They are not invisible narrative selection.

## 21.5 Not everything reaches the President

A significant agency failure, state conflict, local condition, legal concern, or investigation can remain below presidential awareness when:

- it has not been observed;
- information has not reached the administration;
- staff does not have jurisdiction or access;
- delegated owners resolve it;
- evidence remains weak;
- reporting thresholds are not met;
- a senior official makes a fallible non-escalation decision.

The world remains coherent without presidential drama.

# 22. Internal conflict, hierarchy, and decision rights

## 22.1 Hierarchy is scoped

Reporting lines may govern:

- assignment;
- review;
- access;
- evaluation;
- resource allocation;
- escalation;
- removal recommendation.

A reporting line does not automatically transfer substantive authority held by law, office, agency, or procedure.

## 22.2 Cross-cutting conflicts

Common conflicts include:

- White House policy objective versus agency statutory duty;
- OMB fiscal restraint versus program delivery;
- Legislative Affairs feasibility versus policy ambition;
- Counsel legal caution versus political urgency;
- Press timing versus factual verification;
- IGA flexibility versus national uniformity;
- national-security secrecy versus public explanation;
- Personnel confirmability versus expertise or loyalty;
- Cabinet secretary agency mission versus White House direction;
- short-term election timing versus long-term implementation.

The conflict becomes gameplay only when it changes available routes, timing, resources, relationships, or presidential decisions.

## 22.3 Resolution below the President

Staff or Cabinet actors may resolve conflicts below the President when delegated authority, hierarchy, procedure, or standing instruction permits.

The result must record:

- who decided;
- authority or delegation;
- consulted parties;
- affected assignments/resources;
- resulting instruction or action;
- reporting or later review.

## 22.4 Presidential resolution

The President may resolve a conflict by:

- choosing one objective;
- narrowing scope;
- changing lead office;
- reallocating an owner-scoped resource;
- accepting legal/fiscal/implementation risk;
- directing another round of work;
- replacing leadership;
- seeking new authority;
- allowing a deadline/default.

The President does not directly set the external result.

# 23. Turnover, records, transition, and succession

## 23.1 Record ownership

Administration records may belong to different legal/institutional owners, including:

- Presidential records;
- agency Federal records;
- office or institution records;
- privileged legal records;
- classified records;
- personal/private material;
- campaign or party material.

The game need not implement full records law now, but it must not treat all administration information as one transferable memory store.

## 23.2 Departure of one official

When an official leaves:

- office assignment ends;
- replacement/acting/vacancy process begins as applicable;
- official office records remain;
- actor-private knowledge and relationships remain with the actor unless validly recorded or transferred;
- active assignments need reassignment, suspension, default, or explicit closure;
- delegations are evaluated under their terms;
- access changes;
- prior acts remain historical facts.

## 23.3 Presidential transition

At administration turnover:

- constitutional office transfer is resolved separately;
- agencies prepare and retain transition-relevant institutional records;
- incoming officials receive only legitimate briefings and access;
- current laws, budgets, programs, queues, court orders, contracts, and operational obligations persist;
- outgoing presidential records transfer according to their owner/rules;
- campaign/transition-team private material does not automatically become government record;
- institutional accounts and public functions may transfer while prior content remains historical;
- incoming leaders may change priorities through valid acts rather than world reset.

## 23.4 Continuity risk

A transition can create specific risks:

- vacant leadership;
- acting-service limitations;
- delayed confirmation;
- incomplete handoff;
- loss of private source relationships;
- altered trust with Congress/states/allies;
- changed queue priorities;
- agency uncertainty;
- conflicting outgoing and incoming instructions with effective dates.

These are concrete states, not `TransitionPenalty -20%`.

# 24. Temporary councils, crisis cells, and special initiatives

## 24.1 Creation

A President or authorized owner may create a temporary council or task force when a supported objective crosses normal office boundaries.

The creation must identify:

- authority or organizational basis;
- purpose;
- chair;
- participating offices/institutions;
- delegated scope;
- resources and staff support;
- reporting route;
- records;
- effective interval or sunset;
- relationship to existing owners.

## 24.2 No authority multiplication

**[DI — HARD INVARIANT LC-ADM12]** A task force does not gain the combined authority of every participant or become a new owner of their domains, money, records, or operations.

It coordinates. Members act through their own authority unless a valid delegation says otherwise.

## 24.3 Crisis operation

A crisis cell may accelerate coordination because:

- participants receive rapid operational reports;
- meetings occur more frequently;
- assignments and thresholds change;
- resources are validly reassigned;
- presidential decisions are bundled.

It cannot bypass receipt, legal authority, agency ownership, or independent external resolution merely because the matter is a crisis.

## 24.4 Sunset and persistence

When the temporary body ends:

- its records persist according to ownership;
- assignments close or return to permanent owners;
- unresolved obligations remain;
- no hidden status is lost;
- members and agencies continue separately.

# 25. Player-facing administration gameplay

## 25.1 What the President actually decides

Administration gameplay should create presidential decisions such as:

- select or replace a senior official;
- nominate versus retain acting leadership;
- choose confirmability versus expertise, ideology, loyalty, or relationship access;
- assign a policy portfolio to the Vice President, Cabinet secretary, or White House lead;
- define standing delegation and reporting thresholds;
- decide which office leads a cross-agency effort;
- resolve a Cabinet or White House disagreement;
- request more analysis at the cost of time or opportunity;
- accept a packet with uncertainty;
- personally intervene in a meeting or delegate it;
- decide whether to preserve agency autonomy, centralize coordination, or seek new authority;
- reallocate specific staff, budget review, investigative, legal, or implementation capability;
- respond to resignation, vacancy, leak, misconduct, missed deadline, or failed coordination;
- decide whether a specialized council or task force is justified;
- decide whether a matter should remain delegated.

## 25.2 What should not become routine clicks

The player should not normally click:

- every memo routing step;
- every interoffice email;
- every career staff assignment;
- every agency case;
- every meeting attendee;
- every clearance acknowledgment;
- every routine briefing;
- every grant or program transaction;
- every press question;
- every low-level legal review;
- every ordinary queue transition.

These proceed through standing process, delegation, office judgment, and aggregate routine work.

## 25.3 Administration strategy, not staff optimization

The player’s recurring strategic questions are:

- Who do I trust with this portfolio?
- What authority am I delegating?
- What must come back to me?
- Which analysis or dissent do I need before deciding?
- Which queue or capability is being displaced?
- Is the agency’s objection legal, operational, political, or self-protective?
- Is this vacancy tolerable?
- Is another review worth losing the current opportunity?
- Does this conflict require my decision or can the administration resolve it?
- What happens if I allow the default?

## 25.4 Player-facing information requirements

A later interface should permit bounded inspection of:

- administration organization and functional ownership;
- current officeholders and vacancies;
- acting versus confirmed status;
- known office mandates and authority;
- major delegations;
- lead/support/consult roles on workstreams;
- owner-specific queues and conflicts relevant to presidential decisions;
- pending nominations and confirmation state;
- decision packets and dissent;
- deadlines/defaults;
- staff and agency assessments with confidence and provenance;
- turnover and record history.

This is an information contract, not final UI authority.

# 26. Anti-bureaucracy and anti-magic contract

## 26.1 Anti-magic tests

Reject a design when:

- `Staff` knows a fact no office received;
- `Cabinet` makes one decision without a locus;
- `ChiefOfStaff` chooses the important event through a drama score;
- `StaffQuality` modifies every outcome;
- `AgencyResistance` decides compliance without actors, law, capacity, or procedure;
- `PresidentialPriority` directly accelerates an unrelated queue;
- `DecisionPacket` creates legal authority or material results;
- `PoliticalOffice` spends campaign resources from government state;
- a new administration resets agency records or obligations;
- a vacancy simply applies a universal debuff;
- a task force absorbs all member authority;
- the player controls every subordinate decision.

## 26.2 Anti-click tests

Reject a design when:

- time cannot advance until every memo is acknowledged;
- every office generates one mandatory daily decision;
- routine work is surfaced merely to prove simulation depth;
- the player must manually route every document;
- delegation is less efficient than clicking everything with no compensating reason;
- every disagreement requires presidential resolution;
- every successful delegated action interrupts the President.

## 26.3 Functional compression test

For any proposed office or actor, ask:

1. What distinct decision, information route, authority, resource, relationship, or record does it preserve?
2. Could another retained owner perform the function without changing a presidential decision or consequence?
3. If combined, what disagreement or queue collision would disappear incorrectly?
4. If individualized, what single actor identity changes?
5. What player-facing information is genuinely useful?

If no load-bearing distinction survives, compress it.

# 27. Adversarial administration proofs

These are paper contract tests, not runtime proofs.

## Proof A — deteriorating employment data

### Canonical chain

```text
Labor condition changes
→ measurement process produces a scheduled release
→ economic-analysis office receives the artifact
→ economic advisers assess level, geography, groups, uncertainty, and revisions
→ Domestic/Economic policy coordination requests agency and program implications
→ OMB assesses fiscal and implementation consequences
→ Legislative Affairs assesses current congressional opportunity
→ offices disagree over emergency response versus waiting for confirmation
→ executive secretariat assembles sources, dissent, options, deadline/default
→ Chief of Staff validates presidential need and calendar
→ President chooses, requests revision, delegates, or waits
```

### Valid quiet branch

If the change is within uncertainty, no threshold or deadline is reached, and delegated monitoring remains adequate, no presidential interruption occurs. The condition and staff work continue.

### Anti-cheat

The President never sees raw hidden employment truth. CEA/economic staff do not own the official release or Labor condition. OMB does not directly set fiscal consequences. Legislative Affairs does not set congressional response.

## Proof B — Housing implementation failure

### Canonical chain

```text
program/agency operations accumulate delays
→ career program office records missed milestones or recipient reports
→ agency leadership receives operational report
→ standing delegation permits corrective technical assistance within limits
→ agency acts without President
→ later threshold is reached because cost, state conflict, or statutory deadline exceeds delegation
→ Cabinet secretary and Domestic Policy coordinate
→ OMB identifies resource collision
→ IGA receives governor requests
→ Counsel assesses available flexibility
→ President receives only the unresolved cross-owner choice
```

### Forks

- agency resolves quietly under standing authority;
- political leader suppresses escalation through a canonical act and later consequences;
- vacancy leaves no authorized discretionary official, forcing acting/appointment decision;
- the President chooses flexibility, resources, legislation, enforcement, or inaction through actual routes.

### Anti-cheat

The workstream cannot manufacture a failure. `Staff` cannot solve implementation generically. A priority label cannot add agency capability.

## Proof C — legislative compromise

```text
Congressional leader communicates a conditional floor opportunity
→ Legislative Affairs receives and records it
→ policy lead evaluates substantive concession
→ OMB evaluates fiscal terms
→ Counsel evaluates legal/procedural implications
→ Communications evaluates public explanation
→ campaign commitment conflict is referenced
→ Staff Secretary assembles competing views
→ President accepts, counteroffers, refuses, delegates further negotiation, or lets offer expire
→ Congress independently resolves scheduling, amendment, and votes
```

### Anti-cheat

There is no one `CabinetPosition`. Legislative Affairs cannot transfer votes. The option menu arises from the actual offer, authority, and timing.

## Proof D — cyber incident crossing domestic and security systems

```text
agency/network owner detects anomalous operational evidence
→ technical and intelligence artifacts are produced with uncertainty
→ authorized security offices receive them
→ National Security coordination convenes relevant agencies
→ cyber coordination office tracks responsibilities
→ OMB identifies continuity/funding implications
→ Counsel identifies legal authorities and limits
→ Domestic/Economic offices assess supply, infrastructure, and household effects
→ Press prepares contingent communication routes
→ unresolved attribution, response authority, or resource tradeoff reaches President
```

### Non-occurrence branch

No hidden crisis director is required. If no incident occurs, ordinary cyber monitoring and agency queues continue without a replacement dramatic event.

### Anti-cheat

A crisis cell does not own the network event or combine agency authorities. Classified access does not imply truth. Public communication does not set belief.

## Proof E — investigative report and leak

```text
underlying agency failure or misconduct exists through its owner
→ source, record, filing, or anomaly reaches journalist
→ outlet investigates and publishes evidence
→ Press office receives press inquiry/public artifact
→ agency leadership and career records offices review known facts
→ Counsel assesses legal/privilege/litigation implications
→ Chief of Staff assesses personnel and coordination implications
→ responsible policy office assesses operational correction
→ President is asked only for reserved personnel, legal, communication,
  or policy decisions; routine fact-finding continues below
```

### Anti-cheat

Press cannot rewrite the underlying facts. Counsel cannot declare the story false by memo. The President may remain unaware until valid receipt/escalation. A correction is a new artifact.

## Proof F — major vacancy and confirmation

```text
agency head resigns
→ office assignment ends
→ vacancy record and acting-service route are evaluated
→ career institution and unresolved queues persist
→ Personnel identifies viable acting and nominee options
→ Counsel/agency legal function assesses authority and timing
→ Legislative Affairs assesses confirmation environment
→ President selects acting/nominee strategy or allows default
→ Senate independently resolves confirmation
→ acting authority may expire or change
```

### Anti-cheat

The agency does not vanish. A vacancy does not apply one generic competence penalty. A nominee is not automatically confirmed. Private outgoing knowledge does not transfer automatically.

## Proof G — Cabinet disagreement below presidential threshold

```text
agency A and agency B disagree over implementation sequence
→ interagency working process receives both records
→ standing delegation grants policy coordinator authority to choose sequence
  within enacted law and approved fiscal envelope
→ coordinator resolves and records decision
→ agencies proceed or use valid dispute route
→ President receives summary only, not a forced choice
```

### Anti-cheat

Not every disagreement is gameplay. The coordinator uses delegated authority, not hidden presidential ownership.

## Proof H — transition between administrations

```text
successor entitlement and office transfer occur
→ outgoing White House assignments end
→ agencies retain laws, operations, queues, records, court orders, and budgets
→ agency transition briefing artifacts are available through valid routes
→ incoming administration creates new White House topology and assignments
→ acting officials or vacancies exist where appointments are incomplete
→ new officeholders receive institutional records but not all outgoing private knowledge
→ priorities change through new instructions, appointments, budgets, and lawful actions
```

### Anti-cheat

The world does not reset. `AdministrationMemory` is not copied wholesale. Old political relationships remain historical but do not automatically become incoming trust.

# 29. Administration information and legibility contract

This section defines information requirements, not UI layout.

## 29.1 Administration overview

The player should be able to understand, at a glance:

- who holds major offices;
- which offices are vacant or acting;
- how the administration’s functional closure is organized;
- which workstreams each office leads or supports;
- major delegations;
- major queue collisions and deadlines relevant to the President;
- unresolved appointments and confirmations;
- current senior disputes requiring or approaching presidential attention;
- turnover and recent changes.

## 29.2 Office detail

An office view may expose:

- mandate and jurisdiction;
- current holder and assignment status;
- major official records and standing delegations;
- active assignments and deadlines;
- owner-scoped capacity relevant to supported work;
- known institutional relationships;
- required reporting paths;
- current known risks and uncertainty;
- succession and acting rules.

## 29.3 Officeholder detail

An officeholder view may expose only legitimately known:

- biography and public record;
- current office and prior generated history;
- relationships;
- known expertise and experience;
- staff or presidential assessments with uncertainty;
- public positions;
- commitments and prior conduct;
- conflicts or vulnerabilities discovered through valid records.

It may not expose exact hidden loyalty, future defection, private belief, or universal competence.

## 29.4 Decision package

The player-facing package should answer:

1. What happened or what are we trying to do?
2. Who received or initiated it?
3. Why does it need the President?
4. What is known and from which sources?
5. What is uncertain or disputed?
6. Which offices agree or dissent, and why?
7. What routes are genuinely available?
8. What authority, fiscal, administrative, state, congressional, legal, and communication constraints apply?
9. What is the deadline?
10. What happens without action?
11. Who resolves each downstream result?

## 29.5 No debug administration view

The player may not see:

- hidden actor objectives;
- exact private loyalty;
- unread artifacts;
- suppressed records not yet discovered;
- exact future queue completion;
- exact congressional decisions;
- debug-level agency capacity;
- secret dissent the administration has not recorded or received.

# 30. Step 5 audit gate

The detached Step 5 audit must answer:

> **Can the administration receive and interpret bounded information, coordinate persistent offices and agency leadership, own specific assignments, queues, delegations, records, access, and deadlines, generate real task-grounded disagreement, and escalate only when a valid presidential decision exists—while offices and institutions persist through turnover and without a universal `StaffQuality` or `AdministrationCapacity`, hidden drama director, Cabinet hive mind, shadow ownership of agency/law/fiscal/material state, or bureaucracy-clicking loop?**

PASS requires all of the following:

1. administration, office, officeholder, assignment, agency, career institution, coordination body, and decision package remain distinct;
2. office continuity and holder interpretation are separated;
3. every supported function has a declared owner or coordination rule without requiring one frozen org chart;
4. President and Vice President remain distinct offices/actors;
5. Chief of Staff routing cannot become omniscient or silently erase mandatory state;
6. executive-secretariat packet assembly cannot become substantive truth or option ownership;
7. policy councils coordinate rather than own domains/agencies;
8. OMB, Counsel, Legislative Affairs, IGA, Personnel, Press, and agency leadership remain within their actual semantic roles;
9. official political work and campaign operations remain separate;
10. assignments and queues have owner-specific resources, deadlines, defaults, and records;
11. delegation preserves subordinate autonomy and reserved authority;
12. staff knowledge requires receipt/access and preserves support status;
13. disagreement arises from evidence, duties, objectives, risk, or institutional position rather than drama;
14. competence is task-specific and observable through concrete work, not one scalar;
15. escalation follows the accepted bounded-attention chain;
16. routine work and delegated success can remain below presidential attention;
17. appointments, vacancies, acting service, removals, and confirmation preserve separate legal/institutional stages;
18. Cabinet and agencies are not hive minds;
19. transition preserves agency operations, records, queues, law, fiscal state, obligations, and bounded information ownership;
20. temporary bodies coordinate without multiplying authority;
21. hostile proofs remain coherent without curated outcomes;
22. the player receives strategic administration decisions rather than clerical workflow clicks.

A PASS accepts only the semantic internal-administration contract. It does not prove fun, UI, exact AI, exact staffing, historical calibration, generated prehistory, balance, performance, or implementation feasibility.

# 31. External research and provenance ledger

These official sources establish real-world design constraints. They do not dictate one administration topology or prove this candidate.

## 31.1 Executive branch and EOP

- [The Executive Branch — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/government/executive-branch/)
  - The President heads the executive branch; departments and agencies carry out day-to-day administration; the Executive Office provides immediate presidential staff and support.
- [Executive Office of the President — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/eop/)
  - Confirms that the EOP contains several distinct advisory and coordinating entities rather than one undifferentiated staff.

## 31.2 Staff Secretary and presidential paper

- [Presidential Departments — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/internships/presidential-departments/)
  - Describes the Staff Secretary’s role preparing the daily briefing book, coordinating across White House components, and vetting presidential actions, speeches, events, correspondence, releases, and memoranda.

## 31.3 Legislative and intergovernmental liaison

- [White House Office of Legislative Affairs appointments and office description](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/02/president-trump-announces-appointments-to-the-white-house-office-of-legislative-affairs/)
  - Provides a concrete House/Senate liaison organization and reporting relationship.
- [White House Office of Intergovernmental Affairs appointments and office description](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/02/president-trump-announces-appointments-to-the-white-house-office-of-intergovernmental-affairs/)
  - Identifies IGA as the principal liaison to state, local, tribal, and territorial governments and an interagency coordination channel.

## 31.4 Budget, management, and regulatory coordination

- [OMB offices — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/freedom-of-information-act-foia/)
  - Documents distinct OMB functions including Budget Review, Legislative Reference, Resource Management Offices, OIRA, Federal Financial Management, Procurement Policy, and E-Government.
- [OMB Circular A-11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/omb/information-resources/a11/)
  - Grounds preparation, submission, and execution of the federal budget as a real recurring process rather than one money meter.

## 31.5 Economic, science, and specialized advice

- [Council of Economic Advisers — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/cea/)
  - Establishes an evidence- and data-based economic advisory function.
- [Office of Science and Technology Policy — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ostp/)
  - Establishes scientific/technical advice and interagency coordination, including interaction with OMB on federal research priorities.

## 31.6 Legal review and interagency disputes

- [Office of Legal Counsel — Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/olc)
  - Describes legal advice to the President and executive agencies, review of executive orders and proclamations, and handling of complex or interagency legal questions.
- [OLC organizational mission and functions — Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/doj/office-legal-counsel)
  - Supports separation among White House Counsel requests, agency legal questions, OLC advice, and formal presidential action.

## 31.7 Appointments and vacancies

- [Federal Vacancies reporting — U.S. GAO](https://www.gao.gov/legal/federal-vacancies-reform-act/report-a-vacancy)
  - Shows that vacancy, acting service, nomination, withdrawal/rejection/return, confirmation, and end of acting service are separately reportable states.
- [GAO decision on acting-service timing](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-331539)
  - Demonstrates that acting authority has legal bases and time limits rather than being a generic weaker appointment.

## 31.8 Records and transitions

- [Presidential Transitions — National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/presidential-records/support-to-the-white-house/presidential-transitions)
  - Distinguishes outgoing presidential records, agency transition briefing materials, incoming access, and transfer of institutional public accounts.
- [Records Management in the White House — National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/presidential-records/support-to-the-white-house/records-management)
  - Grounds presidential-record ownership and the Office of Records Management function.
- [Transition-team materials guidance — National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/records-mgmt/memos/ac09-2017)
  - Distinguishes private transition-team materials from agency or presidential records depending on later incorporation and use.

## 31.9 Research inference limits

The sources show that real administrations use multiple offices, agencies, legal owners, budget processes, liaisons, personnel states, and record regimes.

They do not prove:

- that every administration must use the same reporting lines;
- that every real office belongs in the game;
- an exact staff count;
- a universal decision process;
- the correct gameplay balance;
- that all real legal doctrine must be implemented.

Step 5 uses them to identify functions and ownership seams, then applies the project’s deletion/compression rule.

# 33. Candidate disposition

## **READY FOR DETACHED LIVING COUNTRY STEP-5 AUDIT**

The proposed answer is:

> **The presidency should be served by a flexible but causally closed federation of persistent White House offices, Cabinet and agency leadership, career institutions, assignments, queues, delegations, access rules, decision-package processes, and records. Offices and institutions persist; officeholders interpret and act. Routine work resolves under real authority and standing delegation. Only information-bounded, unresolved presidential choices escalate. Competence, disagreement, congestion, turnover, and implementation quality arise from task-specific people, offices, evidence, resources, duties, and deadlines—not one magical Staff or Cabinet system.**

No Step 5 Living Country authority exists until a detached audit is preserved, any blocking findings are repaired, the unchanged gate passes, and a separate authority action explicitly accepts the resulting composite.
