# 2. Human recipient identity and role-context contract

## 2.1 One human, several contexts

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-MIR01] An individualized human has one underlying human identity and one Step-4 population-linkage status for an effective interval, even when the human participates through several recipient, account, office, organization, campaign, or source contexts.**

Potential contexts include:

- ordinary/private person;
- exact Population identity or statistically represented population relationship;
- individualized actor;
- officeholder;
- employee or organization member;
- campaign participant;
- platform account holder;
- outlet subscriber;
- confidential source;
- recipient of a classified or privileged briefing.

A context is not another person.

A platform account is not another voter.

An office assignment is not another mind.

A Population carrier, actor overlay, office assignment, and account may reference the same human without adding another person weight.

## 2.2 Recipient-context declaration

Every load-bearing delivery, presentation, exposure opportunity, notice, memory, or belief transition concerning an individualized human must declare or allow audit of:

- underlying human identity or Step-4 linkage status;
- immediate recipient surface/context;
- access or classification basis;
- artifact/proposition;
- channel/account/office/relationship through which the item arrived;
- delivery/presentation occurrence identity;
- whether exact, modeled, bounded, unsupported, or unknown;
- which recipient-owned state may be updated;
- whether another context may use the information;
- whether a separate communication or institutional record is required;
- overlap/reconciliation with any aggregate audience scope;
- effective time.

These are semantic obligations, not one mandatory runtime record.

## 2.3 Recipient contexts do not share one automatic knowledge pool

Potential state owners remain distinct:

### Human/private recipient state

May include:

- personal notice;
- personal memory;
- private source recognition;
- personally held belief or uncertainty;
- private attribution and salience.

### Individualized actor-private state

May include:

- role-relevant beliefs and evaluations;
- personal strategic interpretation;
- private relationships and trust;
- intentions;
- memories used in actor choice.

### Office/institutional state

May include:

- official receipt;
- accessible record;
- staff analysis;
- decision packet;
- classified or privileged institutional knowledge;
- office assignment and queue.

### Organization/campaign state

May include:

- organizational receipt;
- adopted analysis;
- internal poll;
- message plan;
- member communication;
- campaign record.

### Population aggregate state

May include:

- exact, modeled, or bounded audience delivery;
- purpose-bounded population belief, attribution, salience, preference, or turnout distributions;
- no named-person assertion unless support permits it.

No context automatically writes all others.

## 2.4 Same human may legitimately use privately learned information

The separation above does not require amnesia.

An exact individualized actor who personally notices a public article may legitimately use that personal knowledge when making a later actor decision.

The valid chain is:

```text
public presentation
→ exact human notices
→ human/actor-private memory or belief is updated
→ actor later evaluates a decision using personally available knowledge
```

This does not mean:

- the office received an official record;
- staff know;
- the institution adopted the belief;
- a classified record became public;
- the population aggregate learned what the actor learned.

If the office or organization needs the information as institutional knowledge, the human must:

- communicate it;
- submit or forward the artifact;
- create a note or record;
- ask staff to review;
- or use another accepted institutional route.

## 2.5 Official role receipt does not become ordinary public exposure

A classified, privileged, embargoed, internal, or official delivery to an officeholder may update:

- office receipt;
- actor-private role knowledge;
- a purpose-bounded official memory or assessment.

It does not automatically update:

- public Population belief;
- ordinary-person media exposure;
- public salience;
- platform engagement;
- household or social-network knowledge.

The holder may later disclose, leak, brief, discuss, or publicly act only through a separate eligible communication/action with its own authority, provenance, and consequences.

## 2.6 Ordinary-person exposure does not become institutional receipt

An officeholder’s private encounter with a public story does not automatically create:

- an office record;
- agency knowledge;
- staff receipt;
- official analysis;
- institution-wide notice.

The person may act from personal knowledge where that actor may legitimately do so.

Institutional propagation still requires a communication, record, meeting, request, or other valid route.

## 2.7 Statistical actor linkage

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-MIR02] An actor with `STATISTICALLY_REPRESENTED_WITHOUT_EXACT_IDENTITY` may not inherit exact delivery, notice, memory, trust, belief, attribution, salience, or channel-use facts from an aggregate audience or Population scope.**

If a modeled statewide audience received a story, the named statistically represented governor cannot be asserted to have seen it.

Permitted answers include:

- exact, if independently evidenced through the governor’s account/office/statement;
- modeled, if an explicit model targets that actor and preserves uncertainty;
- bounded;
- unsupported;
- unknown.

The aggregate audience’s belief distribution also cannot initialize the governor’s exact private belief.

## 2.8 Exact actor inside aggregate audience

An exact Population-linked actor may also lie inside an aggregate audience scope.

The system must declare whether the aggregate exposure:

1. excludes the exact person because exact recipients were carved out;
2. includes the exact person but does not resolve personal exposure;
3. is reconciled through a joint allocation containing that person;
4. remains modeled/bounded with unknown membership.

A later exact personal delivery may be recorded.

The same person may not be added again to unique reach merely because the exact and aggregate representations both exist.

Aggregate and exact exposure counts must use Step 3’s overlap, support, and scope rules.

## 2.9 Human recipient identity does not require fully individualized public cognition

The contract does not require every named actor to receive fully individualized public-media simulation.

A named actor may have:

- exact office receipts;
- actor-private memories;
- public exposure left modeled or unknown;
- no exact personal media habits;
- no exact Population belief join.

Individualization occurs only where supported consequences require it.

## 2.10 Organization and office recipients

An organization or office can receive information independently of whether every member or employee notices it.

An institutional receipt may create:

- official availability;
- an assignment;
- a queue item;
- staff analysis;
- a briefing packet;
- an adopted position after a valid decision process.

It does not automatically create personal notice or belief among all members.

Conversely, several members knowing something privately does not automatically create organization receipt, institutional knowledge, or adopted position.

# 3. Delivery, occurrence consumption, and repetition

## 3.1 One delivery occurrence is not several because several views reference it

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-MIR03] One canonical presentation or delivery occurrence may be consumed at most once by the same underlying recipient context at the same semantic stage.**

A delivery referenced by:

- Population exposure view;
- actor dossier;
- office briefing view;
- workstream;
- historical record;

remains one occurrence.

Projection aliases do not create additional exposure, notice, memory, or belief updates.

## 3.2 Distinct presentations may create legitimate repetition

The same recipient may legitimately encounter the same artifact or proposition several times through:

- feed presentation;
- television segment;
- email;
- office packet;
- friend’s message;
- search;
- later correction.

Each presentation requires its own canonical occurrence or supported aggregate presentation process.

The recipient process then determines whether it was:

- noticed;
- recognized as repetition;
- source-confused;
- ignored;
- reinforcing;
- contradictory;
- correction exposure;
- new evidence;
- merely another derivative.

Distinct presentation is not independent evidence by itself.

## 3.3 Consumption key

A load-bearing update should be capable of identifying a semantic consumption key such as:

```text
recipient identity/context
+ presentation/delivery occurrence
+ proposition or bounded content target
+ cognitive stage
```

This is a semantic idempotency obligation, not a required technical key format.

## 3.4 Cross-context transfer requires a real route

Information may move between contexts only through a valid transfer, such as:

- person tells staff;
- office sends a memo;
- member forwards an article to organization;
- campaign receives an internal poll;
- classified briefing is shared under authorized access;
- officeholder privately remembers something while acting;
- organization broadcasts to members;
- leak creates a new disclosure path.

The transfer has:

- sender;
- recipient context;
- artifact/proposition;
- access/classification basis;
- time;
- occurrence identity;
- resulting receipt;
- possible breach or legal consequence.

## 3.5 No retroactive context rewriting

If a senator personally noticed an article Monday and forwarded it to staff Wednesday:

- personal notice occurred Monday;
- office receipt occurred Wednesday;
- the office did not know Monday merely because its holder did;
- the actor may have privately acted Tuesday if the action was personally available;
- later records preserve both times.

# 4. Closed exposure terminology

## 4.1 Availability

**Availability** means an artifact can be accessed through a channel under stated conditions at a time.

Examples:

- public webpage exists;
- subscriber archive contains item;
- classified system contains authorized memo;
- broadcast is scheduled/accessible in catchment.

Availability does not mean the channel presented it to a recipient.

## 4.2 Presentation or delivery

**Presentation/delivery** means a channel, platform, institution, person, or process placed or transmitted the artifact/content onto a declared recipient surface or scope.

Examples:

- feed displayed item;
- newsletter delivered;
- television segment aired into catchment;
- memo sent to office inbox;
- friend sent message;
- search returned result.

Presentation/delivery does not mean the recipient perceived it.

## 4.3 Exposure opportunity

**Exposure opportunity** means the presented content was available for perception by the declared recipient or modeled audience under the represented context.

It can be:

- exact;
- modeled;
- bounded;
- unsupported.

It does not mean cognitive notice.

## 4.4 Notice

**Notice** is recipient-owned incorporation that the content, signal, or proposition was perceived at least minimally.

Notice may occur without comprehension, source recognition, memory, trust, or belief.

## 4.5 Attention

**Attention** is recipient-owned cognitive allocation beyond minimal notice.

It may affect comprehension, memory, evaluation, or action.

It is not one global public resource.

## 4.6 Engagement

**Engagement** is a separately owned behavioral occurrence such as click, view duration, reaction, comment, share, search, or reply.

Some engagement may indicate attention; it does not establish belief or truth.

## 4.7 Ownership

`InformationEnvironment` may own represented:

- availability;
- presentation/delivery;
- exposure opportunity.

Recipient owners own:

- notice;
- attention;
- comprehension;
- memory;
- trust;
- belief;
- attribution;
- salience.

The informal word `exposure` is invalid in a load-bearing contract unless it declares which stage it means.

# 5. Proposition identity, support, and truth

## 5.1 Proposition identity is not truth authority

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-MIR04] A proposition identity names or bounds the semantic content of an assertion, question, denial, prediction, attribution, or belief target. It does not own whether the proposition is true.**

Underlying truth remains with the relevant material, social, legal, institutional, actor, fiscal, population, historical, or other semantic owners.

Evidentiary support remains with Step 7 evidence, method, and assessment processes.

## 5.2 Proposition relationships require provenance

A relation such as:

- supports;
- contradicts;
- corrects;
- qualifies;
- rebuts;
- confirms;
- falsifies;

is not an automatic truth edge.

It must be one of:

- a speaker/producer claim;
- an analytic or evidentiary assessment;
- a logically/legally established relation under a supported process;
- an unresolved relation.

The relation has owner, evidence, time, method, and access.

## 5.3 No omniscient PropositionGraph

A proposition registry or semantic graph may support:

- identity;
- lineage;
- duplicate detection;
- query;
- linking claims, artifacts, evidence, corrections, and beliefs.

It may not:

- read canonical world truth automatically;
- label every claim true/false for the player;
- resolve causal attribution;
- grant outlets or platforms correct fact-checking;
- overwrite recipient belief.

## 5.4 Truth may be distributed across owners

A proposition may combine several facts.

Example:

```text
The President knew about an unlawful payment before signing the directive.
```

Assessing it may require:

- payment occurrence;
- legal status;
- President’s receipt/knowledge;
- directive time;
- evidence and uncertainty.

The proposition identity does not collapse those owners.

# 6. Moderation, annotation, and recipient action

## 6.1 Platform labels and context panels

A warning label, community annotation, fact-check panel, context link, authenticity marker, or recommendation restriction must be represented through distinct facts:

1. eligible actor/platform decision;
2. annotation or linked information artifact;
3. evidence and claimed assessment;
4. distribution/presentation consequence;
5. recipient exposure opportunity;
6. recipient notice and interpretation.

The label does not:

- change the original proposition’s truth;
- prove the platform’s assessment correct;
- guarantee that the recipient sees the label;
- guarantee correction;
- set belief;
- erase original exposure.

## 6.2 Moderation action versus public claim

A platform may remove or restrict content under its own rule even when truth remains unresolved.

A government, campaign, outlet, or user may claim that moderation was justified, biased, censored, insufficient, or unlawful.

Those claims proceed through normal information and legal routes.

## 6.3 Willingness is not action

**[HARD INVARIANT LC-MIR05] Recipient willingness, intention, preference, propensity, or readiness to act is not the later action occurrence or result.**

Population or actor state may include:

- willingness to share;
- intention to vote;
- readiness to protest;
- donation propensity;
- support for joining an organization;
- likelihood assessment.

The later:

- share;
- vote;
- donation;
- protest attendance;
- contact;
- organization membership;
- purchase;
- compliance;

requires the appropriate actor, aggregate, organization, market, election, or institutional process.

Examples:

```text
willing to share
≠ shared
```

```text
intends to vote
≠ ballot cast
```

```text
supports protest
≠ attended protest
```

Step 4’s decision/action/result separation remains controlling.

- Briefing;

- media view;

The same person:

- sees headline in feed;
- hears television segment later;
- receives friend’s message.

Result: three distinct presentation opportunities with shared proposition lineage, not one occurrence and not three independent evidentiary sources by default.

## 7.3 Office/public separation test

A classified report reaches agency and President.

Result: no public population exposure until a separate release/leak/communication occurs.

## 7.4 Private-to-office transfer test

A governor personally notices a local report and asks staff to investigate.

Result:

- personal notice;
- governor instruction/communication;
- staff assignment;
- institutional receipt;
- no retroactive office knowledge before the request.

## 7.5 Aggregate/exact overlap test

An exact named journalist is also statistically inside a regional broadcast audience.

Result: the audience estimate does not prove the journalist watched; a later exact viewing event may be recorded without summing the person twice in unique reach.

## 7.6 Statistical actor test

A statistically represented senator is in a district where modeled exposure is 65%.

Result: personal notice remains unknown unless independently observed.

## 7.7 Moderation-label test

Platform labels a false-looking but unresolved claim.

Result: label, ranking consequence, recipient exposure, recipient belief, and later truth assessment remain separate.
