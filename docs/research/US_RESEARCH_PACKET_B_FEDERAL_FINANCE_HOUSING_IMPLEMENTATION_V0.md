## 3. Executive summary

Federal fiscal execution is not one “money available” fact. Congress supplies legal budget authority; appropriations are one form of that authority; apportionment distributes budgetary resources for controlled obligation; an agency incurs an obligation when it creates a definite legal liability; an outlay occurs when that obligation is liquidated by payment. Purpose, time, and amount constraints continue to govern. An unobligated balance therefore does not itself establish authority to redirect money to another purpose (`USR-CLM-0079`–`0091`).

HOME is the strongest single institutional analog for the first vertical's grant-to-Housing chain. Current law authorizes the program; HUD makes formula allocations to states and qualifying local participating jurisdictions; participation uses consolidated-plan and grant-agreement machinery rather than a competitive federal project application; participating jurisdictions select and underwrite projects; eligible activities include acquisition, rehabilitation, and new construction; HOME funds may be loans, grants, or other assistance; project commitment, IDIS setup, drawdown, recipient expenditure, construction, final draw, and project completion are distinct events (`USR-CLM-0092`–`0108`). This is an evidence-basis conclusion, not a configuration selection.

HOME does not literally reproduce the synthetic topology. States are automatically eligible for state allocations, local governments and consortia can be participating jurisdictions, allocations are formula-based, designation is generally continuing, and the federal step is closer to plan approval plus grant agreement than case-by-case discretionary approval of a state's Housing project. A preliminary local award is not a HOME project commitment. The 25-percent statutory/regulatory match is measured against HOME funds drawn and has exceptions; no evidence supports the synthetic 55-percent rate (`USR-CLM-0093`–`0107`, `0132`–`0133`).

HTF is the strongest state-centered alternative or complement. It sends formula grants to states or state-designated entities, requires state allocation planning, and lets states distribute assistance through eligible recipients or subgrantees for production and preservation. Its funding originates primarily from GSE assessments rather than the ordinary annual-appropriation chain. GAO's bounded sample shows that HTF commonly occupies only one layer in a larger financing stack and that completed production can lag commitments, making it useful evidence for state administration and delayed material delivery but not a full substitute for HOME's fiscal route (`USR-CLM-0113`–`0122`).

CDBG is a weaker Housing-production analog. It is a broad community-development formula program for entitlement communities and states, with states distributing funds in nonentitlement areas. HUD records Hawaii as a real long-running example of a state declining to administer the state CDBG program, but HUD then administers grants for eligible nonentitlement counties; this is not evidence of a common clean state refusal that leaves an entire state outside federal activity. New housing construction is generally ineligible under CDBG regulations, subject to bounded exceptions, while rehabilitation and related activities are eligible (`USR-CLM-0123`–`0127`).

LIHTC is a material-financing complement and contrast, not a federal grant analog. Federal tax law allocates credit authority through state/local housing credit agencies and qualified allocation plans; projects then raise investor equity and often combine other financing before being placed in service. It helps explain why a state allocation or federal subsidy does not equal a completed project, but it does not create the HUD grant-award/obligation/drawdown chain (`USR-CLM-0128`–`0131`).

No single program reproduces the fixture. The strongest bounded evidence basis is HOME for federal grant, participating-jurisdiction, agreement, drawdown, and project stages; HTF for a more literally state-centered allocation and delayed-production path; and LIHTC only as evidence of layered project finance. Later configuration must decide whether and how to compress those facts. Packet B does not make that decision (`USR-CLM-0131`–`0142`).

## 5. Primary-source findings

### 5.1 Federal fiscal authority and the disputed-funds boundary

The Antideficiency Act bars officers from making obligations or expenditures exceeding an available appropriation/fund amount and from obligating before an appropriation unless law authorizes it. The bona fide-needs rule limits fixed-period appropriations to obligations properly incurred during their period of availability. Section 1501 separately requires documentary evidence for recorded obligations, including grants or subsidies payable under authorized agreements, plans, or formulas (`USR-CLM-0081`–`0083`, `0086`; USR-SRC-0033–`0034`).

Apportionment is a control on the rate and distribution of obligations, not a grant of a different statutory purpose. Transfers between appropriations require statutory authority. Reprogramming within a lump-sum appropriation is different from transfer and remains lawful only within the appropriation's object and applicable limitations. The Impoundment Control Act supplies procedures for proposed rescissions and deferrals; a rescission proposal does not by itself permanently cancel the authority (`USR-CLM-0084`–`0085`, `0090`–`0091`, `0136`–`0137`; USR-SRC-0031, `0035`–`0037`).

This supports a plausible underlying legal dispute if an executive official attempts to repurpose or withhold Housing funds without applicable authority. It does not establish the merits, forum, injunction standard, order, compliance response, or appeal; those remain Packet C.

### 5.2 HOME institutional and fiscal chain

Public Law 119-101 expressly reauthorized HOME on July 11, 2026. The current statute and regulations establish a formula program administered by HUD for state and qualifying local participating jurisdictions. States receive state allocations; local jurisdictions can qualify individually or through consortia. A consolidated plan and HOME grant agreement are federal-interface requirements. This is not a competition in which HUD chooses among state project applications (`USR-CLM-0092`–`0097`; USR-SRC-0039–`0043`).

Participating jurisdictions choose among eligible Housing activities and normally select the downstream projects. HOME permits acquisition, rehabilitation, reconstruction, and new construction, subject to income targeting, property standards, underwriting, affordability, environmental, written-agreement, reporting, and other conditions. Participating jurisdictions must examine sources and uses, market demand, project viability, developer capacity, and firm financing before committing HOME development funds (`USR-CLM-0098`–`0107`; USR-SRC-0040–`0044`, `0055`).

The real sequence is layered. A federal grant agreement and federal obligation precede or frame program access. A participating jurisdiction's legally binding written project agreement is its project commitment; a preliminary award is not enough. The project is set up in IDIS, funds are drawn for eligible costs on an advance or reimbursement basis subject to cash-management rules, and downstream entities perform or finance the Housing activity. HOME's definition of project completion adds construction/rehabilitation completion, compliance records, final draw, and IDIS completion data. Payment alone is therefore not completion (`USR-CLM-0086`–`0089`, `0097`, `0100`–`0105`, `0141`).

The usual HOME match is 25 percent of HOME funds drawn, with statutory/regulatory exclusions and waiver authority; it is not a universal project-cost share and is not the synthetic 55-percent match. The law also permits grant, loan, equity, interest-subsidy, and other assistance forms. Layered project finance is therefore ordinary to the mechanism rather than a second copy of federal budget authority (`USR-CLM-0106`–`0108`).

### 5.3 July 2026 HOME legal transition

Public Law 119-101 removed former 42 U.S.C. §12748(g), titled by the new law as the expiration of the right to draw HOME Investment Trust funds, and changed allocation, eligibility, enforcement, inspection, and other provisions. The same law directs future HUD rulemaking for some amendments. As retrieved on 2026-08-21, eCFR Part 92 still contains regulatory commitment/expenditure and recapture provisions framed under the prior statute, and HUD pages contain pre-amendment explanations (`USR-CLM-0109`–`0112`, `0140`).

The safe P0 conclusion is not that every old deadline vanished, nor that every old regulation is unquestionably unchanged in effect. The amended statute controls where inconsistent; program-specific regulatory provisions remain published until amended or otherwise displaced; and the exact operational treatment of affected grants requires freeze-time revalidation. Packet B therefore relies on unaffected current stage distinctions and labels changed deadline/eligibility details `SUPPORTED_WITH_BOUNDS` rather than forcing a false timeless rule.

### 5.4 Housing Trust Fund

HTF is administered by HUD but funded principally through statutory assessments on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which makes its federal finance path materially different from an ordinary annual appropriation. HUD distributes formula grants to states; a state may administer directly or designate a state housing finance agency or other qualifying entity. State allocation plans govern distribution to eligible recipients and projects. Eligible uses include production and preservation through acquisition, new construction, reconstruction, and rehabilitation (`USR-CLM-0113`–`0117`; USR-SRC-0045–`0047`).

HTF also separates grant agreement, commitment, IDIS setup/draw, expenditure, and project completion. Regulations provide commitment and expenditure periods, written agreements, day-to-day grantee management, and project/affordability oversight. These features make HTF the strongest state-centered alternative/complement, but its formula allocation still does not establish a general federal discretionary approval of a state application (`USR-CLM-0114`–`0119`, `0122`).

GAO's 2023 report supplies bounded operational evidence. In its 12 selected grantees and 70 completed projects, HTF represented about 10 percent of total financing and LIHTC was the largest source; GAO expressly says the selected-grantee results are not generalizable. National program data in the report also separated completed units/projects from larger committed pipelines as of March 2022. That supports layered finance and real latency, but not a universal percentage, delay distribution, or effectiveness coefficient (`USR-CLM-0120`–`0121`; USR-SRC-0048`).

### 5.5 CDBG and LIHTC contrasts

CDBG is a formula community-development program reaching entitlement communities and states, with states distributing funds in nonentitlement areas. Its eligible Housing activities include rehabilitation and related support, while regulations generally prohibit new housing construction subject to specific exceptions. HUD reports that Hawaii permanently elected not to administer the state CDBG program and that HUD instead awards eligible nonentitlement counties. This establishes that nonparticipation can exist, but not that state refusal is a normal all-or-nothing Housing-production decision (`USR-CLM-0123`–`0127`; USR-SRC-0049–`0051`).

LIHTC is a tax-credit mechanism. Federal law caps and allocates credit authority to housing credit agencies; qualified allocation plans and project-level agency determinations govern allocations; owners claim credits after satisfying placed-in-service and compliance requirements. Investor equity and other public/private sources finance development. LIHTC is consequently strong evidence for downstream project selection, layered financing, and the time between allocation and placed-in-service status, but it is not a HUD grant award, federal grant obligation, or cash draw (`USR-CLM-0128`–`0131`; USR-SRC-0052–`0054`, `0056`).

## 7. Contradictions, ambiguity, and supported bounds

1. **HOME statute/regulation transition.** Public Law 119-101 removed former 42 U.S.C. §12748(g) and changed several substantive provisions. Current Part 92 still displays pre-amendment deadline and allocation language. Packet B neither deletes every regulatory deadline nor treats every old sentence as controlling; `USR-CLM-0109` and `0140` require freeze-time revalidation.
2. **HOME “application” terminology.** HUD may call consolidated-plan or grant materials submissions, but the program's resource distribution is formula-based and designation generally continues. Evidence does not support a generic annual competitive state application (`USR-CLM-0093`–`0097`, `0133`).
3. **HOME match.** The 25-percent rule applies to funds drawn with exclusions and waiver authority; it is not necessarily 25 percent of total project cost. Project leverage can be much larger or structured differently (`USR-CLM-0106`–`0108`).
4. **HTF operational sample.** GAO's 12-grantee/70-project financing findings are explicitly nongeneralizable. They support existence and character of layered finance, not national percentages or performance calibration (`USR-CLM-0120`).
5. **State refusal.** CDBG supplies one durable nonadministration case, but the federal response routes funds to counties rather than ending activity statewide. HOME automatically includes states in its formula structure, and HTF uses state intent/plans. “State refuses” is therefore only partially supported as a cross-program abstraction (`USR-CLM-0126`, `0132`).
6. **Administrative capacity.** Formal underwriting and management requirements plus observed pipeline evidence show capacity can matter. They do not establish one scalar capacity measure, a causal effect size, or a state-by-state ranking (`USR-CLM-0134`–`0135`).
7. **“Disbursement.”** Federal sources use `outlay`, `payment`, `drawdown`, `expenditure`, and `disbursement` at different ownership layers. Packet B preserves the actor and accounting context rather than asserting a universal synonym (`USR-CLM-0088`–`0089`, `0138`–`0139`).
8. **Program family versus one program.** HOME best spans the first vertical, but HTF better grounds a state-only recipient and LIHTC better grounds financing layers. This is an evidenced composite basis, not permission to merge their legal rules (`USR-CLM-0131`).

No evidence is classified `CONFLICTED`; the apparent conflicts are temporal, definitional, or scope differences that can be bounded without forcing false certainty.
