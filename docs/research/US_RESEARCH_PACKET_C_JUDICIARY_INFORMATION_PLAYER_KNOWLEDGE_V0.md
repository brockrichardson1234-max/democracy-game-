## 3. Executive summary

Packet C's strongest bounded hostile-route evidence is not a generic executive command to “redirect $500 million.” It is a final HUD directive that withholds or diverts formula-allocated HOME budget authority from its authorized program purpose, affecting an existing state or local participating jurisdiction. Packet B's purpose, transfer, and impoundment constraints make that action legally contestable; GAO precedent confirms that executive withholding without recognized authority is not made lawful merely by leaving funds unobligated. (`USR-CLM-0143`–`0145`)

A participating jurisdiction needs concrete, particularized, actual or imminent programmatic or financial injury, causation, and redressability. Statehood alone is not the injury, and the synthetic `State A` is not the only plausible claimant. A final, reviewable HUD directive can support a prospective, nonmonetary Administrative Procedure Act route in a U.S. district court, but the route is **SUPPORTED_WITH_BOUNDS**: finality, reviewability, injury, venue, and relief depend on the exact directive and grant stage. A demand that is substantively enforcement of a contractual obligation to pay money may instead fall within Tucker Act jurisdiction in the Court of Federal Claims. (`USR-CLM-0146`–`0156`, `0174`)

A preliminary injunction is the stronger first-vertical analog than an ex parte or short-lived TRO because it requires notice and can preserve a status quo while merits litigation proceeds. The court's legal reasoning is not the operative command: Rule 65 requires the injunction/order itself to state its terms specifically and describe the restrained or required conduct. An order binds the parties and specified related persons with actual notice; current Supreme Court authority strongly cabins relief to the plaintiffs rather than authorizing an automatic universal command. (`USR-CLM-0157`–`0164`)

Order entry, notice to government counsel, agency response, appeal, and stay are separate stages. DOJ ordinarily conducts litigation for federal agencies, CM/ECF creates a docket and sends registered counsel an electronic notice, and Rule 65 makes actual notice legally relevant. An interlocutory injunction is appealable, but the appeal does not itself stay it. The government ordinarily must request a stay first in district court and, if necessary, in the court of appeals; a stay is discretionary and analytically distinct from an appeal. (`USR-CLM-0165`–`0173`)

No single official Housing product supplies both program delivery and material Housing truth. IDIS/PR20 and HUD performance reporting are the leading administrative basis for HOME allocations, commitments, draws, activity status, and reported completions. Census products are the leading broader basis: BPS measures permits (authorization, not starts or completions), the Survey of Construction measures starts/completions at national and regional resolution, and ACS supplies sample-based period estimates such as rent burden with published margins of error and finer geography through five-year products. The leading evidence basis is therefore a bounded composite: **HOME/IDIS administrative records + ACS broader affordability/stock estimates**, with **BPS/SOC as a production-pipeline complement**. (`USR-CLM-0175`–`0192`)

Release lag is real rather than a synthetic timer. BPS asks about permits authorized in the prior month and publishes preliminary national/region results on a scheduled monthly cadence; late reports and annual benchmarking revise portions of the series. ACS annual products describe a 12- or 60-month observation period and are released later; as of the research date, 2024 remains the latest published standard one-year ACS release and Census has not fixed the delayed 2025 release date. That mutable statement requires freeze-time revalidation. (`USR-CLM-0180`–`0188`)

The executive administration can plausibly know its own program, grant, fiscal, litigation, order, and response records through the responsible institutions, plus public official releases. That does not establish instantaneous presidential access to every agency database. IDIS documentation supports HUD headquarters, field-office, and grantee access categories; Title 13 and Commerce release rules prevent treating unreleased Census microdata or final statistical releases as government-wide omniscience. Media embargo access proves that captured-but-not-public information can exist, but does not establish general executive-policy access. Exact Population belief, attribution, salience, preference, or turnout disposition remains unestablished as a direct government administrative record; a survey or poll is a measurement artifact, not the latent state. (`USR-CLM-0193`–`0201`)

## 5. Primary-source findings

### 5.1 Underlying disputed action and review route

Packet B established that an unobligated HOME balance is not unrestricted money and that purpose-changing transfer or withholding requires authority. Packet C adds GAO's controlling institutional distinction between unlawful withholding and a reasonable programmatic delay. The selected research route therefore requires a concrete final HUD directive, not mere slow administration. (`USR-CLM-0143`–`0145`)

APA §702 supports review by a person adversely affected and waives sovereign immunity for qualifying nonmonetary relief; §§704 and 706 bound review to final agency action and specified judicial remedies, subject to §701. Federal-question jurisdiction and federal-officer venue provisions support a district-court forum. These statutes do not cure a claim that is substantively contractual payment enforcement. (`USR-CLM-0149`–`0156`)

The Ninth Circuit's *San Francisco v. Trump* decision is a bounded institutional analog, not nationwide controlling proof for HOME. It demonstrates that local governments can litigate an executive effort to withdraw federal grants without congressional authorization and that relief scope is independently adjudicated. The 2025 *Department of Education v. California* order supplies the important contrary boundary: reinstating and paying terminated grants can sound in contract and fall outside APA district-court relief. (`USR-CLM-0155`–`0156`)

### 5.2 Interim relief, order, notice, and response

Rule 65 distinguishes a noticed preliminary injunction from a TRO, limits an ex parte TRO, requires specificity in the operative order, and bounds who is legally bound. *Winter* supplies the national preliminary-injunction factors. *Trump v. CASA* requires the first-vertical evidence basis to remain plaintiff-specific unless a different valid remedial vehicle supports broader relief. (`USR-CLM-0157`–`0164`)

DOJ's litigation role and CM/ECF notice supply a real institutional handoff from court to government counsel. Actual notice can arrive by electronic docket notice or otherwise; the simulation's abstract “order received” is a compression candidate, not a literal federal-law field. Compliance remains a later agency/government action. (`USR-CLM-0165`–`0168`)

### 5.3 Appeal and stay

An order granting or refusing an injunction is interlocutorily appealable. Rule 62 expressly provides that an injunction is not automatically stayed even if appealed, and FRAP 8 ordinarily sends the government first to district court for a stay or injunction pending appeal. *Nken* makes a stay discretionary and supplies the familiar four-factor stay test. The synthetic “appeal while complying; order remains active” route is therefore institutionally defensible when no stay has issued. (`USR-CLM-0169`–`0173`)

### 5.4 Official Housing information

IDIS reports distinguish authorized funds, commitments, disbursements, open/closed activities, and reported completed units. A dated PR20 is an official administrative artifact, while HUD's annual performance report is another published derivative. Neither establishes the entire regional Housing market. (`USR-CLM-0175`–`0178`)

BPS, SOC, and ACS measure different referents and at different resolutions. BPS is an authorization measure. SOC provides national/region starts and completions. ACS supplies sample-based period estimates for detailed Housing characteristics, with margins of error and geography/vintage rules. (`USR-CLM-0179`–`0188`)

### 5.5 Player-administration information boundary

IDIS documentation supports role-bounded administrative access at HUD headquarters, field offices, and grantees. Census law and release policy support a different boundary: confidential inputs and unreleased products stay within the statistical process or authorized embargo, and policy interpretation remains distinct from the neutral statistical product. Institutional availability therefore follows the source-owning organization and record pathway. (`USR-CLM-0193`–`0201`)

## 7. Contradictions, ambiguity, and supported bounds

1. **District court versus Court of Federal Claims.** Prospective relief against a final unlawful diversion/withholding decision remains a plausible APA route, but an order whose substance enforces a grant-payment contract may fall under the Tucker Act. The selected route is therefore `SUPPORTED_WITH_BOUNDS`, not a universal grant-dispute rule. (`USR-CLM-0155`, `0174`)
2. **Finality/reviewability.** A presidential announcement, internal deliberation, OMB discussion, or routine program delay is not necessarily final HUD agency action. The route requires a consummated directive with legal consequences and law to apply. (`USR-CLM-0144`, `0150`–`0151`)
3. **Claimant identity.** HOME includes states and local participating jurisdictions. Which claimant has the cleanest injury depends on allocation, agreement, commitment, and threatened action. Packet C does not freeze a state claimant. (`USR-CLM-0145`–`0148`)
4. **Relief scope.** *CASA* materially narrows a generic nationwide-order assumption. The first-vertical route can remain plaintiff-specific without losing the contested-authority seam. (`USR-CLM-0163`)
5. **HOME legal transition.** Packet B's July 2026 HOME amendment remains a mutable dependency. Packet C does not resolve HUD's post-amendment regulatory implementation and carries the freeze-revalidation requirement forward. (`USR-CLM-0143`, Packet B `0109`, `0140`)
6. **Administrative completion.** IDIS “completion” is a program-defined administrative status requiring material and reporting predicates, but it is still not a general Housing-market measure. (`USR-CLM-0178`)
7. **ACS current vintage.** Census reported on 2026-08-06 that the 2025 one-year release date remained undetermined. That current status can change and must be freeze-revalidated. (`USR-CLM-0187`)
8. **Pre-release knowledge.** Census personnel and accredited media can have bounded pre-release access under controls. No evidence establishes that all executive policy officials or the President have general access to underlying microdata or final unreleased products. (`USR-CLM-0193`–`0196`)
9. **No cross-packet contradiction recorded.** Packet C narrows Packet B's hostile-action claim and uses its stage distinctions; it does not contradict a Packet A/B atomic claim.

### A. Correct current BPS methodology (`USR-RQ-006`)

The earlier comparison's phrase “monthly sample of roughly 8,400 permit-issuing offices” is corrected. About 8,400 is the count of high-activity places Census contacts monthly, not a probability sample drawn from the approximately 19,900-place permit universe. Beginning January 2022, BPS monthly aggregates are non-probability entire-universe tabulations/estimates combining received reports with imputation for nonreporters and places not contacted monthly. Annual reporters, Survey of Construction inputs for documented areas, and documented third-party inputs also enter the estimation process. (`USR-CLM-0180`, `0240`; `USR-SRC-0075`, `0098`)

Because the current design is non-probability, sampling error cannot be quantified. Response and other nonsampling errors, corrections, annual processing, and mutable vintage remain material. The broader use strategy remains bounded: BPS measures permit authorizations, SOC measures starts/completions, ACS supports sampled stock/affordability measures, and IDIS/HUD records program stages. None singly owns material Housing truth. (`USR-CLM-0179`–`0192`, `0216`, `0240`)
